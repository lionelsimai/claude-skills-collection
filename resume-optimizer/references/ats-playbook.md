# ATS Playbook — how applicant tracking systems read a resume

Most online applications are parsed by an applicant tracking system (ATS)
before a human sees them. The parser extracts name, contact, titles, dates,
employers, education and skills into fields, then recruiters search and rank
those fields. A resume "optimised for AI" is a resume the parser reads
correctly and the recruiter's keyword search can find.

## What parsers do badly

- **Tables and multi-column layouts** — text is read across rows or columns
  in the wrong order; whole blocks can vanish.
- **Text boxes, graphics, icons, photos** — usually dropped entirely.
- **Headers and footers** — many parsers skip them, including the contact
  details people put there.
- **Creative section headings** — "Where I've been" does not map to the
  Experience field. Use Summary, Experience, Education, Skills,
  Certifications.
- **Unusual fonts and symbols** — stick to standard fonts; use simple
  hyphens or standard bullets.
- **Scanned images of resumes** — no extractable text at all.

## Formatting rules that survive parsing

1. Single column, top to bottom, chronological.
2. Standard fonts: Calibri, Arial, Georgia, Helvetica. Body 10–12pt,
   headings 13–16pt.
3. Contact details as plain text at the top of the body — never in a
   header/footer. Spell out the LinkedIn URL.
4. Dates in one consistent format: `Jan 2023 – Present`.
5. Job title, employer, location on their own lines, in that order.
6. Submit PDF unless the portal asks for DOCX; prepare both. Name the
   file `Firstname-Lastname-Role.pdf`.
7. Length: one page under ~8 years of experience, two pages above that.

## How recruiters search

Recruiters search the parsed database with the job's own vocabulary: tool
names, certifications, method names, role titles. Two consequences:

- Use the posting's exact term when it truthfully names the candidate's
  skill ("Power BI", "stakeholder management", "A/B testing").
- Put the target role title (or a truthful near-equivalent) near the top —
  in the summary line — because title searches weight it heavily.

## What "AI ready" must never mean

- No hidden keyword text, white-on-white lists or repeated skill blocks.
  Modern systems flag them and recruiters treat them as dishonesty.
- No claims the candidate cannot defend in an interview. The parser is
  the first reader, not the only one.
