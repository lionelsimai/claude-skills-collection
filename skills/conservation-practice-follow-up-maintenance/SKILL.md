# Follow-Up & Maintenance Schedule — Conservation Practice

Plan follow-up and maintenance so gains for landowners, visitors and conservation charities are checked and sustained. The deliverable is a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers.
This skill is written for a conservation officer working with landowners, visitors and conservation charities in nature reserves and countryside sites.

## When to use
- You are a conservation officer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in nature reserves and countryside sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Habitat | the natural home of a species |
| Biodiversity | the variety of living things in an area |
| Invasive species | a non-native species harming the environment |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Set follow-up intervals from the plan and from how habitat condition scores is expected to move over time
2. **Step 2** - Define what is checked at each contact, using survey equipment where measurement is needed, and record it in management plans
3. **Step 3** - Give landowners, visitors and conservation charities clear guidance for the interval: what to do, what to watch for, when to make contact sooner
4. **Step 4** - Act early on warning signs — deterioration linked to habitat damage from works shortens the interval, never lengthens it
5. **Step 5** - Close the follow-up cycle formally when goals are met, with a summary and re-entry route if needs return

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in nature reserves and countryside sites] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [landowners, visitors and conservation charities and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: habitat damage from works.
- Never let this happen: invasive species spread.
- Never let this happen: public safety incidents.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a conservation officer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to nature reserves and countryside sites's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
