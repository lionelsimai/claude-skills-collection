# Intake & Initial Assessment — Management Accounting

Run a structured intake and initial assessment for business managers and leadership teams in a finance department. The deliverable is a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
This skill is written for a management accountant working with business managers and leadership teams in a finance department.

## When to use
- You are a management accountant preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a finance department.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Variance | the difference between budgeted and actual figures |
| Forecast | a prediction of future financial performance |
| Cost centre | a unit of a business tracked for its costs |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm identity, consent and the reason for referral or engagement, and open the record that will hold management accounts
2. **Step 2** - Gather history and current circumstances from business managers and leadership teams, using the standard intake structure for a finance department
3. **Step 3** - Record baseline observations and measurements relevant to report delivery timeliness, noting anything that changes the priority of the case
4. **Step 4** - Flag immediate concerns — especially misleading forecasts — and escalate or fast-track anything urgent before proceeding
5. **Step 5** - Summarise findings for business managers and leadership teams in plain language, agree the next step, and file the intake section of management accounts

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: misleading forecasts.
- Never let this happen: cost allocations distorted.
- Never let this happen: deadline slippage.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a management accountant; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a finance department's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
