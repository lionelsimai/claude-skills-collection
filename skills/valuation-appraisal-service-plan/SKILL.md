# Service Plan Design — Valuation & Appraisal

Design a realistic, measurable service plan for lenders, buyers, sellers and investors as a chartered valuation surveyor. The deliverable is a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates.
This skill is written for a chartered valuation surveyor working with lenders, buyers, sellers and investors in properties and surveying offices.

## When to use
- You are a chartered valuation surveyor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in properties and surveying offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Start from the assessment findings and agree goals with lenders, buyers, sellers and investors — specific enough to measure against report turnaround
2. **Step 2** - Break each goal into actions sequenced for properties and surveying offices, naming who does what and by when
3. **Step 3** - Build the plan around valuation reports so progress, changes and decisions are recorded in one place
4. **Step 4** - Price or resource the plan honestly: time, valuation software and materials, with contingencies for overvaluation losses
5. **Step 5** - Set the review date and the trigger points that would change the plan, and confirm the plan with all parties

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in properties and surveying offices] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [lenders, buyers, sellers and investors and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: overvaluation losses.
- Never let this happen: missed defects affecting value.
- Never let this happen: conflicts undeclared.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a chartered valuation surveyor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to properties and surveying offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
