# Service Plan Design — Executive Chef Practice

Design a realistic, measurable service plan for diners, kitchen teams and restaurant owners as a executive chef. The deliverable is a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates.
This skill is written for a executive chef working with diners, kitchen teams and restaurant owners in professional kitchens.

## When to use
- You are a executive chef preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in professional kitchens.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Start from the assessment findings and agree goals with diners, kitchen teams and restaurant owners — specific enough to measure against food cost control
2. **Step 2** - Break each goal into actions sequenced for professional kitchens, naming who does what and by when
3. **Step 3** - Build the plan around menu plans so progress, changes and decisions are recorded in one place
4. **Step 4** - Price or resource the plan honestly: time, kitchen equipment and materials, with contingencies for food poisoning risks
5. **Step 5** - Set the review date and the trigger points that would change the plan, and confirm the plan with all parties

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in professional kitchens] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [diners, kitchen teams and restaurant owners and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: food poisoning risks.
- Never let this happen: cross-contamination.
- Never let this happen: burns and cuts in service.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a executive chef; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to professional kitchens's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
