# New Client Onboarding Pack — Sustainability Management

Onboard businesses, investors and regulators smoothly into a sustainability manager's service in organisations across all sectors. The deliverable is an onboarding pack: welcome information, expectations, first steps and contacts.
This skill is written for a sustainability manager working with businesses, investors and regulators in organisations across all sectors.

## When to use
- You are a sustainability manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in organisations across all sectors.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an onboarding pack: welcome information, expectations, first steps and contacts.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Scope 3 | indirect emissions in a value chain |
| Carbon inventory | a measured record of an organisation's emissions |
| Assurance | independent checking of reported sustainability data |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Send a welcome pack before the first appointment: what the service does, what happens first, what to bring from carbon inventories
2. **Step 2** - Set expectations honestly — timescales, costs where relevant, and how progress on emissions reduction progress will be shared
3. **Step 3** - Cover rights and responsibilities on both sides, including confidentiality limits and how to raise concerns
4. **Step 4** - Make the first contact easy: named contact, directions to organisations across all sectors, and what to do if plans change
5. **Step 5** - Check in after the first session or milestone and fix friction early, before it becomes disengagement or greenwashing accusations

2. Detail, following the workflow above

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: greenwashing accusations.
- Never let this happen: data gaps in reporting.
- Never let this happen: missed regulatory deadlines.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a sustainability manager; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to organisations across all sectors's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
