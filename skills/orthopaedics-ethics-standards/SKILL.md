# Ethics & Professional Standards — Orthopaedics

Apply the ethical codes and professional standards that govern a orthopaedic surgeon. The deliverable is an ethics guide with principles, common dilemmas and decision steps.
This skill is written for a orthopaedic surgeon working with patients with bone, joint and muscle injuries or conditions in an orthopaedic theatre and fracture clinic.

## When to use
- You are a orthopaedic surgeon preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in an orthopaedic theatre and fracture clinic.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an ethics guide with principles, common dilemmas and decision steps.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Fracture | a break in a bone |
| Arthroplasty | surgical replacement of a joint |
| Compartment syndrome | dangerous pressure build-up within a muscle compartment |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Know the code that applies in an orthopaedic theatre and fracture clinic: consent, confidentiality, honesty, competence and conflicts of interest
2. **Step 2** - Work through dilemmas with a framework: facts, stakeholders, options, code provisions, then decide and record
3. **Step 3** - Protect patients with bone, joint and muscle injuries or conditions first where duties conflict, and be alert to how wrong-site surgery can arise from ethical shortcuts
4. **Step 4** - Declare conflicts of interest early and manage them transparently rather than hoping they stay invisible
5. **Step 5** - Raise concerns about others' practice through the proper channels, with evidence from operation notes, and support colleagues who do the same

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: wrong-site surgery.
- Never let this happen: compartment syndrome missed.
- Never let this happen: post-operative infection.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a orthopaedic surgeon; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to an orthopaedic theatre and fracture clinic's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
