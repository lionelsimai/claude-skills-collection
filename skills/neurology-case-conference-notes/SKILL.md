# Case Conference & Meeting Notes — Neurology

Prepare for, contribute to and record case conferences and multi-professional meetings. The deliverable is meeting preparation notes and a decision-focused record of outcomes.
This skill is written for a neurologist working with patients with brain, spinal cord and nerve disorders in a neurology department and outpatient clinic.

## When to use
- You are a neurologist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a neurology department and outpatient clinic.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces meeting preparation notes and a decision-focused record of outcomes.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| EEG | a recording of electrical activity in the brain |
| Lesion | an area of damaged tissue |
| Aura | warning symptoms some patients get before a seizure or migraine |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Prepare a one-page brief from neurological examination records: current position, key evidence, the questions the meeting must answer
2. **Step 2** - Present your contribution concisely, leading with the position on stroke assessment timeliness and any live concerns about missed stroke presentations
3. **Step 3** - Listen for information that changes the picture; update your understanding rather than defending a position
4. **Step 4** - Record decisions, actions, owners and deadlines during the meeting — not from memory afterwards
5. **Step 5** - Circulate the record promptly to attendees in a neurology department and outpatient clinic, correct errors fast, and file it with the main record

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a neurology department and outpatient clinic] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients with brain, spinal cord and nerve disorders and other stakeholders]

The finished output is meeting preparation notes and a decision-focused record of outcomes, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed stroke presentations.
- Never let this happen: status epilepticus delays.
- Never let this happen: medication interactions.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a neurologist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a neurology department and outpatient clinic's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
