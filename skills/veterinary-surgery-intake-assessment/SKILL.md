# Intake & Initial Assessment — Veterinary Surgery

Run a structured intake and initial assessment for animals and their owners in a veterinary practice and animal hospital. The deliverable is a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
This skill is written for a veterinary surgeon working with animals and their owners in a veterinary practice and animal hospital.

## When to use
- You are a veterinary surgeon preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a veterinary practice and animal hospital.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Zoonosis | a disease transmissible between animals and humans |
| Neutering | surgical removal of reproductive organs |
| Prophylaxis | treatment given to prevent disease |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm identity, consent and the reason for referral or engagement, and open the record that will hold clinical records
2. **Step 2** - Gather history and current circumstances from animals and their owners, using the standard intake structure for a veterinary practice and animal hospital
3. **Step 3** - Record baseline observations and measurements relevant to appointment waiting times, noting anything that changes the priority of the case
4. **Step 4** - Flag immediate concerns — especially anaesthetic incidents — and escalate or fast-track anything urgent before proceeding
5. **Step 5** - Summarise findings for animals and their owners in plain language, agree the next step, and file the intake section of clinical records

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: anaesthetic incidents.
- Never let this happen: zoonotic infection risk.
- Never let this happen: missed diagnoses from limited history.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a veterinary surgeon; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a veterinary practice and animal hospital's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
