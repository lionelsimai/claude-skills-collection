# Progress Review & Adjustment — Carpentry

Review progress for homeowners, builders and commercial clients against their plan and adjust it with evidence. The deliverable is a progress review summary with decisions and an updated plan.
This skill is written for a carpenter working with homeowners, builders and commercial clients in workshops and construction sites.

## When to use
- You are a carpenter preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in workshops and construction sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a progress review summary with decisions and an updated plan.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Snagging | fixing small defects at the end of a job |
| Mitred joint | a joint cut at an angle, typically 45 degrees |
| Stud wall | a framed internal wall of timber uprights |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Pull together cutting lists and the latest results for job completion times since the last review
2. **Step 2** - Compare actual progress with the plan's goals and timelines; identify what moved, what stalled and why
3. **Step 3** - Discuss the evidence with homeowners, builders and commercial clients honestly, including where the original plan no longer fits
4. **Step 4** - Agree adjustments: continue, intensify, change approach or close — and record the reasoning
5. **Step 5** - Update the plan and review date, and communicate changes to everyone involved in workshops and construction sites

The finished output is a progress review summary with decisions and an updated plan, structured as:

2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan

2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against job completion times.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in workshops and construction sites.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: machinery injuries.
- Never let this happen: measurement errors wasting material.
- Never let this happen: structural work done wrong.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a carpenter; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to workshops and construction sites's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
