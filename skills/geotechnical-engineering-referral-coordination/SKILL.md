# Referral & Coordination — Geotechnical Engineering

Refer developers, contractors and infrastructure owners onward and coordinate with other professionals and services. The deliverable is a referral pack and a coordination log with responses tracked.
This skill is written for a geotechnical engineer working with developers, contractors and infrastructure owners in site investigation teams and design offices.

## When to use
- You are a geotechnical engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in site investigation teams and design offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a referral pack and a coordination log with responses tracked.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Borehole | a drilled hole used to sample ground and groundwater |
| Bearing capacity | the load the ground can safely support |
| Settlement | downward movement of the ground or a structure |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm the referral is appropriate: what developers, contractors and infrastructure owners need that sits outside the geotechnical engineer scope in site investigation teams and design offices
2. **Step 2** - Prepare the referral from ground investigation reports — relevant history, current status, the specific question being asked
3. **Step 3** - With consent, send the referral and log who received it and when; chase non-responses on a set schedule
4. **Step 4** - Share incoming findings back into the main record so the plan and investigation turnaround stay joined up
5. **Step 5** - Close the loop with developers, contractors and infrastructure owners: explain what happens next, who owns it, and what to do if things worsen — mindful of ground conditions misjudged

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: ground conditions misjudged.
- Never let this happen: slope failures.
- Never let this happen: foundation settlement.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a geotechnical engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to site investigation teams and design offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
