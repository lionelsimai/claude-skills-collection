# Scheduling & Workload Management — Management Accounting

Plan schedules, caseloads and turnaround times for a management accountant in a finance department. The deliverable is a workable schedule template with priorities, buffers and escalation rules.
This skill is written for a management accountant working with business managers and leadership teams in a finance department.

## When to use
- You are a management accountant preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a finance department.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a workable schedule template with priorities, buffers and escalation rules.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Variance | the difference between budgeted and actual figures |
| Forecast | a prediction of future financial performance |
| Cost centre | a unit of a business tracked for its costs |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Map demand: appointment or job types, durations, and the deadlines that drive report delivery timeliness
2. **Step 2** - Build the schedule around fixed commitments in a finance department first, then fit flexible work around them
3. **Step 3** - Protect buffer time for overruns, urgent cases and the admin behind management accounts — schedules without buffers fail by week two
4. **Step 4** - Define triage rules so urgent work (for example anything involving misleading forecasts) displaces routine work predictably
5. **Step 5** - Review the schedule weekly against actuals and adjust booking rules, staffing or ERP systems availability

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: misleading forecasts.
- Never let this happen: cost allocations distorted.
- Never let this happen: deadline slippage.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a management accountant; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a finance department's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
