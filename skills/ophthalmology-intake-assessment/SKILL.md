# Intake & Initial Assessment — Ophthalmology

Run a structured intake and initial assessment for patients with eye conditions and vision problems in an eye hospital clinic and theatre. The deliverable is a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
This skill is written for a ophthalmologist working with patients with eye conditions and vision problems in an eye hospital clinic and theatre.

## When to use
- You are a ophthalmologist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in an eye hospital clinic and theatre.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Visual acuity | the sharpness of vision, often recorded as a fraction |
| OCT | a scan showing cross-sections of the retina |
| Glaucoma | optic nerve damage often linked to raised eye pressure |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm identity, consent and the reason for referral or engagement, and open the record that will hold visual acuity charts
2. **Step 2** - Gather history and current circumstances from patients with eye conditions and vision problems, using the standard intake structure for an eye hospital clinic and theatre
3. **Step 3** - Record baseline observations and measurements relevant to cataract surgery outcomes, noting anything that changes the priority of the case
4. **Step 4** - Flag immediate concerns — especially missed glaucoma progression — and escalate or fast-track anything urgent before proceeding
5. **Step 5** - Summarise findings for patients with eye conditions and vision problems in plain language, agree the next step, and file the intake section of visual acuity charts

2. Detail, following the workflow above

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed glaucoma progression.
- Never let this happen: endophthalmitis after surgery.
- Never let this happen: wrong lens implant.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a ophthalmologist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to an eye hospital clinic and theatre's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
