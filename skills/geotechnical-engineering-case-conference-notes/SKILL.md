# Case Conference & Meeting Notes — Geotechnical Engineering

Prepare for, contribute to and record case conferences and multi-professional meetings. The deliverable is meeting preparation notes and a decision-focused record of outcomes.
This skill is written for a geotechnical engineer working with developers, contractors and infrastructure owners in site investigation teams and design offices.

## When to use
- You are a geotechnical engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in site investigation teams and design offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces meeting preparation notes and a decision-focused record of outcomes.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Borehole | a drilled hole used to sample ground and groundwater |
| Bearing capacity | the load the ground can safely support |
| Settlement | downward movement of the ground or a structure |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Prepare a one-page brief from ground investigation reports: current position, key evidence, the questions the meeting must answer
2. **Step 2** - Present your contribution concisely, leading with the position on investigation turnaround and any live concerns about ground conditions misjudged
3. **Step 3** - Listen for information that changes the picture; update your understanding rather than defending a position
4. **Step 4** - Record decisions, actions, owners and deadlines during the meeting — not from memory afterwards
5. **Step 5** - Circulate the record promptly to attendees in site investigation teams and design offices, correct errors fast, and file it with the main record

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in site investigation teams and design offices] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [developers, contractors and infrastructure owners and other stakeholders]

The finished output is meeting preparation notes and a decision-focused record of outcomes, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in ground investigation reports.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against investigation turnaround.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in site investigation teams and design offices.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: ground conditions misjudged.
- Never let this happen: slope failures.
- Never let this happen: foundation settlement.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a geotechnical engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to site investigation teams and design offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
