# Follow-Up & Maintenance Schedule — Dementia Care

Plan follow-up and maintenance so gains for people living with dementia and their families are checked and sustained. The deliverable is a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers.
This skill is written for a dementia care specialist working with people living with dementia and their families in care homes, hospitals and homes.

## When to use
- You are a dementia care specialist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in care homes, hospitals and homes.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Life story work | gathering a person's history to personalise care |
| Capacity | a person's ability to make a specific decision |
| Reminiscence | using memories and familiar items to support wellbeing |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Set follow-up intervals from the plan and from how distress incident rates is expected to move over time
2. **Step 2** - Define what is checked at each contact, using memory aids where measurement is needed, and record it in life story documents
3. **Step 3** - Give people living with dementia and their families clear guidance for the interval: what to do, what to watch for, when to make contact sooner
4. **Step 4** - Act early on warning signs — deterioration linked to distress misread as behaviour problems shortens the interval, never lengthens it
5. **Step 5** - Close the follow-up cycle formally when goals are met, with a summary and re-entry route if needs return

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in care homes, hospitals and homes] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [people living with dementia and their families and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in life story documents.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against distress incident rates.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in care homes, hospitals and homes.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: distress misread as behaviour problems.
- Never let this happen: unsafe wandering.
- Never let this happen: families not involved.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a dementia care specialist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to care homes, hospitals and homes's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
