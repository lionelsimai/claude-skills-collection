# Handover Briefing — Conveyancing

Hand over home buyers, sellers and lenders or active work to another professional without losing critical detail. The deliverable is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
This skill is written for a conveyancer working with home buyers, sellers and lenders in a conveyancing firm.

## When to use
- You are a conveyancer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a conveyancing firm.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Exchange | when a property sale becomes legally binding |
| Title | the legal evidence of property ownership |
| Stamp duty | a tax payable on property purchases in many jurisdictions |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Summarise the current position from contracts: what is done, what is in progress, what is waiting
2. **Step 2** - Highlight live risks first — missed exchange deadlines — and the controls or watchpoints already in place
3. **Step 3** - State the next actions with owners and deadlines, and the results expected for exchange-to-completion times
4. **Step 4** - Walk the receiving professional through the brief, answer questions, and confirm understanding
5. **Step 5** - Record that handover happened, who accepted it, and where the full record lives in a conveyancing firm

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a conveyancing firm] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [home buyers, sellers and lenders and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in contracts.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against exchange-to-completion times.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in a conveyancing firm.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed exchange deadlines.
- Never let this happen: title defects overlooked.
- Never let this happen: fraud attempts undetected.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a conveyancer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a conveyancing firm's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
