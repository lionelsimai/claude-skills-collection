# Risk Assessment — Medical Laboratory Science

Identify, score and control the risks in work for patients whose diagnosis depends on laboratory results, via clinical teams in a hospital pathology laboratory. The deliverable is a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners.
This skill is written for a biomedical scientist working with patients whose diagnosis depends on laboratory results, via clinical teams in a hospital pathology laboratory.

## When to use
- You are a biomedical scientist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a hospital pathology laboratory.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Quality control | checks that a test system is performing correctly |
| Turnaround time | time from sample receipt to result |
| Haemolysis | breakdown of red blood cells that can spoil a sample |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Map the activity step by step and list what could go wrong at each stage for patients whose diagnosis depends on laboratory results, via clinical teams and staff
2. **Step 2** - Score each risk for likelihood and impact, giving special weight to sample mislabelling
3. **Step 3** - For every high risk, define a control that removes, reduces or contains it, using laboratory analysers where relevant
4. **Step 4** - Record the assessment in test requests, assign an owner to each control, and set a review date
5. **Step 5** - Brief everyone involved before work starts; stop and reassess if conditions in a hospital pathology laboratory change

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: sample mislabelling.
- Never let this happen: delayed critical results.
- Never let this happen: analyser faults unnoticed.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a biomedical scientist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a hospital pathology laboratory's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
