# Feedback & Complaints Handling — Commercial Diving

Collect feedback from offshore, construction and salvage clients and handle complaints fairly and visibly. The deliverable is a feedback and complaints process with response standards and learning log.
This skill is written for a commercial diver working with offshore, construction and salvage clients in underwater work sites and support vessels.

## When to use
- You are a commercial diver preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in underwater work sites and support vessels.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a feedback and complaints process with response standards and learning log.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Umbilical | the cable and hoses supplying air and communications to a diver |
| Decompression | controlled ascent stops letting gases leave the body safely |
| Dive plan | the documented method and limits for a dive |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Invite feedback routinely — not only when things go wrong — and make it easy to give in underwater work sites and support vessels
2. **Step 2** - Acknowledge complaints quickly, thank the person, and explain the process and timeframe honestly
3. **Step 3** - Investigate from evidence in dive plans; separate the facts from the feelings, and respect both
4. **Step 4** - Respond with findings, an apology where warranted, and concrete remedies or explanations
5. **Step 5** - Log themes and feed them into quality work — repeated complaints about decompression sickness are an early-warning system, not a nuisance

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: decompression sickness.
- Never let this happen: equipment failure underwater.
- Never let this happen: communication loss.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a commercial diver; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to underwater work sites and support vessels's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
