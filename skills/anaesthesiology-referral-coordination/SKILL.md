# Referral & Coordination — Anaesthesiology

Refer patients undergoing surgery or painful procedures onward and coordinate with other professionals and services. The deliverable is a referral pack and a coordination log with responses tracked.
This skill is written for a anaesthetist working with patients undergoing surgery or painful procedures in operating theatres and recovery units.

## When to use
- You are a anaesthetist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in operating theatres and recovery units.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a referral pack and a coordination log with responses tracked.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Airway | the passage through which a patient breathes under anaesthesia |
| Induction | the phase of starting anaesthesia |
| PONV | post-operative nausea and vomiting |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm the referral is appropriate: what patients undergoing surgery or painful procedures need that sits outside the anaesthetist scope in operating theatres and recovery units
2. **Step 2** - Prepare the referral from anaesthetic charts — relevant history, current status, the specific question being asked
3. **Step 3** - With consent, send the referral and log who received it and when; chase non-responses on a set schedule
4. **Step 4** - Share incoming findings back into the main record so the plan and pre-operative assessment completion stay joined up
5. **Step 5** - Close the loop with patients undergoing surgery or painful procedures: explain what happens next, who owns it, and what to do if things worsen — mindful of airway emergencies

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in operating theatres and recovery units] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients undergoing surgery or painful procedures and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a referral pack and a coordination log with responses tracked, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in anaesthetic charts.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against pre-operative assessment completion.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in operating theatres and recovery units.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: airway emergencies.
- Never let this happen: drug errors under pressure.
- Never let this happen: post-operative respiratory depression.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a anaesthetist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to operating theatres and recovery units's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
