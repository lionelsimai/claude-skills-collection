# Safety Protocol — Disability Support Work

Work safely in homes, day services and community settings: a practical safety protocol for a disability support worker and people with disabilities and their families. The deliverable is a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions.
This skill is written for a disability support worker working with people with disabilities and their families in homes, day services and community settings.

## When to use
- You are a disability support worker preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in homes, day services and community settings.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Identify the hazards present in homes, day services and community settings for staff, people with disabilities and their families and visitors, starting with personal care done unsafely
2. **Step 2** - Write controls in order: eliminate, substitute, engineer, administrate, then protective equipment such as assistive technology
3. **Step 3** - Set pre-start checks and stop-work authority — anyone can halt work that looks unsafe, without penalty
4. **Step 4** - Define what happens when something goes wrong: first response, escalation, recording in support plans
5. **Step 5** - Review the protocol after every incident and at least annually; brief and re-brief everyone it covers

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in homes, day services and community settings] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [people with disabilities and their families and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in support plans.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against goal progress.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in homes, day services and community settings.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: personal care done unsafely.
- Never let this happen: communication needs ignored.
- Never let this happen: restrictive practices misused.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a disability support worker; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to homes, day services and community settings's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
