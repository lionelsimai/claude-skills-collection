# Safety Protocol — Port Operations

Work safely in ports and container terminals: a practical safety protocol for a port operations manager and shipping lines, hauliers and cargo owners. The deliverable is a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions.
This skill is written for a port operations manager working with shipping lines, hauliers and cargo owners in ports and container terminals.

## When to use
- You are a port operations manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in ports and container terminals.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Berth | the place where a vessel moors to load or unload |
| TEU | a standard unit equal to a twenty-foot container |
| Turnaround | the time a vessel or truck spends in port |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Identify the hazards present in ports and container terminals for staff, shipping lines, hauliers and cargo owners and visitors, starting with crane accidents
2. **Step 2** - Write controls in order: eliminate, substitute, engineer, administrate, then protective equipment such as terminal operating systems
3. **Step 3** - Set pre-start checks and stop-work authority — anyone can halt work that looks unsafe, without penalty
4. **Step 4** - Define what happens when something goes wrong: first response, escalation, recording in berth plans
5. **Step 5** - Review the protocol after every incident and at least annually; brief and re-brief everyone it covers
