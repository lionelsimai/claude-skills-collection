# Handover Briefing — Conservation Architecture

Hand over heritage bodies, owners of historic buildings and councils or active work to another professional without losing critical detail. The deliverable is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
This skill is written for a conservation architect working with heritage bodies, owners of historic buildings and councils in historic buildings and design studios.

## When to use
- You are a conservation architect preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in historic buildings and design studios.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Listed building | a building protected by law for its heritage value |
| Fabric | the physical material of a historic building |
| Reversibility | repairs that can be undone without harming original material |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Summarise the current position from condition surveys: what is done, what is in progress, what is waiting
2. **Step 2** - Highlight live risks first — inappropriate repairs damaging fabric — and the controls or watchpoints already in place
3. **Step 3** - State the next actions with owners and deadlines, and the results expected for survey turnaround
4. **Step 4** - Walk the receiving professional through the brief, answer questions, and confirm understanding
5. **Step 5** - Record that handover happened, who accepted it, and where the full record lives in historic buildings and design studios

## Output Format

- Never let this happen: inappropriate repairs damaging fabric.
- Never let this happen: consent refusals.
- Never let this happen: hidden defects.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a conservation architect; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to historic buildings and design studios's rules.
