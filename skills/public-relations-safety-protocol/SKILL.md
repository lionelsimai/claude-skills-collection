# Safety Protocol — Public Relations

Work safely in PR agencies and client organisations: a practical safety protocol for a public relations consultant and clients, journalists and stakeholders. The deliverable is a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions.
This skill is written for a public relations consultant working with clients, journalists and stakeholders in PR agencies and client organisations.

## When to use
- You are a public relations consultant preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in PR agencies and client organisations.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Embargo | information released for publication at a set future time |
| Coverage | media reporting about a client or story |
| Key message | a core point a campaign aims to communicate |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Identify the hazards present in PR agencies and client organisations for staff, clients, journalists and stakeholders and visitors, starting with inaccurate statements to media
2. **Step 2** - Write controls in order: eliminate, substitute, engineer, administrate, then protective equipment such as media databases
3. **Step 3** - Set pre-start checks and stop-work authority — anyone can halt work that looks unsafe, without penalty
4. **Step 4** - Define what happens when something goes wrong: first response, escalation, recording in media lists
5. **Step 5** - Review the protocol after every incident and at least annually; brief and re-brief everyone it covers

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in PR agencies and client organisations] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [clients, journalists and stakeholders and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: inaccurate statements to media.
- Never let this happen: crises mishandled.
- Never let this happen: embargo breaches.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a public relations consultant; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to PR agencies and client organisations's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
