# Emergency Response Procedure — Learning & Development

Respond to emergencies in organisations across all sectors calmly, in role and in the right order. The deliverable is an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
This skill is written for a learning and development specialist working with employees and organisational leaders in organisations across all sectors.

## When to use
- You are a learning and development specialist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in organisations across all sectors.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Recognise the emergency early: know the presentations that matter for employees and organisational leaders, especially training that changes nothing
2. **Step 2** - Take the immediate actions a learning and development specialist is trained and authorised for, using learning platforms as drilled
3. **Step 3** - Call for help with a structured message: location in organisations across all sectors, situation, what has been done, what is needed
4. **Step 4** - Keep employees and organisational leaders and bystanders informed and safe while help arrives; hand over with a concise summary
5. **Step 5** - Record the event fully in training needs analyses afterwards, debrief the team, and update the procedure with lessons learned

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: training that changes nothing.
- Never let this happen: accessibility overlooked.
- Never let this happen: evaluation skipped.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a learning and development specialist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to organisations across all sectors's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
