# New Client Onboarding Pack — Pulmonology

Onboard patients with lung and breathing conditions smoothly into a pulmonologist's service in a respiratory ward and lung function laboratory. The deliverable is an onboarding pack: welcome information, expectations, first steps and contacts.
This skill is written for a pulmonologist working with patients with lung and breathing conditions in a respiratory ward and lung function laboratory.

## When to use
- You are a pulmonologist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a respiratory ward and lung function laboratory.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an onboarding pack: welcome information, expectations, first steps and contacts.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Spirometry | a breathing test measuring how much and how fast air moves |
| Exacerbation | a sudden worsening of a chronic condition |
| Hypoxia | abnormally low oxygen levels in the body |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Send a welcome pack before the first appointment: what the service does, what happens first, what to bring from spirometry reports
2. **Step 2** - Set expectations honestly — timescales, costs where relevant, and how progress on spirometry quality rate will be shared
3. **Step 3** - Cover rights and responsibilities on both sides, including confidentiality limits and how to raise concerns
4. **Step 4** - Make the first contact easy: named contact, directions to a respiratory ward and lung function laboratory, and what to do if plans change
5. **Step 5** - Check in after the first session or milestone and fix friction early, before it becomes disengagement or missed lung cancer signs

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a respiratory ward and lung function laboratory] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients with lung and breathing conditions and other stakeholders]

The finished output is an onboarding pack: welcome information, expectations, first steps and contacts, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in spirometry reports.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against spirometry quality rate.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in a respiratory ward and lung function laboratory.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed lung cancer signs.
- Never let this happen: ventilator-associated harm.
- Never let this happen: untreated sleep apnoea.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a pulmonologist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a respiratory ward and lung function laboratory's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
