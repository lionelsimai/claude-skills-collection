# Assessment & Findings Report — Air Traffic Control

Assess pilots, airlines and airport operators and write a defensible findings report as a air traffic controller. The deliverable is a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
This skill is written for a air traffic controller working with pilots, airlines and airport operators in control towers and radar centres.

## When to use
- You are a air traffic controller preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in control towers and radar centres.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Separation | the safe distance maintained between aircraft |
| Handover | transferring responsibility for traffic between controllers |
| Squawk | the transponder code identifying an aircraft on radar |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Review the intake record and traffic logs so the assessment builds on verified information, not assumptions
2. **Step 2** - Carry out the assessment using radar systems, following the recognised method for control towers and radar centres
3. **Step 3** - Compare findings against the benchmarks behind traffic throughput and note where evidence is strong, weak or missing
4. **Step 4** - Draft the report: findings first, then interpretation, then recommendations in priority order
5. **Step 5** - Check the draft for accuracy and for loss of separation; have it reviewed where the profession's standards require a second sign-off

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in control towers and radar centres] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [pilots, airlines and airport operators and other stakeholders]

2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: loss of separation.
- Never let this happen: communication misunderstandings.
- Never let this happen: fatigue on shift.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a air traffic controller; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to control towers and radar centres's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
