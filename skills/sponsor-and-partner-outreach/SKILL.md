# Sponsor and Partner Outreach

## Work a partnership ladder, not one-off asks

- Lead with the partner result: the audience they reach, the room they get, and the pipeline that follows. Scope, owner, timeline and the measure of success come before any price talk.

- Offer a small entry first, such as a single sponsored room or session, with a clear annual partner tier above it for partners who want the year, not the night.

- Set a named pipeline quota for every sponsored activity. An activity with no quota is a cost, not a channel.

## Draft the outreach

- Open with a true signal about the partner, such as an APAC expansion, a launch or a community push.

- Name the audience with verified numbers only. Every audience, attendance and reach figure must match the reconciled source count before it appears in any proposal or email.

- Make the ask specific: a 20 minute call, one named entry offer, and one line on what the annual tier adds.

## Hold the approval line

- Never send a proposal, a price or a claim without the user's explicit approval of that exact version.

- Log every ask made, with the date, the tier offered and the reply. An unanswered ask gets one follow-up, then it rests until there is a new signal.

## When to use

Use this skill when the task matches its description above and you need a repeatable, checked workflow rather than an improvised answer.

## Guardrails and quality checks

- Never fabricate facts, numbers, clients or results. Use only sources the user provides or verifies.
- Keep drafts as drafts until a human approves any external send, payment or commitment.
- Check the final output against the workflow above before delivering it, and state anything you could not verify.

## Output format and example

Deliver the result in the structure the workflow defines. Example request: "Use the Sponsor and Partner Outreach skill for my situation: [describe the business, audience and goal]." Example output: a completed, checked deliverable following every step above, with open questions listed at the end rather than guessed.
