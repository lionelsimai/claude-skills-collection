# Quality Audit — Tax Law

Audit the quality of tax solicitor work in a law firm and tax tribunals and drive measurable improvement. The deliverable is an audit report with scores, evidence and an improvement plan.
This skill is written for a tax solicitor working with individuals, businesses and charities with tax questions in a law firm and tax tribunals.

## When to use
- You are a tax solicitor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a law firm and tax tribunals.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an audit report with scores, evidence and an improvement plan.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Clearance | advance confirmation from a tax authority about treatment of a transaction |
| Disclosure | informing the tax authority of relevant facts |
| Penalty mitigation | reducing penalties through cooperation and care |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Define the audit sample and criteria up front, anchored to opinion turnaround and the standards for a law firm and tax tribunals
2. **Step 2** - Review a sample of tax opinions and observe practice where permitted, scoring consistently
3. **Step 3** - Separate isolated errors from patterns — patterns point to system fixes, not individual blame
4. **Step 4** - Report findings with evidence, and agree improvement actions that address the root causes, including filing deadlines missed
5. **Step 5** - Re-audit after the agreed interval and publish the change in scores so improvement is visible

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: filing deadlines missed.
- Never let this happen: penalties from wrong advice.
- Never let this happen: privilege mishandled.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a tax solicitor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a law firm and tax tribunals's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
