# Service Plan Design — Podiatry

Design a realistic, measurable service plan for patients with foot and lower limb conditions as a podiatrist. The deliverable is a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates.
This skill is written for a podiatrist working with patients with foot and lower limb conditions in a podiatry clinic and community settings.

## When to use
- You are a podiatrist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a podiatry clinic and community settings.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Ulcer | an open sore that is slow to heal |
| Biomechanics | how the body moves and bears load |
| Neuropathy | nerve damage reducing sensation, common in diabetes |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Start from the assessment findings and agree goals with patients with foot and lower limb conditions — specific enough to measure against ulcer healing rates
2. **Step 2** - Break each goal into actions sequenced for a podiatry clinic and community settings, naming who does what and by when
3. **Step 3** - Build the plan around foot assessments so progress, changes and decisions are recorded in one place
4. **Step 4** - Price or resource the plan honestly: time, surgical instruments and materials, with contingencies for diabetic foot ulcers missed
5. **Step 5** - Set the review date and the trigger points that would change the plan, and confirm the plan with all parties

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a podiatry clinic and community settings] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients with foot and lower limb conditions and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: diabetic foot ulcers missed.
- Never let this happen: infection after nail surgery.
- Never let this happen: vascular problems unrecognised.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a podiatrist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a podiatry clinic and community settings's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
