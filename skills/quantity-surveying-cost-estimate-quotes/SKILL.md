# Cost Estimates & Quotes — Quantity Surveying

Build transparent, defensible cost estimates and quotes as a quantity surveyor. The deliverable is an itemised estimate with assumptions, exclusions and validity terms.
This skill is written for a quantity surveyor working with developers, contractors and public clients in construction projects and offices.

## When to use
- You are a quantity surveyor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in construction projects and offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an itemised estimate with assumptions, exclusions and validity terms.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Bill of quantities | an itemised list of materials and work for pricing |
| Valuation | an assessment of work completed for interim payment |
| Final account | the agreed total cost at a project's end |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Scope the work precisely from the assessment and bills of quantities; list what is included and excluded
2. **Step 2** - Cost each element: professional time, measurement software usage, materials, travel and third-party fees
3. **Step 3** - Add contingency proportionate to uncertainty — and say so openly rather than burying it, which protects against cost overruns unreported
4. **Step 4** - Present the quote with options where genuine choices exist, and relate value to outcomes such as valuation timeliness
5. **Step 5** - Record acceptance, variations and final costs against the estimate so future quotes get more accurate

- Never let this happen: cost overruns unreported.
- Never let this happen: measurement errors.
- Never let this happen: contractual deadlines missed.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a quantity surveyor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to construction projects and offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
