# Emergency Response Procedure — Secondary Mathematics Education

Respond to emergencies in a secondary school mathematics department calmly, in role and in the right order. The deliverable is an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
This skill is written for a secondary mathematics teacher working with secondary school students and their parents in a secondary school mathematics department.

## When to use
- You are a secondary mathematics teacher preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a secondary school mathematics department.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

| Term | Meaning |

| Scheme of work | the planned sequence of topics across a term or year |
| Intervention | targeted extra teaching for pupils who are behind |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Recognise the emergency early: know the presentations that matter for secondary school students and their parents, especially misconceptions unaddressed
2. **Step 2** - Take the immediate actions a secondary mathematics teacher is trained and authorised for, using graphing software as drilled
3. **Step 3** - Call for help with a structured message: location in a secondary school mathematics department, situation, what has been done, what is needed
4. **Step 4** - Keep secondary school students and their parents and bystanders informed and safe while help arrives; hand over with a concise summary
5. **Step 5** - Record the event fully in schemes of work afterwards, debrief the team, and update the procedure with lessons learned

- Never let this happen: misconceptions unaddressed.
- Never let this happen: exam entry errors.
- Never let this happen: low-attaining pupils overlooked.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a secondary mathematics teacher; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a secondary school mathematics department's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
