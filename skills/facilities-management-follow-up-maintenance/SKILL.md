# Follow-Up & Maintenance Schedule — Facilities Management

Plan follow-up and maintenance so gains for building occupants, owners and service teams are checked and sustained. The deliverable is a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers.
This skill is written for a facilities manager working with building occupants, owners and service teams in commercial buildings and campuses.

## When to use
- You are a facilities manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in commercial buildings and campuses.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| CAFM | computer-aided facilities management software |
| PPM | planned preventive maintenance scheduled in advance |
| Asset register | the inventory of building equipment and its condition |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Set follow-up intervals from the plan and from how work order completion is expected to move over time
2. **Step 2** - Define what is checked at each contact, using CAFM systems where measurement is needed, and record it in maintenance schedules
3. **Step 3** - Give building occupants, owners and service teams clear guidance for the interval: what to do, what to watch for, when to make contact sooner
4. **Step 4** - Act early on warning signs — deterioration linked to life-safety systems unmaintained shortens the interval, never lengthens it
5. **Step 5** - Close the follow-up cycle formally when goals are met, with a summary and re-entry route if needs return

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: life-safety systems unmaintained.
- Never let this happen: contractor failures.
- Never let this happen: energy waste.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a facilities manager; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to commercial buildings and campuses's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
