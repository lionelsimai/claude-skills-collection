# Mentoring & Training Junior Staff — Appliance Repair

Train and mentor junior colleagues toward competent, safe appliance repair technician practice. The deliverable is a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
This skill is written for a appliance repair technician working with homeowners, landlords and retailers in customers' homes and workshops.

## When to use
- You are a appliance repair technician preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in customers' homes and workshops.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Diagnostic code | a fault code displayed by an appliance's controls |
| PAT testing | checking portable appliances for electrical safety |
| Planned obsolescence | designing products with a limited lifespan |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Assess the trainee's current level honestly against the competencies required in customers' homes and workshops
2. **Step 2** - Sequence learning from observation to supervised practice to independent work, using multimeters under supervision first
3. **Step 3** - Set explicit sign-off criteria for high-risk tasks — especially anything touching electric shock — and never sign off early
4. **Step 4** - Give feedback from direct observation and from job cards, specific and timely, with strengths named too
5. **Step 5** - Record progress, keep the training file current, and escalate persistent gaps through supervision, not gossip

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: electric shock.
- Never let this happen: gas appliance risks.
- Never let this happen: repeat faults.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a appliance repair technician; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to customers' homes and workshops's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
