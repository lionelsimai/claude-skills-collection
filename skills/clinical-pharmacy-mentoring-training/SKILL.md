# Mentoring & Training Junior Staff — Clinical Pharmacy

Train and mentor junior colleagues toward competent, safe clinical pharmacist practice. The deliverable is a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
This skill is written for a clinical pharmacist working with patients with complex medication needs and prescribing teams in hospital wards and clinics.

## When to use
- You are a clinical pharmacist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in hospital wards and clinics.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Polypharmacy | the use of many medicines by one patient |
| Formulary | the approved list of medicines for an organisation |
| Titration | adjusting a dose gradually to effect |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Assess the trainee's current level honestly against the competencies required in hospital wards and clinics
2. **Step 2** - Sequence learning from observation to supervised practice to independent work, using electronic prescribing systems under supervision first
3. **Step 3** - Set explicit sign-off criteria for high-risk tasks — especially anything touching dangerous interactions missed — and never sign off early
4. **Step 4** - Give feedback from direct observation and from medication reviews, specific and timely, with strengths named too
5. **Step 5** - Record progress, keep the training file current, and escalate persistent gaps through supervision, not gossip

## Profession-Specific Components
| Component | Details for Clinical Pharmacy |
|-----------|-------------------------------|
| Core documentation | medication reviews; prescription charts; intervention logs; discharge medication lists |
| Core tools & equipment | electronic prescribing systems; medicines reference databases; inhaler technique devices; tablet counters |
| People involved | patients with complex medication needs and prescribing teams; the clinical pharmacist; colleagues and other services in hospital wards and clinics |
| Primary record | medication reviews — keep it current, dated and attributable |

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in hospital wards and clinics] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients with complex medication needs and prescribing teams and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in medication reviews.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against medication review completion.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in hospital wards and clinics.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: dangerous interactions missed.
- Never let this happen: look-alike medicine confusion.
- Never let this happen: discharge medicines errors.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a clinical pharmacist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to hospital wards and clinics's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
