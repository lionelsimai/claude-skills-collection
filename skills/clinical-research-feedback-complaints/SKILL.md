# Feedback & Complaints Handling — Clinical Research

Collect feedback from trial participants, sponsors and investigators and handle complaints fairly and visibly. The deliverable is a feedback and complaints process with response standards and learning log.
This skill is written for a clinical research practitioner working with trial participants, sponsors and investigators in hospitals and research sites.

## When to use
- You are a clinical research practitioner preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in hospitals and research sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a feedback and complaints process with response standards and learning log.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Protocol | the detailed plan for a clinical trial |
| Informed consent | a participant's voluntary agreement after full explanation |
| Adverse event | an untoward medical occurrence in a participant |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Invite feedback routinely — not only when things go wrong — and make it easy to give in hospitals and research sites
2. **Step 2** - Acknowledge complaints quickly, thank the person, and explain the process and timeframe honestly
3. **Step 3** - Investigate from evidence in protocols; separate the facts from the feelings, and respect both
4. **Step 4** - Respond with findings, an apology where warranted, and concrete remedies or explanations
5. **Step 5** - Log themes and feed them into quality work — repeated complaints about consent failures are an early-warning system, not a nuisance

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in hospitals and research sites] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [trial participants, sponsors and investigators and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a feedback and complaints process with response standards and learning log, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan

2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against recruitment to target.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in hospitals and research sites.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: consent failures.
- Never let this happen: protocol deviations unreported.
- Never let this happen: participant safety signals missed.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a clinical research practitioner; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to hospitals and research sites's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
