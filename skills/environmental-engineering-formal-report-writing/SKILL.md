# Formal Report Writing — Environmental Engineering

Write formal reports as a environmental engineer that stand up to scrutiny from any reader. The deliverable is a report template with structure, evidence standards and review steps.
This skill is written for a environmental engineer working with water companies, industries and regulators in treatment plants, client sites and design offices.

## When to use
- You are a environmental engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in treatment plants, client sites and design offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a report template with structure, evidence standards and review steps.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Consent | permission to discharge treated water or waste |
| Effluent | liquid waste discharged from a process |
| Remediation | cleaning up contaminated land or water |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Establish the report's purpose, audience and deadline before writing; gather source material from impact assessments
2. **Step 2** - Structure: summary, background, method, findings, analysis, recommendations — in that order
3. **Step 3** - Write findings factually and separately from opinion; label professional judgement as judgement
4. **Step 4** - Support key points with evidence and with permit compliance where outcomes are claimed; acknowledge limitations openly
5. **Step 5** - Proofread for accuracy and tone, check names, dates and figures twice, and consider pollution incidents before release

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: pollution incidents.
- Never let this happen: consent breaches.
- Never let this happen: design underperformance.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a environmental engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to treatment plants, client sites and design offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
