# Service Plan Design — Customs Brokerage

Design a realistic, measurable service plan for importers and exporters as a customs broker. The deliverable is a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates.
This skill is written for a customs broker working with importers and exporters in customs offices and freight operations.

## When to use
- You are a customs broker preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in customs offices and freight operations.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Tariff classification | assigning the correct commodity code to goods |
| Duty | tax charged on imported goods |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Start from the assessment findings and agree goals with importers and exporters — specific enough to measure against declaration accuracy
2. **Step 2** - Break each goal into actions sequenced for customs offices and freight operations, naming who does what and by when
3. **Step 3** - Build the plan around customs declarations so progress, changes and decisions are recorded in one place
4. **Step 4** - Price or resource the plan honestly: time, customs software and materials, with contingencies for misclassification penalties
5. **Step 5** - Set the review date and the trigger points that would change the plan, and confirm the plan with all parties

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in customs offices and freight operations] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [importers and exporters and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: misclassification penalties.
- Never let this happen: clearance delays.
- Never let this happen: prohibited goods shipped.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a customs broker; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to customs offices and freight operations's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
