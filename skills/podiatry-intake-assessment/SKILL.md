# Intake & Initial Assessment — Podiatry

Run a structured intake and initial assessment for patients with foot and lower limb conditions in a podiatry clinic and community settings. The deliverable is a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
This skill is written for a podiatrist working with patients with foot and lower limb conditions in a podiatry clinic and community settings.

## When to use
- You are a podiatrist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a podiatry clinic and community settings.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Ulcer | an open sore that is slow to heal |
| Biomechanics | how the body moves and bears load |
| Neuropathy | nerve damage reducing sensation, common in diabetes |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm identity, consent and the reason for referral or engagement, and open the record that will hold foot assessments
2. **Step 2** - Gather history and current circumstances from patients with foot and lower limb conditions, using the standard intake structure for a podiatry clinic and community settings
3. **Step 3** - Record baseline observations and measurements relevant to ulcer healing rates, noting anything that changes the priority of the case
4. **Step 4** - Flag immediate concerns — especially diabetic foot ulcers missed — and escalate or fast-track anything urgent before proceeding
5. **Step 5** - Summarise findings for patients with foot and lower limb conditions in plain language, agree the next step, and file the intake section of foot assessments

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: diabetic foot ulcers missed.
- Never let this happen: infection after nail surgery.
- Never let this happen: vascular problems unrecognised.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a podiatrist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a podiatry clinic and community settings's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
