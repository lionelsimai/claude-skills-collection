# Records & Documentation — Midwifery

Create and maintain accurate, auditable records for work with pregnant and postnatal women and newborn babies. The deliverable is a documentation standard, templates and a completed record set.
This skill is written for a midwife working with pregnant and postnatal women and newborn babies in a maternity unit, birth centre and community clinics.

## When to use
- You are a midwife preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a maternity unit, birth centre and community clinics.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a documentation standard, templates and a completed record set.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Postnatal | the period after birth |
| Continuity of carer | care from the same midwife or small team |
| Fundal height | measurement used to track baby growth in pregnancy |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Use antenatal records as the single source of truth; record facts, observations and decisions — not speculation
2. **Step 2** - Write entries contemporaneously where possible, dated and attributable, in the format a maternity unit, birth centre and community clinics requires
3. **Step 3** - Record measurements and outcomes that feed antenatal appointment attendance exactly as observed, with units and method noted
4. **Step 4** - Store and share records under the confidentiality and retention rules that apply in a maternity unit, birth centre and community clinics, guarding against fetal distress escalated late
5. **Step 5** - Audit a sample of records monthly for completeness and correct anything missing while the work is still current

2. Detail, following the workflow above

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: fetal distress escalated late.
- Never let this happen: postnatal depression unrecognised.
- Never let this happen: newborn feeding problems missed.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a midwife; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a maternity unit, birth centre and community clinics's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
