# Handover Briefing — Port Operations

Hand over shipping lines, hauliers and cargo owners or active work to another professional without losing critical detail. The deliverable is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
This skill is written for a port operations manager working with shipping lines, hauliers and cargo owners in ports and container terminals.

## When to use
- You are a port operations manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in ports and container terminals.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Berth | the place where a vessel moors to load or unload |
| TEU | a standard unit equal to a twenty-foot container |
| Turnaround | the time a vessel or truck spends in port |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Summarise the current position from berth plans: what is done, what is in progress, what is waiting
2. **Step 2** - Highlight live risks first — crane accidents — and the controls or watchpoints already in place
3. **Step 3** - State the next actions with owners and deadlines, and the results expected for berth utilisation
4. **Step 4** - Walk the receiving professional through the brief, answer questions, and confirm understanding
5. **Step 5** - Record that handover happened, who accepted it, and where the full record lives in ports and container terminals

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in ports and container terminals] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [shipping lines, hauliers and cargo owners and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against berth utilisation.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: crane accidents.
- Never let this happen: cargo damage.
- Never let this happen: berth congestion.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a port operations manager; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to ports and container terminals's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
