# Follow-Up & Maintenance Schedule — Financial Planning

Plan follow-up and maintenance so gains for individuals and families planning their financial future are checked and sustained. The deliverable is a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers.
This skill is written for a financial planner working with individuals and families planning their financial future in a financial planning firm and client meetings.

## When to use
- You are a financial planner preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a financial planning firm and client meetings.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Fact find | the detailed gathering of a client's circumstances and goals |
| Suitability | how well a recommendation matches a client's needs and risk tolerance |
| Cashflow modelling | projecting income, spending and assets over a lifetime |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Set follow-up intervals from the plan and from how review completion rates is expected to move over time
2. **Step 2** - Define what is checked at each contact, using cashflow modelling software where measurement is needed, and record it in fact finds
3. **Step 3** - Give individuals and families planning their financial future clear guidance for the interval: what to do, what to watch for, when to make contact sooner
4. **Step 4** - Act early on warning signs — deterioration linked to unsuitable recommendations shortens the interval, never lengthens it
5. **Step 5** - Close the follow-up cycle formally when goals are met, with a summary and re-entry route if needs return

2. Detail, following the workflow above

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: unsuitable recommendations.
- Never let this happen: risk profiles mismatched.
- Never let this happen: reviews overdue.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a financial planner; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a financial planning firm and client meetings's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
