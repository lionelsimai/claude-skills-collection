# Client Explanation & Communication Guide — English Language Teaching

Explain complex English language teacher matters to learners of English of all ages and levels in plain, honest language. The deliverable is a communication pack: explanation script, FAQs and a written summary.
This skill is written for a English language teacher working with learners of English of all ages and levels in a language school, classroom or online.

## When to use
- You are a English language teacher preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a language school, classroom or online.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a communication pack: explanation script, FAQs and a written summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| L1 | a learner's first language |
| CEFR | the Common European Framework levels for language ability |
| Scaffolding | support that helps learners achieve beyond their current level |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Identify what learners of English of all ages and levels actually need to decide or understand, and the misconceptions to clear up first
2. **Step 2** - Translate the technical content — including terms behind lesson plans — into plain language without losing accuracy
3. **Step 3** - Structure the explanation: what we found, what it means, the options, the recommendation and the risks such as learners placed at wrong level
4. **Step 4** - Check understanding by asking learners of English of all ages and levels to play it back in their own words; correct gaps kindly
5. **Step 5** - Follow up with a written summary the same day and record the conversation in the file

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a language school, classroom or online] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [learners of English of all ages and levels and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a communication pack: explanation script, FAQs and a written summary, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in lesson plans.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against level progression rates.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in a language school, classroom or online.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: learners placed at wrong level.
- Never let this happen: speaking practice neglected.
- Never let this happen: assessment bias.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a English language teacher; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a language school, classroom or online's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
