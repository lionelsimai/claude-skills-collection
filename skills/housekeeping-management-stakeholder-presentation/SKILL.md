# Stakeholder Presentation — Housekeeping Management

Present housekeeping manager findings and proposals to decision-makers and stakeholders. The deliverable is a presentation structure with evidence summary and decision asks.
This skill is written for a housekeeping manager working with hotel guests and housekeeping teams in hotels and serviced apartments.

## When to use
- You are a housekeeping manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in hotels and serviced apartments.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a presentation structure with evidence summary and decision asks.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

| Term | Meaning |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Open with the decision or conclusion, then the evidence — busy stakeholders should not wait for the point
2. **Step 2** - Support claims with data from room inspection checklists and movement in inspection pass rates, with sources and limits stated
3. **Step 3** - Address the hard parts directly, including chemical injuries and what it would take to reduce it
4. **Step 4** - Make the ask specific: what decision, by when, with what resources for work in hotels and serviced apartments
5. **Step 5** - Follow up in writing the same day: decisions made, actions agreed, owners and dates

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in hotels and serviced apartments] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [hotel guests and housekeeping teams and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a presentation structure with evidence summary and decision asks, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: chemical injuries.
- Never let this happen: guest property losses.
- Never let this happen: missed safety hazards in rooms.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a housekeeping manager; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to hotels and serviced apartments's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
