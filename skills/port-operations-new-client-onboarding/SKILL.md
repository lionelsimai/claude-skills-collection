# New Client Onboarding Pack — Port Operations

Onboard shipping lines, hauliers and cargo owners smoothly into a port operations manager's service in ports and container terminals. The deliverable is an onboarding pack: welcome information, expectations, first steps and contacts.
This skill is written for a port operations manager working with shipping lines, hauliers and cargo owners in ports and container terminals.

## When to use
- You are a port operations manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in ports and container terminals.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an onboarding pack: welcome information, expectations, first steps and contacts.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Berth | the place where a vessel moors to load or unload |
| TEU | a standard unit equal to a twenty-foot container |
| Turnaround | the time a vessel or truck spends in port |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Send a welcome pack before the first appointment: what the service does, what happens first, what to bring from berth plans
2. **Step 2** - Set expectations honestly — timescales, costs where relevant, and how progress on berth utilisation will be shared
3. **Step 3** - Cover rights and responsibilities on both sides, including confidentiality limits and how to raise concerns
4. **Step 4** - Make the first contact easy: named contact, directions to ports and container terminals, and what to do if plans change
5. **Step 5** - Check in after the first session or milestone and fix friction early, before it becomes disengagement or crane accidents

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: crane accidents.
- Never let this happen: cargo damage.
- Never let this happen: berth congestion.
