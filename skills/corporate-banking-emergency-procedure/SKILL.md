# Emergency Response Procedure — Corporate Banking

Respond to emergencies in bank offices and client sites calmly, in role and in the right order. The deliverable is an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
This skill is written for a corporate relationship manager working with companies and institutions in bank offices and client sites.

## When to use
- You are a corporate relationship manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in bank offices and client sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Facility | an agreed lending arrangement such as a loan or overdraft |
| Covenant | a condition a borrower must maintain |
| Credit paper | the document presenting a lending case for approval |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Recognise the emergency early: know the presentations that matter for companies and institutions, especially credit losses from weak analysis
2. **Step 2** - Take the immediate actions a corporate relationship manager is trained and authorised for, using lending systems as drilled
3. **Step 3** - Call for help with a structured message: location in bank offices and client sites, situation, what has been done, what is needed
4. **Step 4** - Keep companies and institutions and bystanders informed and safe while help arrives; hand over with a concise summary
5. **Step 5** - Record the event fully in credit papers afterwards, debrief the team, and update the procedure with lessons learned

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: credit losses from weak analysis.
- Never let this happen: covenant breaches spotted late.
- Never let this happen: concentration risks.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a corporate relationship manager; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to bank offices and client sites's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
