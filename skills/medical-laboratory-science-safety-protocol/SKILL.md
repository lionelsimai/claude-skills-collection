# Safety Protocol — Medical Laboratory Science

Work safely in a hospital pathology laboratory: a practical safety protocol for a biomedical scientist and patients whose diagnosis depends on laboratory results, via clinical teams. The deliverable is a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions.
This skill is written for a biomedical scientist working with patients whose diagnosis depends on laboratory results, via clinical teams in a hospital pathology laboratory.

## When to use
- You are a biomedical scientist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a hospital pathology laboratory.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

| Term | Meaning |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Identify the hazards present in a hospital pathology laboratory for staff, patients whose diagnosis depends on laboratory results, via clinical teams and visitors, starting with sample mislabelling
2. **Step 2** - Write controls in order: eliminate, substitute, engineer, administrate, then protective equipment such as laboratory analysers
3. **Step 3** - Set pre-start checks and stop-work authority — anyone can halt work that looks unsafe, without penalty
4. **Step 4** - Define what happens when something goes wrong: first response, escalation, recording in test requests
5. **Step 5** - Review the protocol after every incident and at least annually; brief and re-brief everyone it covers

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: sample mislabelling.
- Never let this happen: delayed critical results.
- Never let this happen: analyser faults unnoticed.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a biomedical scientist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a hospital pathology laboratory's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
