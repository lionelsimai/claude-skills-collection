# Intake & Initial Assessment — Environmental Law

Run a structured intake and initial assessment for developers, businesses and community groups in a law firm and planning inquiries. The deliverable is a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
This skill is written for a environmental solicitor working with developers, businesses and community groups in a law firm and planning inquiries.

## When to use
- You are a environmental solicitor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a law firm and planning inquiries.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Environmental permit | official permission for activities affecting the environment |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm identity, consent and the reason for referral or engagement, and open the record that will hold permit applications
2. **Step 2** - Gather history and current circumstances from developers, businesses and community groups, using the standard intake structure for a law firm and planning inquiries
3. **Step 3** - Record baseline observations and measurements relevant to permit success rates, noting anything that changes the priority of the case
4. **Step 4** - Flag immediate concerns — especially permit breaches unadvised — and escalate or fast-track anything urgent before proceeding
5. **Step 5** - Summarise findings for developers, businesses and community groups in plain language, agree the next step, and file the intake section of permit applications

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: permit breaches unadvised.
- Never let this happen: consultation deadlines missed.
- Never let this happen: contaminated land liabilities.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a environmental solicitor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a law firm and planning inquiries's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
