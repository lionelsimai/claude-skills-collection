# Metrics & Outcomes Review — Environmental Engineering

Track, interpret and act on the outcome metrics that matter for a environmental engineer. The deliverable is a metrics dashboard definition and an interpretation/action review.
This skill is written for a environmental engineer working with water companies, industries and regulators in treatment plants, client sites and design offices.

## When to use
- You are a environmental engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in treatment plants, client sites and design offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a metrics dashboard definition and an interpretation/action review.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Consent | permission to discharge treated water or waste |
| Effluent | liquid waste discharged from a process |
| Remediation | cleaning up contaminated land or water |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Define each metric precisely — start with permit compliance — including source data in impact assessments and collection method
2. **Step 2** - Set review cadence and comparison baselines so trends, not single data points, drive decisions
3. **Step 3** - Investigate movements before celebrating or panicking: check data quality, case mix and one-off causes
4. **Step 4** - Link metric movements to practice changes and to risks such as pollution incidents; test one change at a time where possible
5. **Step 5** - Report results honestly to stakeholders in treatment plants, client sites and design offices, including where outcomes fell short and what changes

**Context:** [Situation in treatment plants, client sites and design offices] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [water companies, industries and regulators and other stakeholders]

1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above

4. Review point and escalation route

2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against permit compliance.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in treatment plants, client sites and design offices.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: pollution incidents.
- Never let this happen: consent breaches.
- Never let this happen: design underperformance.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a environmental engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to treatment plants, client sites and design offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
