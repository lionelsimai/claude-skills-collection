# Mentoring & Training Junior Staff — Orthopaedics

Train and mentor junior colleagues toward competent, safe orthopaedic surgeon practice. The deliverable is a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
This skill is written for a orthopaedic surgeon working with patients with bone, joint and muscle injuries or conditions in an orthopaedic theatre and fracture clinic.

## When to use
- You are a orthopaedic surgeon preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in an orthopaedic theatre and fracture clinic.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Fracture | a break in a bone |
| Arthroplasty | surgical replacement of a joint |
| Compartment syndrome | dangerous pressure build-up within a muscle compartment |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Assess the trainee's current level honestly against the competencies required in an orthopaedic theatre and fracture clinic
2. **Step 2** - Sequence learning from observation to supervised practice to independent work, using operating microscopes and drills under supervision first
3. **Step 3** - Set explicit sign-off criteria for high-risk tasks — especially anything touching wrong-site surgery — and never sign off early
4. **Step 4** - Give feedback from direct observation and from operation notes, specific and timely, with strengths named too
5. **Step 5** - Record progress, keep the training file current, and escalate persistent gaps through supervision, not gossip

## Output Format

1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: wrong-site surgery.
- Never let this happen: compartment syndrome missed.
- Never let this happen: post-operative infection.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a orthopaedic surgeon; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to an orthopaedic theatre and fracture clinic's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
