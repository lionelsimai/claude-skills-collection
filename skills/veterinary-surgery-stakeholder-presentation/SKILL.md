# Stakeholder Presentation — Veterinary Surgery

Present veterinary surgeon findings and proposals to decision-makers and stakeholders. The deliverable is a presentation structure with evidence summary and decision asks.
This skill is written for a veterinary surgeon working with animals and their owners in a veterinary practice and animal hospital.

## When to use
- You are a veterinary surgeon preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a veterinary practice and animal hospital.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a presentation structure with evidence summary and decision asks.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Zoonosis | a disease transmissible between animals and humans |
| Neutering | surgical removal of reproductive organs |
| Prophylaxis | treatment given to prevent disease |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Open with the decision or conclusion, then the evidence — busy stakeholders should not wait for the point
2. **Step 2** - Support claims with data from clinical records and movement in appointment waiting times, with sources and limits stated
3. **Step 3** - Address the hard parts directly, including anaesthetic incidents and what it would take to reduce it
4. **Step 4** - Make the ask specific: what decision, by when, with what resources for work in a veterinary practice and animal hospital
5. **Step 5** - Follow up in writing the same day: decisions made, actions agreed, owners and dates

| Core tools & equipment | anaesthetic machines; surgical instruments; X-ray and ultrasound; kennels and recovery wards |

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a veterinary practice and animal hospital] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [animals and their owners and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a presentation structure with evidence summary and decision asks, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in clinical records.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against appointment waiting times.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in a veterinary practice and animal hospital.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: anaesthetic incidents.
- Never let this happen: zoonotic infection risk.
- Never let this happen: missed diagnoses from limited history.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a veterinary surgeon; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a veterinary practice and animal hospital's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
