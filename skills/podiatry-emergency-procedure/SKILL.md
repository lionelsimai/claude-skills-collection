# Emergency Response Procedure — Podiatry

Respond to emergencies in a podiatry clinic and community settings calmly, in role and in the right order. The deliverable is an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
This skill is written for a podiatrist working with patients with foot and lower limb conditions in a podiatry clinic and community settings.

## When to use
- You are a podiatrist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a podiatry clinic and community settings.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Ulcer | an open sore that is slow to heal |
| Biomechanics | how the body moves and bears load |
| Neuropathy | nerve damage reducing sensation, common in diabetes |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Recognise the emergency early: know the presentations that matter for patients with foot and lower limb conditions, especially diabetic foot ulcers missed
2. **Step 2** - Take the immediate actions a podiatrist is trained and authorised for, using surgical instruments as drilled
3. **Step 3** - Call for help with a structured message: location in a podiatry clinic and community settings, situation, what has been done, what is needed
4. **Step 4** - Keep patients with foot and lower limb conditions and bystanders informed and safe while help arrives; hand over with a concise summary
5. **Step 5** - Record the event fully in foot assessments afterwards, debrief the team, and update the procedure with lessons learned

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: diabetic foot ulcers missed.
- Never let this happen: infection after nail surgery.
- Never let this happen: vascular problems unrecognised.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a podiatrist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a podiatry clinic and community settings's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
