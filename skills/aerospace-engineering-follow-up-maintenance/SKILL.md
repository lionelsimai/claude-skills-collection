# Follow-Up & Maintenance Schedule — Aerospace Engineering

Plan follow-up and maintenance so gains for aircraft manufacturers, airlines and space agencies are checked and sustained. The deliverable is a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers.
This skill is written for a aerospace engineer working with aircraft manufacturers, airlines and space agencies in design offices, test facilities and hangars.

## When to use
- You are a aerospace engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in design offices, test facilities and hangars.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Certification | official approval that a design meets safety standards |
| Fatigue life | how long a component withstands repeated loading |
| Thrust | the force produced by an engine |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Set follow-up intervals from the plan and from how test programme milestones is expected to move over time
2. **Step 2** - Define what is checked at each contact, using simulation software where measurement is needed, and record it in design analyses
3. **Step 3** - Give aircraft manufacturers, airlines and space agencies clear guidance for the interval: what to do, what to watch for, when to make contact sooner
4. **Step 4** - Act early on warning signs — deterioration linked to test incidents shortens the interval, never lengthens it
5. **Step 5** - Close the follow-up cycle formally when goals are met, with a summary and re-entry route if needs return

2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: test incidents.
- Never let this happen: certification delays.
- Never let this happen: fatigue failures.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a aerospace engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to design offices, test facilities and hangars's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
