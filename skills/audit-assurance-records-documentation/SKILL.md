# Records & Documentation — Audit & Assurance

Create and maintain accurate, auditable records for work with companies, charities and public bodies requiring an audit. The deliverable is a documentation standard, templates and a completed record set.
This skill is written for a external auditor working with companies, charities and public bodies requiring an audit in an accountancy firm and client sites.

## When to use
- You are a external auditor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in an accountancy firm and client sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a documentation standard, templates and a completed record set.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Materiality | the size at which errors could influence decisions |
| Working papers | the evidence and analysis behind an audit |
| Independence | freedom from interests that could bias the auditor |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Use audit plans as the single source of truth; record facts, observations and decisions — not speculation
2. **Step 2** - Write entries contemporaneously where possible, dated and attributable, in the format an accountancy firm and client sites requires
3. **Step 3** - Record measurements and outcomes that feed audit completion times exactly as observed, with units and method noted
4. **Step 4** - Store and share records under the confidentiality and retention rules that apply in an accountancy firm and client sites, guarding against material misstatements missed
5. **Step 5** - Audit a sample of records monthly for completeness and correct anything missing while the work is still current

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: material misstatements missed.
- Never let this happen: independence threats.
- Never let this happen: deadline overruns.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a external auditor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to an accountancy firm and client sites's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
