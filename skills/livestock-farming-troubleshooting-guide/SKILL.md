# Troubleshooting & Problem Solving — Livestock Farming

Diagnose and resolve common problems a livestock farmer meets in farms and livestock units. The deliverable is a troubleshooting guide: symptoms, likely causes, checks and fixes.
This skill is written for a livestock farmer working with meat, milk and egg buyers and the food chain in farms and livestock units.

## When to use
- You are a livestock farmer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in farms and livestock units.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a troubleshooting guide: symptoms, likely causes, checks and fixes.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Describe the problem precisely from the record in animal health records — when it started, what changed, who it affects
2. **Step 2** - Work from most likely and most dangerous causes first; rule out anything connected to disease outbreaks early
3. **Step 3** - Run checks in a logical order using handling systems, changing one variable at a time so the cause can be isolated
4. **Step 4** - Apply the fix, then verify against animal health scores that the problem is actually resolved, not just quieter
5. **Step 5** - Document the diagnosis and fix so the next occurrence is faster, and flag recurring problems for root-cause work

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: disease outbreaks.
- Never let this happen: animal welfare failures.
- Never let this happen: medicine record errors.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a livestock farmer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to farms and livestock units's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
