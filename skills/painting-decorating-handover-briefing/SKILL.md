# Handover Briefing — Painting & Decorating

Hand over homeowners, landlords and commercial clients or active work to another professional without losing critical detail. The deliverable is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
This skill is written for a painter and decorator working with homeowners, landlords and commercial clients in homes, offices and building sites.

## When to use
- You are a painter and decorator preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in homes, offices and building sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

| Term | Meaning |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Summarise the current position from quotes: what is done, what is in progress, what is waiting
2. **Step 2** - Highlight live risks first — falls from height — and the controls or watchpoints already in place
3. **Step 3** - State the next actions with owners and deadlines, and the results expected for job completion times
4. **Step 4** - Walk the receiving professional through the brief, answer questions, and confirm understanding
5. **Step 5** - Record that handover happened, who accepted it, and where the full record lives in homes, offices and building sites

- Never let this happen: falls from height.
- Never let this happen: poor preparation causing early failure.
- Never let this happen: lead paint hazards.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a painter and decorator; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to homes, offices and building sites's rules.
