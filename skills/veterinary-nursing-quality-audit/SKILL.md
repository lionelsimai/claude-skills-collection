# Quality Audit — Veterinary Nursing

Audit the quality of veterinary nurse work in a veterinary practice and drive measurable improvement. The deliverable is an audit report with scores, evidence and an improvement plan.
This skill is written for a veterinary nurse working with animals in care and their owners in a veterinary practice.

## When to use
- You are a veterinary nurse preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a veterinary practice.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an audit report with scores, evidence and an improvement plan.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Hospitalisation chart | a record of an inpatient animal's care and observations |
| Analgesia | pain relief |
| Triage | prioritising patients by urgency in practice |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Define the audit sample and criteria up front, anchored to patient monitoring compliance and the standards for a veterinary practice
2. **Step 2** - Review a sample of nursing care plans and observe practice where permitted, scoring consistently
3. **Step 3** - Separate isolated errors from patterns — patterns point to system fixes, not individual blame
4. **Step 4** - Report findings with evidence, and agree improvement actions that address the root causes, including unrecognised pain in animals
5. **Step 5** - Re-audit after the agreed interval and publish the change in scores so improvement is visible

## Output Format

The finished output is an audit report with scores, evidence and an improvement plan, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against patient monitoring compliance.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in a veterinary practice.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: unrecognised pain in animals.
- Never let this happen: fluid therapy errors.
- Never let this happen: escape or injury in kennels.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a veterinary nurse; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a veterinary practice's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
