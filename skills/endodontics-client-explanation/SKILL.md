# Client Explanation & Communication Guide — Endodontics

Explain complex endodontist matters to patients with dental pulp and root canal conditions in plain, honest language. The deliverable is a communication pack: explanation script, FAQs and a written summary.
This skill is written for a endodontist working with patients with dental pulp and root canal conditions in a dental practice and referral clinic.

## When to use
- You are a endodontist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a dental practice and referral clinic.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a communication pack: explanation script, FAQs and a written summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Pulp | the soft tissue inside a tooth containing nerves and blood vessels |
| Root canal | the space inside a tooth root cleaned and filled during treatment |
| Periapical | relating to the tissue around the tip of a tooth root |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Identify what patients with dental pulp and root canal conditions actually need to decide or understand, and the misconceptions to clear up first
2. **Step 2** - Translate the technical content — including terms behind endodontic assessments — into plain language without losing accuracy
3. **Step 3** - Structure the explanation: what we found, what it means, the options, the recommendation and the risks such as missed root canal anatomy
4. **Step 4** - Check understanding by asking patients with dental pulp and root canal conditions to play it back in their own words; correct gaps kindly
5. **Step 5** - Follow up with a written summary the same day and record the conversation in the file

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed root canal anatomy.
- Never let this happen: instrument separation in canal.
- Never let this happen: persistent infection.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a endodontist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a dental practice and referral clinic's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
