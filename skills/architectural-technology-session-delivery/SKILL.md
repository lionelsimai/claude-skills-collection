# Session Delivery Guide — Architectural Technology

Prepare for and deliver a professional session or appointment for architects, developers and homeowners in design studios and construction sites. The deliverable is a session plan, delivery checklist and post-session record.
This skill is written for a architectural technologist working with architects, developers and homeowners in design studios and construction sites.

## When to use
- You are a architectural technologist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in design studios and construction sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a session plan, delivery checklist and post-session record.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Building regulations | legal standards for design and construction work |
| Detailing | the precise drawings of how building elements join |
| Clash detection | finding conflicts between building systems in a model |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Review technical drawings before the session so you know the history, goals and any cautions such as regulation non-compliance
2. **Step 2** - Prepare the space and CAD and BIM software; check everything works and is safe before architects, developers and homeowners arrive
3. **Step 3** - Open the session by agreeing the focus and what a good outcome looks like, linked to drawing issue timeliness
4. **Step 4** - Deliver the session, adapting in the moment while staying inside the agreed plan and professional scope
5. **Step 5** - Close by summarising what was covered, confirming follow-ups, and completing the session record the same day

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: regulation non-compliance.
- Never let this happen: detailing errors causing site problems.
- Never let this happen: clash detection missed.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a architectural technologist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to design studios and construction sites's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
