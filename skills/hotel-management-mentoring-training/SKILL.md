# Mentoring & Training Junior Staff — Hotel Management

Train and mentor junior colleagues toward competent, safe hotel manager practice. The deliverable is a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
This skill is written for a hotel manager working with guests, hotel teams and owners in hotels and resorts.

## When to use
- You are a hotel manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in hotels and resorts.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Assess the trainee's current level honestly against the competencies required in hotels and resorts
2. **Step 2** - Sequence learning from observation to supervised practice to independent work, using property management systems under supervision first
3. **Step 3** - Set explicit sign-off criteria for high-risk tasks — especially anything touching guest safety incidents — and never sign off early
4. **Step 4** - Give feedback from direct observation and from occupancy reports, specific and timely, with strengths named too
5. **Step 5** - Record progress, keep the training file current, and escalate persistent gaps through supervision, not gossip

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in hotels and resorts] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [guests, hotel teams and owners and other stakeholders]

1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: guest safety incidents.
- Never let this happen: overbooking crises.
- Never let this happen: data breaches on guest records.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a hotel manager; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to hotels and resorts's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
