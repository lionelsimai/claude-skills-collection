# Handover Briefing — Broadcast Journalism

Hand over audiences, contributors and news editors or active work to another professional without losing critical detail. The deliverable is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
This skill is written for a broadcast journalist working with audiences, contributors and news editors in newsrooms and outside broadcasts.

## When to use
- You are a broadcast journalist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in newsrooms and outside broadcasts.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Running order | the planned sequence of items in a programme |
| Compliance | checking content against broadcast rules and law |
| Package | a pre-recorded report within a programme |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Summarise the current position from running orders: what is done, what is in progress, what is waiting
2. **Step 2** - Highlight live risks first — inaccurate reporting broadcast — and the controls or watchpoints already in place
3. **Step 3** - State the next actions with owners and deadlines, and the results expected for audience figures
4. **Step 4** - Walk the receiving professional through the brief, answer questions, and confirm understanding
5. **Step 5** - Record that handover happened, who accepted it, and where the full record lives in newsrooms and outside broadcasts

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in newsrooms and outside broadcasts] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [audiences, contributors and news editors and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in running orders.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against audience figures.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in newsrooms and outside broadcasts.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: inaccurate reporting broadcast.
- Never let this happen: contributor harm.
- Never let this happen: legal risks in reports.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a broadcast journalist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to newsrooms and outside broadcasts's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
