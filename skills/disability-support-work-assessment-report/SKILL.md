# Assessment & Findings Report — Disability Support Work

Assess people with disabilities and their families and write a defensible findings report as a disability support worker. The deliverable is a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
This skill is written for a disability support worker working with people with disabilities and their families in homes, day services and community settings.

## When to use
- You are a disability support worker preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in homes, day services and community settings.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Person-centred | support designed around the individual's wishes |
| Communication passport | a document explaining how a person communicates |
| Positive behaviour support | understanding behaviour to reduce distress and restriction |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Review the intake record and support plans so the assessment builds on verified information, not assumptions
2. **Step 2** - Carry out the assessment using assistive technology, following the recognised method for homes, day services and community settings
3. **Step 3** - Compare findings against the benchmarks behind goal progress and note where evidence is strong, weak or missing
4. **Step 4** - Draft the report: findings first, then interpretation, then recommendations in priority order
5. **Step 5** - Check the draft for accuracy and for personal care done unsafely; have it reviewed where the profession's standards require a second sign-off

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in homes, day services and community settings] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [people with disabilities and their families and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: personal care done unsafely.
- Never let this happen: communication needs ignored.
- Never let this happen: restrictive practices misused.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a disability support worker; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to homes, day services and community settings's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
