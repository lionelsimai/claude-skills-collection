# Session Delivery Guide — Landscape Architecture

Prepare for and deliver a professional session or appointment for developers, councils and private clients in design studios and outdoor sites. The deliverable is a session plan, delivery checklist and post-session record.
This skill is written for a landscape architect working with developers, councils and private clients in design studios and outdoor sites.

## When to use
- You are a landscape architect preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in design studios and outdoor sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a session plan, delivery checklist and post-session record.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |

| Planting plan | the layout and specification of plants for a site |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Review design concepts before the session so you know the history, goals and any cautions such as planting failures
2. **Step 2** - Prepare the space and CAD software; check everything works and is safe before developers, councils and private clients arrive
3. **Step 3** - Open the session by agreeing the focus and what a good outcome looks like, linked to design approval rates
4. **Step 4** - Deliver the session, adapting in the moment while staying inside the agreed plan and professional scope
5. **Step 5** - Close by summarising what was covered, confirming follow-ups, and completing the session record the same day

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: planting failures.
- Never let this happen: drainage problems overlooked.
- Never let this happen: inaccessible designs.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a landscape architect; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to design studios and outdoor sites's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
