# Incident Reporting & Response — Pharmacy Practice

Respond to, record and learn from incidents involving patients and customers collecting medicines and advice in a community pharmacy. The deliverable is a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list.
This skill is written for a pharmacy technician working with patients and customers collecting medicines and advice in a community pharmacy.

## When to use
- You are a pharmacy technician preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a community pharmacy.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Dispensing | preparing and supplying medicines against a prescription |
| Controlled drug | a medicine subject to strict legal controls |
| SOP | standard operating procedure |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Make the situation safe first; attend to people before paperwork, and escalate if dispensing errors is still live
2. **Step 2** - Record facts while they are fresh: what happened, when, who was involved, what was observed — in dispensing records
3. **Step 3** - Notify the people and bodies who must know, within the timeframes that apply in a community pharmacy
4. **Step 4** - Support those affected, including honest communication with patients and customers collecting medicines and advice about what happened and what happens next
5. **Step 5** - Analyse causes without blame, agree preventive actions with owners and dates, and track them to closure

## Profession-Specific Components
| Component | Details for Pharmacy Practice |
|-----------|-------------------------------|
| Core documentation | dispensing records; accuracy check logs; stock records; patient counselling notes |
| Core tools & equipment | dispensing systems; labelling printers; stock control software; counting devices |
| People involved | patients and customers collecting medicines and advice; the pharmacy technician; colleagues and other services in a community pharmacy |
| Primary record | dispensing records — keep it current, dated and attributable |

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a community pharmacy] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients and customers collecting medicines and advice and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in dispensing records.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against dispensing accuracy rate.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in a community pharmacy.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: dispensing errors.
- Never let this happen: controlled drug discrepancies.
- Never let this happen: expired stock supplied.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a pharmacy technician; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a community pharmacy's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
