# Records & Documentation — Gastroenterology

Create and maintain accurate, auditable records for work with patients with digestive system conditions. The deliverable is a documentation standard, templates and a completed record set.
This skill is written for a gastroenterologist working with patients with digestive system conditions in an endoscopy unit and gastroenterology clinic.

## When to use
- You are a gastroenterologist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in an endoscopy unit and gastroenterology clinic.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a documentation standard, templates and a completed record set.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Endoscopy | examining the digestive tract with a flexible camera |
| Cirrhosis | advanced scarring of the liver |
| IBD | inflammatory bowel disease, mainly Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Use endoscopy reports as the single source of truth; record facts, observations and decisions — not speculation
2. **Step 2** - Write entries contemporaneously where possible, dated and attributable, in the format an endoscopy unit and gastroenterology clinic requires
3. **Step 3** - Record measurements and outcomes that feed endoscopy waiting times exactly as observed, with units and method noted
4. **Step 4** - Store and share records under the confidentiality and retention rules that apply in an endoscopy unit and gastroenterology clinic, guarding against missed gastrointestinal bleeding
5. **Step 5** - Audit a sample of records monthly for completeness and correct anything missing while the work is still current

2. Detail, following the workflow above

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed gastrointestinal bleeding.
- Never let this happen: sedation complications.
- Never let this happen: delayed cancer pathway.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a gastroenterologist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to an endoscopy unit and gastroenterology clinic's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
