# Quality Audit — Masonry

Audit the quality of mason and bricklayer work in construction sites and historic buildings and drive measurable improvement. The deliverable is an audit report with scores, evidence and an improvement plan.
This skill is written for a mason and bricklayer working with builders, homeowners and heritage organisations in construction sites and historic buildings.

## When to use
- You are a mason and bricklayer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in construction sites and historic buildings.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an audit report with scores, evidence and an improvement plan.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Define the audit sample and criteria up front, anchored to work completion rates and the standards for construction sites and historic buildings
2. **Step 2** - Review a sample of laying records and observe practice where permitted, scoring consistently
3. **Step 3** - Separate isolated errors from patterns — patterns point to system fixes, not individual blame
4. **Step 4** - Report findings with evidence, and agree improvement actions that address the root causes, including structural defects from poor work
5. **Step 5** - Re-audit after the agreed interval and publish the change in scores so improvement is visible

- Never let this happen: structural defects from poor work.
- Never let this happen: falls and manual handling injuries.
- Never let this happen: heritage damage.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a mason and bricklayer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to construction sites and historic buildings's rules.
