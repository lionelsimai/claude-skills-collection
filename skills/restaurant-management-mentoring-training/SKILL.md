# Mentoring & Training Junior Staff — Restaurant Management

Train and mentor junior colleagues toward competent, safe restaurant manager practice. The deliverable is a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
This skill is written for a restaurant manager working with diners, kitchen and front-of-house teams in restaurants and cafes.

## When to use
- You are a restaurant manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in restaurants and cafes.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Assess the trainee's current level honestly against the competencies required in restaurants and cafes
2. **Step 2** - Sequence learning from observation to supervised practice to independent work, using point of sale systems under supervision first
3. **Step 3** - Set explicit sign-off criteria for high-risk tasks — especially anything touching food hygiene failures — and never sign off early
4. **Step 4** - Give feedback from direct observation and from menus and costing sheets, specific and timely, with strengths named too
5. **Step 5** - Record progress, keep the training file current, and escalate persistent gaps through supervision, not gossip

1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: food hygiene failures.
- Never let this happen: allergen incidents.
- Never let this happen: cash handling discrepancies.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a restaurant manager; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to restaurants and cafes's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
