# Records & Documentation — Podiatry

Create and maintain accurate, auditable records for work with patients with foot and lower limb conditions. The deliverable is a documentation standard, templates and a completed record set.
This skill is written for a podiatrist working with patients with foot and lower limb conditions in a podiatry clinic and community settings.

## When to use
- You are a podiatrist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a podiatry clinic and community settings.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a documentation standard, templates and a completed record set.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Ulcer | an open sore that is slow to heal |
| Biomechanics | how the body moves and bears load |
| Neuropathy | nerve damage reducing sensation, common in diabetes |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Use foot assessments as the single source of truth; record facts, observations and decisions — not speculation
2. **Step 2** - Write entries contemporaneously where possible, dated and attributable, in the format a podiatry clinic and community settings requires
3. **Step 3** - Record measurements and outcomes that feed ulcer healing rates exactly as observed, with units and method noted
4. **Step 4** - Store and share records under the confidentiality and retention rules that apply in a podiatry clinic and community settings, guarding against diabetic foot ulcers missed
5. **Step 5** - Audit a sample of records monthly for completeness and correct anything missing while the work is still current

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: diabetic foot ulcers missed.
- Never let this happen: infection after nail surgery.
- Never let this happen: vascular problems unrecognised.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a podiatrist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a podiatry clinic and community settings's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
