# New Client Onboarding Pack — Mechanical Engineering

Onboard manufacturing and product clients smoothly into a mechanical engineer's service in design offices, factories and test facilities. The deliverable is an onboarding pack: welcome information, expectations, first steps and contacts.
This skill is written for a mechanical engineer working with manufacturing and product clients in design offices, factories and test facilities.

## When to use
- You are a mechanical engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in design offices, factories and test facilities.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an onboarding pack: welcome information, expectations, first steps and contacts.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Tolerance | the permitted variation in a dimension |
| Fatigue | weakening of material from repeated loading |
| Prototype | an early version built to test a design |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Send a welcome pack before the first appointment: what the service does, what happens first, what to bring from design files
2. **Step 2** - Set expectations honestly — timescales, costs where relevant, and how progress on prototype test pass rates will be shared
3. **Step 3** - Cover rights and responsibilities on both sides, including confidentiality limits and how to raise concerns
4. **Step 4** - Make the first contact easy: named contact, directions to design offices, factories and test facilities, and what to do if plans change
5. **Step 5** - Check in after the first session or milestone and fix friction early, before it becomes disengagement or component failures

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in design offices, factories and test facilities] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [manufacturing and product clients and other stakeholders]

The finished output is an onboarding pack: welcome information, expectations, first steps and contacts, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in design files.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against prototype test pass rates.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in design offices, factories and test facilities.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: component failures.
- Never let this happen: test safety incidents.
- Never let this happen: design tolerance errors.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a mechanical engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to design offices, factories and test facilities's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
