# Follow-Up & Maintenance Schedule — Restaurant Management

Plan follow-up and maintenance so gains for diners, kitchen and front-of-house teams are checked and sustained. The deliverable is a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers.
This skill is written for a restaurant manager working with diners, kitchen and front-of-house teams in restaurants and cafes.

## When to use
- You are a restaurant manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in restaurants and cafes.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |

| Food cost | the cost of ingredients as a share of menu price |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Set follow-up intervals from the plan and from how food cost percentage is expected to move over time
2. **Step 2** - Define what is checked at each contact, using point of sale systems where measurement is needed, and record it in menus and costing sheets
3. **Step 3** - Give diners, kitchen and front-of-house teams clear guidance for the interval: what to do, what to watch for, when to make contact sooner
4. **Step 4** - Act early on warning signs — deterioration linked to food hygiene failures shortens the interval, never lengthens it
5. **Step 5** - Close the follow-up cycle formally when goals are met, with a summary and re-entry route if needs return

- Never let this happen: allergen incidents.
- Never let this happen: cash handling discrepancies.
