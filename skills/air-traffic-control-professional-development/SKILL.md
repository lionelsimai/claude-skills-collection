# Continuing Professional Development Plan — Air Traffic Control

Plan and evidence continuing professional development for a practising air traffic controller. The deliverable is a CPD plan mapped to competency gaps, with evidence and reflection records.
This skill is written for a air traffic controller working with pilots, airlines and airport operators in control towers and radar centres.

## When to use
- You are a air traffic controller preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in control towers and radar centres.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a CPD plan mapped to competency gaps, with evidence and reflection records.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Separation | the safe distance maintained between aircraft |
| Handover | transferring responsibility for traffic between controllers |
| Squawk | the transponder code identifying an aircraft on radar |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Audit current competence against the standards for control towers and radar centres and the evidence in your recent traffic logs
2. **Step 2** - Choose development priorities that close real gaps — including staying current on loss of separation — not just easy hours
3. **Step 3** - Mix formats: courses, supervised practice, case review, reading and teaching others
4. **Step 4** - After each activity, write a short reflection: what changed in your practice and what it means for traffic throughput
5. **Step 5** - Keep the CPD record inspection-ready and review the plan at appraisal or revalidation time

| Component | Details for Air Traffic Control |
|-----------|-------------------------------|
| Core documentation | traffic logs; incident reports; handover records; weather briefings |
| Core tools & equipment | radar systems; radio equipment; flight data systems; weather displays |
| People involved | pilots, airlines and airport operators; the air traffic controller; colleagues and other services in control towers and radar centres |
| Primary record | traffic logs — keep it current, dated and attributable |

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in control towers and radar centres] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [pilots, airlines and airport operators and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a CPD plan mapped to competency gaps, with evidence and reflection records, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in traffic logs.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against traffic throughput.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in control towers and radar centres.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: loss of separation.
- Never let this happen: communication misunderstandings.
- Never let this happen: fatigue on shift.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a air traffic controller; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to control towers and radar centres's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
