# Referral & Coordination — Port Operations

Refer shipping lines, hauliers and cargo owners onward and coordinate with other professionals and services. The deliverable is a referral pack and a coordination log with responses tracked.
This skill is written for a port operations manager working with shipping lines, hauliers and cargo owners in ports and container terminals.

## When to use
- You are a port operations manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in ports and container terminals.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a referral pack and a coordination log with responses tracked.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Berth | the place where a vessel moors to load or unload |
| TEU | a standard unit equal to a twenty-foot container |
| Turnaround | the time a vessel or truck spends in port |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm the referral is appropriate: what shipping lines, hauliers and cargo owners need that sits outside the port operations manager scope in ports and container terminals
2. **Step 2** - Prepare the referral from berth plans — relevant history, current status, the specific question being asked
3. **Step 3** - With consent, send the referral and log who received it and when; chase non-responses on a set schedule
4. **Step 4** - Share incoming findings back into the main record so the plan and berth utilisation stay joined up
5. **Step 5** - Close the loop with shipping lines, hauliers and cargo owners: explain what happens next, who owns it, and what to do if things worsen — mindful of crane accidents

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: crane accidents.
- Never let this happen: cargo damage.
- Never let this happen: berth congestion.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a port operations manager; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to ports and container terminals's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
