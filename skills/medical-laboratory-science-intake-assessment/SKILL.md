# Intake & Initial Assessment — Medical Laboratory Science

Run a structured intake and initial assessment for patients whose diagnosis depends on laboratory results, via clinical teams in a hospital pathology laboratory. The deliverable is a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
This skill is written for a biomedical scientist working with patients whose diagnosis depends on laboratory results, via clinical teams in a hospital pathology laboratory.

## When to use
- You are a biomedical scientist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a hospital pathology laboratory.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm identity, consent and the reason for referral or engagement, and open the record that will hold test requests
2. **Step 2** - Gather history and current circumstances from patients whose diagnosis depends on laboratory results, via clinical teams, using the standard intake structure for a hospital pathology laboratory
3. **Step 3** - Record baseline observations and measurements relevant to result turnaround time, noting anything that changes the priority of the case
4. **Step 4** - Flag immediate concerns — especially sample mislabelling — and escalate or fast-track anything urgent before proceeding
5. **Step 5** - Summarise findings for patients whose diagnosis depends on laboratory results, via clinical teams in plain language, agree the next step, and file the intake section of test requests

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: sample mislabelling.
- Never let this happen: delayed critical results.
- Never let this happen: analyser faults unnoticed.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a biomedical scientist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a hospital pathology laboratory's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
