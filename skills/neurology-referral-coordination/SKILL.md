# Referral & Coordination — Neurology

Refer patients with brain, spinal cord and nerve disorders onward and coordinate with other professionals and services. The deliverable is a referral pack and a coordination log with responses tracked.
This skill is written for a neurologist working with patients with brain, spinal cord and nerve disorders in a neurology department and outpatient clinic.

## When to use
- You are a neurologist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a neurology department and outpatient clinic.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a referral pack and a coordination log with responses tracked.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| EEG | a recording of electrical activity in the brain |
| Lesion | an area of damaged tissue |
| Aura | warning symptoms some patients get before a seizure or migraine |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm the referral is appropriate: what patients with brain, spinal cord and nerve disorders need that sits outside the neurologist scope in a neurology department and outpatient clinic
2. **Step 2** - Prepare the referral from neurological examination records — relevant history, current status, the specific question being asked
3. **Step 3** - With consent, send the referral and log who received it and when; chase non-responses on a set schedule
4. **Step 4** - Share incoming findings back into the main record so the plan and stroke assessment timeliness stay joined up
5. **Step 5** - Close the loop with patients with brain, spinal cord and nerve disorders: explain what happens next, who owns it, and what to do if things worsen — mindful of missed stroke presentations

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed stroke presentations.
- Never let this happen: status epilepticus delays.
- Never let this happen: medication interactions.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a neurologist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a neurology department and outpatient clinic's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
