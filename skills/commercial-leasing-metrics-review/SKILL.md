# Metrics & Outcomes Review — Commercial Leasing

Track, interpret and act on the outcome metrics that matter for a commercial leasing agent. The deliverable is a metrics dashboard definition and an interpretation/action review.
This skill is written for a commercial leasing agent working with landlords, tenants and investors in commercial properties and agency offices.

## When to use
- You are a commercial leasing agent preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in commercial properties and agency offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a metrics dashboard definition and an interpretation/action review.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Heads of terms | the agreed key points before a lease is drafted |
| Break clause | a right to end a lease early on set terms |
| Alienation | transferring or sharing a lease interest |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Define each metric precisely — start with time to let — including source data in heads of terms and collection method
2. **Step 2** - Set review cadence and comparison baselines so trends, not single data points, drive decisions
3. **Step 3** - Investigate movements before celebrating or panicking: check data quality, case mix and one-off causes
4. **Step 4** - Link metric movements to practice changes and to risks such as misdescribed premises; test one change at a time where possible
5. **Step 5** - Report results honestly to stakeholders in commercial properties and agency offices, including where outcomes fell short and what changes

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: misdescribed premises.
- Never let this happen: missed break clauses.
- Never let this happen: unagreed incentives.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a commercial leasing agent; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to commercial properties and agency offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
