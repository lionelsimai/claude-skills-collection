# Assessment & Findings Report — Pharmacy Practice

Assess patients and customers collecting medicines and advice and write a defensible findings report as a pharmacy technician. The deliverable is a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
This skill is written for a pharmacy technician working with patients and customers collecting medicines and advice in a community pharmacy.

## When to use
- You are a pharmacy technician preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a community pharmacy.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Dispensing | preparing and supplying medicines against a prescription |
| Controlled drug | a medicine subject to strict legal controls |
| SOP | standard operating procedure |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Review the intake record and dispensing records so the assessment builds on verified information, not assumptions
2. **Step 2** - Carry out the assessment using dispensing systems, following the recognised method for a community pharmacy
3. **Step 3** - Compare findings against the benchmarks behind dispensing accuracy rate and note where evidence is strong, weak or missing
4. **Step 4** - Draft the report: findings first, then interpretation, then recommendations in priority order
5. **Step 5** - Check the draft for accuracy and for dispensing errors; have it reviewed where the profession's standards require a second sign-off

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a community pharmacy] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients and customers collecting medicines and advice and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: dispensing errors.
- Never let this happen: controlled drug discrepancies.
- Never let this happen: expired stock supplied.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a pharmacy technician; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a community pharmacy's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
