# Assessment & Findings Report — Mortgage Advisory

Assess home buyers and homeowners and write a defensible findings report as a mortgage adviser. The deliverable is a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
This skill is written for a mortgage adviser working with home buyers and homeowners in advice firms, branches and online.

## When to use
- You are a mortgage adviser preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in advice firms, branches and online.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Affordability | assessment of whether a borrower can sustain repayments |
| Loan to value | the loan as a percentage of the property's value |
| Offer | the lender's formal agreement to lend |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Review the intake record and fact finds so the assessment builds on verified information, not assumptions
2. **Step 2** - Carry out the assessment using mortgage sourcing systems, following the recognised method for advice firms, branches and online
3. **Step 3** - Compare findings against the benchmarks behind application-to-offer times and note where evidence is strong, weak or missing
4. **Step 4** - Draft the report: findings first, then interpretation, then recommendations in priority order
5. **Step 5** - Check the draft for accuracy and for unsuitable product recommendations; have it reviewed where the profession's standards require a second sign-off

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: unsuitable product recommendations.
- Never let this happen: affordability miscalculated.
- Never let this happen: deadlines missed losing purchases.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a mortgage adviser; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to advice firms, branches and online's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
