# Assessment & Findings Report — Environmental Engineering

Assess water companies, industries and regulators and write a defensible findings report as a environmental engineer. The deliverable is a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
This skill is written for a environmental engineer working with water companies, industries and regulators in treatment plants, client sites and design offices.

## When to use
- You are a environmental engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in treatment plants, client sites and design offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Consent | permission to discharge treated water or waste |
| Effluent | liquid waste discharged from a process |
| Remediation | cleaning up contaminated land or water |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Review the intake record and impact assessments so the assessment builds on verified information, not assumptions
2. **Step 2** - Carry out the assessment using modelling software, following the recognised method for treatment plants, client sites and design offices
3. **Step 3** - Compare findings against the benchmarks behind permit compliance and note where evidence is strong, weak or missing
4. **Step 4** - Draft the report: findings first, then interpretation, then recommendations in priority order
5. **Step 5** - Check the draft for accuracy and for pollution incidents; have it reviewed where the profession's standards require a second sign-off

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: pollution incidents.
- Never let this happen: consent breaches.
- Never let this happen: design underperformance.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a environmental engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to treatment plants, client sites and design offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
