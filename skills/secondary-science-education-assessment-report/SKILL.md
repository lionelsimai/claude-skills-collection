# Assessment & Findings Report — Secondary Science Education

Assess secondary school students and write a defensible findings report as a secondary science teacher. The deliverable is a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
This skill is written for a secondary science teacher working with secondary school students in a school science department and laboratories.

## When to use
- You are a secondary science teacher preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a school science department and laboratories.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

| Term | Meaning |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Review the intake record and schemes of work so the assessment builds on verified information, not assumptions
2. **Step 2** - Carry out the assessment using laboratory equipment, following the recognised method for a school science department and laboratories
3. **Step 3** - Compare findings against the benchmarks behind examination results and note where evidence is strong, weak or missing
4. **Step 4** - Draft the report: findings first, then interpretation, then recommendations in priority order
5. **Step 5** - Check the draft for accuracy and for practical safety incidents; have it reviewed where the profession's standards require a second sign-off

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a school science department and laboratories] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [secondary school students and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: practical safety incidents.
- Never let this happen: misconceptions in core concepts.
- Never let this happen: equipment faults.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a secondary science teacher; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a school science department and laboratories's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
