# Intake & Initial Assessment — Customs Brokerage

Run a structured intake and initial assessment for importers and exporters in customs offices and freight operations. The deliverable is a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
This skill is written for a customs broker working with importers and exporters in customs offices and freight operations.

## When to use
- You are a customs broker preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in customs offices and freight operations.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm identity, consent and the reason for referral or engagement, and open the record that will hold customs declarations
2. **Step 2** - Gather history and current circumstances from importers and exporters, using the standard intake structure for customs offices and freight operations
3. **Step 3** - Record baseline observations and measurements relevant to declaration accuracy, noting anything that changes the priority of the case
4. **Step 4** - Flag immediate concerns — especially misclassification penalties — and escalate or fast-track anything urgent before proceeding
5. **Step 5** - Summarise findings for importers and exporters in plain language, agree the next step, and file the intake section of customs declarations

- Never let this happen: clearance delays.
- Never let this happen: prohibited goods shipped.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a customs broker; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to customs offices and freight operations's rules.
