# Client Explanation & Communication Guide — Executive Chef Practice

Explain complex executive chef matters to diners, kitchen teams and restaurant owners in plain, honest language. The deliverable is a communication pack: explanation script, FAQs and a written summary.
This skill is written for a executive chef working with diners, kitchen teams and restaurant owners in professional kitchens.

## When to use
- You are a executive chef preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in professional kitchens.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a communication pack: explanation script, FAQs and a written summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Mise en place | preparing and organising ingredients before service |
| HACCP | the food safety management system based on hazard analysis |
| Ticket time | how long an order takes from printing to serving |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Identify what diners, kitchen teams and restaurant owners actually need to decide or understand, and the misconceptions to clear up first
2. **Step 2** - Translate the technical content — including terms behind menu plans — into plain language without losing accuracy
3. **Step 3** - Structure the explanation: what we found, what it means, the options, the recommendation and the risks such as food poisoning risks
4. **Step 4** - Check understanding by asking diners, kitchen teams and restaurant owners to play it back in their own words; correct gaps kindly
5. **Step 5** - Follow up with a written summary the same day and record the conversation in the file

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: food poisoning risks.
- Never let this happen: cross-contamination.
- Never let this happen: burns and cuts in service.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a executive chef; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to professional kitchens's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
