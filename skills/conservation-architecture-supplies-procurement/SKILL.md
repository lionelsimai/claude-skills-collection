# Supplies & Procurement — Conservation Architecture

Specify, source and manage the supplies and materials used in historic buildings and design studios. The deliverable is a procurement list with specifications, stock levels and supplier checks.
This skill is written for a conservation architect working with heritage bodies, owners of historic buildings and councils in historic buildings and design studios.

## When to use
- You are a conservation architect preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in historic buildings and design studios.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a procurement list with specifications, stock levels and supplier checks.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Listed building | a building protected by law for its heritage value |
| Fabric | the physical material of a historic building |
| Reversibility | repairs that can be undone without harming original material |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - List what the work consumes, drawing on condition surveys and the kit centred on survey equipment, with exact specifications
2. **Step 2** - Set minimum stock levels from usage rates and lead times so work never stalls waiting for supplies
3. **Step 3** - Compare suppliers on quality, reliability and total cost — not unit price alone — and check credentials
4. **Step 4** - Receive and inspect deliveries; reject or quarantine substandard stock before it can contribute to inappropriate repairs damaging fabric
5. **Step 5** - Track usage and waste monthly; adjust order quantities and challenge specifications that no longer fit
