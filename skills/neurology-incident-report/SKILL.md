# Incident Reporting & Response — Neurology

Respond to, record and learn from incidents involving patients with brain, spinal cord and nerve disorders in a neurology department and outpatient clinic. The deliverable is a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list.
This skill is written for a neurologist working with patients with brain, spinal cord and nerve disorders in a neurology department and outpatient clinic.

## When to use
- You are a neurologist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a neurology department and outpatient clinic.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| EEG | a recording of electrical activity in the brain |
| Lesion | an area of damaged tissue |
| Aura | warning symptoms some patients get before a seizure or migraine |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Make the situation safe first; attend to people before paperwork, and escalate if missed stroke presentations is still live
2. **Step 2** - Record facts while they are fresh: what happened, when, who was involved, what was observed — in neurological examination records
3. **Step 3** - Notify the people and bodies who must know, within the timeframes that apply in a neurology department and outpatient clinic
4. **Step 4** - Support those affected, including honest communication with patients with brain, spinal cord and nerve disorders about what happened and what happens next
5. **Step 5** - Analyse causes without blame, agree preventive actions with owners and dates, and track them to closure

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a neurology department and outpatient clinic] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients with brain, spinal cord and nerve disorders and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in neurological examination records.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against stroke assessment timeliness.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in a neurology department and outpatient clinic.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed stroke presentations.
- Never let this happen: status epilepticus delays.
- Never let this happen: medication interactions.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a neurologist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a neurology department and outpatient clinic's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
