# Safety Protocol — Waste Management

Work safely in waste facilities and collection rounds: a practical safety protocol for a waste operations manager and households, businesses and councils. The deliverable is a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions.
This skill is written for a waste operations manager working with households, businesses and councils in waste facilities and collection rounds.

## When to use
- You are a waste operations manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in waste facilities and collection rounds.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Identify the hazards present in waste facilities and collection rounds for staff, households, businesses and councils and visitors, starting with hazardous waste mishandling
2. **Step 2** - Write controls in order: eliminate, substitute, engineer, administrate, then protective equipment such as collection vehicles
3. **Step 3** - Set pre-start checks and stop-work authority — anyone can halt work that looks unsafe, without penalty
4. **Step 4** - Define what happens when something goes wrong: first response, escalation, recording in waste transfer notes
5. **Step 5** - Review the protocol after every incident and at least annually; brief and re-brief everyone it covers

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: hazardous waste mishandling.
- Never let this happen: vehicle accidents.
- Never let this happen: site fires.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a waste operations manager; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to waste facilities and collection rounds's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
