# Follow-Up & Maintenance Schedule — Appliance Repair

Plan follow-up and maintenance so gains for homeowners, landlords and retailers are checked and sustained. The deliverable is a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers.
This skill is written for a appliance repair technician working with homeowners, landlords and retailers in customers' homes and workshops.

## When to use
- You are a appliance repair technician preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in customers' homes and workshops.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Diagnostic code | a fault code displayed by an appliance's controls |
| PAT testing | checking portable appliances for electrical safety |
| Planned obsolescence | designing products with a limited lifespan |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Set follow-up intervals from the plan and from how first-time fix rate is expected to move over time
2. **Step 2** - Define what is checked at each contact, using multimeters where measurement is needed, and record it in job cards
3. **Step 3** - Give homeowners, landlords and retailers clear guidance for the interval: what to do, what to watch for, when to make contact sooner
4. **Step 4** - Act early on warning signs — deterioration linked to electric shock shortens the interval, never lengthens it
5. **Step 5** - Close the follow-up cycle formally when goals are met, with a summary and re-entry route if needs return

2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against first-time fix rate.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in customers' homes and workshops.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: electric shock.
- Never let this happen: gas appliance risks.
- Never let this happen: repeat faults.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a appliance repair technician; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to customers' homes and workshops's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
