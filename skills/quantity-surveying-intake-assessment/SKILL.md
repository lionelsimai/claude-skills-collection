# Intake & Initial Assessment — Quantity Surveying

Run a structured intake and initial assessment for developers, contractors and public clients in construction projects and offices. The deliverable is a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
This skill is written for a quantity surveyor working with developers, contractors and public clients in construction projects and offices.

## When to use
- You are a quantity surveyor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in construction projects and offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Bill of quantities | an itemised list of materials and work for pricing |
| Valuation | an assessment of work completed for interim payment |
| Final account | the agreed total cost at a project's end |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm identity, consent and the reason for referral or engagement, and open the record that will hold bills of quantities
2. **Step 2** - Gather history and current circumstances from developers, contractors and public clients, using the standard intake structure for construction projects and offices
3. **Step 3** - Record baseline observations and measurements relevant to valuation timeliness, noting anything that changes the priority of the case
4. **Step 4** - Flag immediate concerns — especially cost overruns unreported — and escalate or fast-track anything urgent before proceeding
5. **Step 5** - Summarise findings for developers, contractors and public clients in plain language, agree the next step, and file the intake section of bills of quantities

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: cost overruns unreported.
- Never let this happen: measurement errors.
- Never let this happen: contractual deadlines missed.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a quantity surveyor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to construction projects and offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
