# Ethics & Professional Standards — Cardiology

Apply the ethical codes and professional standards that govern a cardiologist. The deliverable is an ethics guide with principles, common dilemmas and decision steps.
This skill is written for a cardiologist working with patients with heart and circulation conditions in a hospital cardiology department and outpatient clinic.

## When to use
- You are a cardiologist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a hospital cardiology department and outpatient clinic.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an ethics guide with principles, common dilemmas and decision steps.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| ECG | a tracing of the heart's electrical activity |
| Ejection fraction | the percentage of blood the left ventricle pumps out each beat |
| Angioplasty | a procedure to widen narrowed coronary arteries |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Know the code that applies in a hospital cardiology department and outpatient clinic: consent, confidentiality, honesty, competence and conflicts of interest
2. **Step 2** - Work through dilemmas with a framework: facts, stakeholders, options, code provisions, then decide and record
3. **Step 3** - Protect patients with heart and circulation conditions first where duties conflict, and be alert to how missed myocardial infarction can arise from ethical shortcuts
4. **Step 4** - Declare conflicts of interest early and manage them transparently rather than hoping they stay invisible
5. **Step 5** - Raise concerns about others' practice through the proper channels, with evidence from ECG reports, and support colleagues who do the same

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a hospital cardiology department and outpatient clinic] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients with heart and circulation conditions and other stakeholders]

The finished output is an ethics guide with principles, common dilemmas and decision steps, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed myocardial infarction.
- Never let this happen: anticoagulant dosing errors.
- Never let this happen: contrast-induced kidney injury.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a cardiologist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a hospital cardiology department and outpatient clinic's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
