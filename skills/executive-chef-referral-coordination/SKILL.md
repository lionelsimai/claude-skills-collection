# Referral & Coordination — Executive Chef Practice

Refer diners, kitchen teams and restaurant owners onward and coordinate with other professionals and services. The deliverable is a referral pack and a coordination log with responses tracked.
This skill is written for a executive chef working with diners, kitchen teams and restaurant owners in professional kitchens.

## When to use
- You are a executive chef preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in professional kitchens.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a referral pack and a coordination log with responses tracked.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm the referral is appropriate: what diners, kitchen teams and restaurant owners need that sits outside the executive chef scope in professional kitchens
2. **Step 2** - Prepare the referral from menu plans — relevant history, current status, the specific question being asked
3. **Step 3** - With consent, send the referral and log who received it and when; chase non-responses on a set schedule
4. **Step 4** - Share incoming findings back into the main record so the plan and food cost control stay joined up
5. **Step 5** - Close the loop with diners, kitchen teams and restaurant owners: explain what happens next, who owns it, and what to do if things worsen — mindful of food poisoning risks

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: food poisoning risks.
- Never let this happen: cross-contamination.
- Never let this happen: burns and cuts in service.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a executive chef; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to professional kitchens's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
