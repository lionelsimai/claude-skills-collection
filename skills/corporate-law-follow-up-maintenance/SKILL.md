# Follow-Up & Maintenance Schedule — Corporate Law

Plan follow-up and maintenance so gains for companies, directors and investors are checked and sustained. The deliverable is a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers.
This skill is written for a corporate solicitor working with companies, directors and investors in a commercial law firm.

## When to use
- You are a corporate solicitor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a commercial law firm.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Due diligence | investigation of a business before a transaction |
| Completion | the point at which a transaction legally takes effect |
| Warranties | contractual statements of fact given by a seller |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Set follow-up intervals from the plan and from how deal completion times is expected to move over time
2. **Step 2** - Define what is checked at each contact, using data room platforms where measurement is needed, and record it in transaction documents
3. **Step 3** - Give companies, directors and investors clear guidance for the interval: what to do, what to watch for, when to make contact sooner
4. **Step 4** - Act early on warning signs — deterioration linked to missed completion deadlines shortens the interval, never lengthens it
5. **Step 5** - Close the follow-up cycle formally when goals are met, with a summary and re-entry route if needs return

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed completion deadlines.
- Never let this happen: undisclosed liabilities.
- Never let this happen: regulatory filing errors.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a corporate solicitor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a commercial law firm's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
