# Progress Review & Adjustment — Family Law

Review progress for clients dealing with divorce, children and family finances against their plan and adjust it with evidence. The deliverable is a progress review summary with decisions and an updated plan.
This skill is written for a family law solicitor working with clients dealing with divorce, children and family finances in a law firm and family court.

## When to use
- You are a family law solicitor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a law firm and family court.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a progress review summary with decisions and an updated plan.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

| Term | Meaning |

| Disclosure | the formal exchange of financial information in family cases |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Pull together client care letters and the latest results for case progression times since the last review
2. **Step 2** - Compare actual progress with the plan's goals and timelines; identify what moved, what stalled and why
3. **Step 3** - Discuss the evidence with clients dealing with divorce, children and family finances honestly, including where the original plan no longer fits
4. **Step 4** - Agree adjustments: continue, intensify, change approach or close — and record the reasoning
5. **Step 5** - Update the plan and review date, and communicate changes to everyone involved in a law firm and family court

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed court deadlines.
- Never let this happen: safeguarding issues unreported.
- Never let this happen: conflicts of interest.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a family law solicitor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a law firm and family court's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
