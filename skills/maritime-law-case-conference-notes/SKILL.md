# Case Conference & Meeting Notes — Maritime Law

Prepare for, contribute to and record case conferences and multi-professional meetings. The deliverable is meeting preparation notes and a decision-focused record of outcomes.
This skill is written for a maritime solicitor working with ship owners, insurers and cargo interests in a shipping law firm and admiralty courts.

## When to use
- You are a maritime solicitor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a shipping law firm and admiralty courts.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces meeting preparation notes and a decision-focused record of outcomes.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Charterparty | the contract for hiring a vessel |
| Bill of lading | the document evidencing receipt and title to cargo |
| General average | shared contribution to losses incurred saving a voyage |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Prepare a one-page brief from charterparties: current position, key evidence, the questions the meeting must answer
2. **Step 2** - Present your contribution concisely, leading with the position on case resolution times and any live concerns about cargo deadlines missed
3. **Step 3** - Listen for information that changes the picture; update your understanding rather than defending a position
4. **Step 4** - Record decisions, actions, owners and deadlines during the meeting — not from memory afterwards
5. **Step 5** - Circulate the record promptly to attendees in a shipping law firm and admiralty courts, correct errors fast, and file it with the main record

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: cargo deadlines missed.
- Never let this happen: vessel arrest errors.
- Never let this happen: jurisdiction mistakes.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a maritime solicitor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a shipping law firm and admiralty courts's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
