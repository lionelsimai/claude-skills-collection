# Assessment & Findings Report — Executive Chef Practice

Assess diners, kitchen teams and restaurant owners and write a defensible findings report as a executive chef. The deliverable is a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
This skill is written for a executive chef working with diners, kitchen teams and restaurant owners in professional kitchens.

## When to use
- You are a executive chef preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in professional kitchens.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Review the intake record and menu plans so the assessment builds on verified information, not assumptions
2. **Step 2** - Carry out the assessment using kitchen equipment, following the recognised method for professional kitchens
3. **Step 3** - Compare findings against the benchmarks behind food cost control and note where evidence is strong, weak or missing
4. **Step 4** - Draft the report: findings first, then interpretation, then recommendations in priority order
5. **Step 5** - Check the draft for accuracy and for food poisoning risks; have it reviewed where the profession's standards require a second sign-off

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: food poisoning risks.
- Never let this happen: cross-contamination.
- Never let this happen: burns and cuts in service.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a executive chef; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to professional kitchens's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
