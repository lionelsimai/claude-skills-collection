# Risk Assessment — Carpentry

Identify, score and control the risks in work for homeowners, builders and commercial clients in workshops and construction sites. The deliverable is a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners.
This skill is written for a carpenter working with homeowners, builders and commercial clients in workshops and construction sites.

## When to use
- You are a carpenter preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in workshops and construction sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Snagging | fixing small defects at the end of a job |
| Mitred joint | a joint cut at an angle, typically 45 degrees |
| Stud wall | a framed internal wall of timber uprights |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Map the activity step by step and list what could go wrong at each stage for homeowners, builders and commercial clients and staff
2. **Step 2** - Score each risk for likelihood and impact, giving special weight to machinery injuries
3. **Step 3** - For every high risk, define a control that removes, reduces or contains it, using power saws where relevant
4. **Step 4** - Record the assessment in cutting lists, assign an owner to each control, and set a review date
5. **Step 5** - Brief everyone involved before work starts; stop and reassess if conditions in workshops and construction sites change

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in workshops and construction sites] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [homeowners, builders and commercial clients and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: machinery injuries.
- Never let this happen: measurement errors wasting material.
- Never let this happen: structural work done wrong.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a carpenter; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to workshops and construction sites's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
