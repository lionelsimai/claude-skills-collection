# Formal Report Writing — Tax Law

Write formal reports as a tax solicitor that stand up to scrutiny from any reader. The deliverable is a report template with structure, evidence standards and review steps.
This skill is written for a tax solicitor working with individuals, businesses and charities with tax questions in a law firm and tax tribunals.

## When to use
- You are a tax solicitor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a law firm and tax tribunals.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a report template with structure, evidence standards and review steps.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Clearance | advance confirmation from a tax authority about treatment of a transaction |
| Disclosure | informing the tax authority of relevant facts |
| Penalty mitigation | reducing penalties through cooperation and care |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Establish the report's purpose, audience and deadline before writing; gather source material from tax opinions
2. **Step 2** - Structure: summary, background, method, findings, analysis, recommendations — in that order
3. **Step 3** - Write findings factually and separately from opinion; label professional judgement as judgement
4. **Step 4** - Support key points with evidence and with opinion turnaround where outcomes are claimed; acknowledge limitations openly
5. **Step 5** - Proofread for accuracy and tone, check names, dates and figures twice, and consider filing deadlines missed before release

## Output Format

The finished output is a report template with structure, evidence standards and review steps, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: filing deadlines missed.
- Never let this happen: penalties from wrong advice.
- Never let this happen: privilege mishandled.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a tax solicitor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a law firm and tax tribunals's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
