# Feedback & Complaints Handling — Executive Chef Practice

Collect feedback from diners, kitchen teams and restaurant owners and handle complaints fairly and visibly. The deliverable is a feedback and complaints process with response standards and learning log.
This skill is written for a executive chef working with diners, kitchen teams and restaurant owners in professional kitchens.

## When to use
- You are a executive chef preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in professional kitchens.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a feedback and complaints process with response standards and learning log.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Invite feedback routinely — not only when things go wrong — and make it easy to give in professional kitchens
2. **Step 2** - Acknowledge complaints quickly, thank the person, and explain the process and timeframe honestly
3. **Step 3** - Investigate from evidence in menu plans; separate the facts from the feelings, and respect both
4. **Step 4** - Respond with findings, an apology where warranted, and concrete remedies or explanations
5. **Step 5** - Log themes and feed them into quality work — repeated complaints about food poisoning risks are an early-warning system, not a nuisance

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: food poisoning risks.
- Never let this happen: cross-contamination.
- Never let this happen: burns and cuts in service.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a executive chef; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to professional kitchens's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
