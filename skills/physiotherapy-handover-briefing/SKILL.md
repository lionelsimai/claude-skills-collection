# Handover Briefing — Physiotherapy

Hand over patients recovering movement, strength and function or active work to another professional without losing critical detail. The deliverable is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
This skill is written for a physiotherapist working with patients recovering movement, strength and function in a physiotherapy department, gym and patients' homes.

## When to use
- You are a physiotherapist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a physiotherapy department, gym and patients' homes.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Range of motion | how far a joint can move |
| Red flags | symptoms suggesting serious underlying conditions |
| Rehabilitation | restoring function after illness or injury |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Summarise the current position from assessment records: what is done, what is in progress, what is waiting
2. **Step 2** - Highlight live risks first — falls during treatment — and the controls or watchpoints already in place
3. **Step 3** - State the next actions with owners and deadlines, and the results expected for functional outcome improvement
4. **Step 4** - Walk the receiving professional through the brief, answer questions, and confirm understanding
5. **Step 5** - Record that handover happened, who accepted it, and where the full record lives in a physiotherapy department, gym and patients' homes

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a physiotherapy department, gym and patients' homes] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients recovering movement, strength and function and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: falls during treatment.
- Never let this happen: overloading healing tissue.
- Never let this happen: missed red-flag symptoms.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a physiotherapist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a physiotherapy department, gym and patients' homes's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
