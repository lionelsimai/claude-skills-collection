# Assessment & Findings Report — Geotechnical Engineering

Assess developers, contractors and infrastructure owners and write a defensible findings report as a geotechnical engineer. The deliverable is a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
This skill is written for a geotechnical engineer working with developers, contractors and infrastructure owners in site investigation teams and design offices.

## When to use
- You are a geotechnical engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in site investigation teams and design offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Borehole | a drilled hole used to sample ground and groundwater |
| Bearing capacity | the load the ground can safely support |
| Settlement | downward movement of the ground or a structure |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Review the intake record and ground investigation reports so the assessment builds on verified information, not assumptions
2. **Step 2** - Carry out the assessment using drilling rigs, following the recognised method for site investigation teams and design offices
3. **Step 3** - Compare findings against the benchmarks behind investigation turnaround and note where evidence is strong, weak or missing
4. **Step 4** - Draft the report: findings first, then interpretation, then recommendations in priority order
5. **Step 5** - Check the draft for accuracy and for ground conditions misjudged; have it reviewed where the profession's standards require a second sign-off

2. Detail, following the workflow above

4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: ground conditions misjudged.
- Never let this happen: slope failures.
- Never let this happen: foundation settlement.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a geotechnical engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to site investigation teams and design offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
