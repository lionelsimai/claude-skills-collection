# Stakeholder Presentation — Water Treatment

Present water treatment operator findings and proposals to decision-makers and stakeholders. The deliverable is a presentation structure with evidence summary and decision asks.
This skill is written for a water treatment operator working with the public, via water companies in treatment works and pumping stations.

## When to use
- You are a water treatment operator preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in treatment works and pumping stations.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a presentation structure with evidence summary and decision asks.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Open with the decision or conclusion, then the evidence — busy stakeholders should not wait for the point
2. **Step 2** - Support claims with data from process logs and movement in water quality compliance, with sources and limits stated
3. **Step 3** - Address the hard parts directly, including contamination reaching supply and what it would take to reduce it
4. **Step 4** - Make the ask specific: what decision, by when, with what resources for work in treatment works and pumping stations
5. **Step 5** - Follow up in writing the same day: decisions made, actions agreed, owners and dates

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in treatment works and pumping stations] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [the public, via water companies and other stakeholders]

2. Detail, following the workflow above

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: contamination reaching supply.
- Never let this happen: chemical dosing errors.
- Never let this happen: process failures.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a water treatment operator; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to treatment works and pumping stations's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
