# Stakeholder Presentation — Quantity Surveying

Present quantity surveyor findings and proposals to decision-makers and stakeholders. The deliverable is a presentation structure with evidence summary and decision asks.
This skill is written for a quantity surveyor working with developers, contractors and public clients in construction projects and offices.

## When to use
- You are a quantity surveyor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in construction projects and offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a presentation structure with evidence summary and decision asks.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Bill of quantities | an itemised list of materials and work for pricing |
| Valuation | an assessment of work completed for interim payment |
| Final account | the agreed total cost at a project's end |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Open with the decision or conclusion, then the evidence — busy stakeholders should not wait for the point
2. **Step 2** - Support claims with data from bills of quantities and movement in valuation timeliness, with sources and limits stated
3. **Step 3** - Address the hard parts directly, including cost overruns unreported and what it would take to reduce it
4. **Step 4** - Make the ask specific: what decision, by when, with what resources for work in construction projects and offices
5. **Step 5** - Follow up in writing the same day: decisions made, actions agreed, owners and dates

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in construction projects and offices] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [developers, contractors and public clients and other stakeholders]

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: cost overruns unreported.
- Never let this happen: measurement errors.
- Never let this happen: contractual deadlines missed.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a quantity surveyor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to construction projects and offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
