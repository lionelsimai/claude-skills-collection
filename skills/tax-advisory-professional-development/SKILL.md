# Continuing Professional Development Plan — Tax Advisory

Plan and evidence continuing professional development for a practising tax adviser. The deliverable is a CPD plan mapped to competency gaps, with evidence and reflection records.
This skill is written for a tax adviser working with individuals and businesses planning their tax affairs in an accountancy or advisory firm.

## When to use
- You are a tax adviser preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in an accountancy or advisory firm.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a CPD plan mapped to competency gaps, with evidence and reflection records.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Tax relief | a legal reduction in tax payable |
| Enquiry | an investigation by the tax authority into a return |
| Residence | the country where a person is treated as living for tax |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Audit current competence against the standards for an accountancy or advisory firm and the evidence in your recent tax computations
2. **Step 2** - Choose development priorities that close real gaps — including staying current on missed filing deadlines — not just easy hours
3. **Step 3** - Mix formats: courses, supervised practice, case review, reading and teaching others
4. **Step 4** - After each activity, write a short reflection: what changed in your practice and what it means for filing deadline compliance
5. **Step 5** - Keep the CPD record inspection-ready and review the plan at appraisal or revalidation time

## Output Format

The finished output is a CPD plan mapped to competency gaps, with evidence and reflection records, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed filing deadlines.
- Never let this happen: aggressive schemes challenged.
- Never let this happen: relief claims overlooked.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a tax adviser; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to an accountancy or advisory firm's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
