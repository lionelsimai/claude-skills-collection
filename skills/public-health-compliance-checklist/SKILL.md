# Compliance & Standards Checklist — Public Health

Check work in public health departments and community settings against the laws, standards and codes that govern a public health practitioner. The deliverable is a completed compliance checklist with gaps and corrective actions.
This skill is written for a public health practitioner working with populations, communities and partner organisations in public health departments and community settings.

## When to use
- You are a public health practitioner preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in public health departments and community settings.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed compliance checklist with gaps and corrective actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Surveillance | ongoing collection and analysis of health data |
| Incidence | the rate of new cases of a condition |
| Intervention | an action intended to improve population health |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - List the obligations that apply: legislation, regulator guidance, professional codes and local policy in public health departments and community settings
2. **Step 2** - Work through each obligation against current practice and needs assessments, marking compliant, partial or missing
3. **Step 3** - Investigate every gap — especially anything touching health inequalities widened — and record the evidence for the judgement
4. **Step 4** - Turn gaps into corrective actions with owners and deadlines, proportionate to the risk
5. **Step 5** - Re-check completed actions, file the checklist, and diarise the next compliance review

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in public health departments and community settings] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [populations, communities and partner organisations and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a completed compliance checklist with gaps and corrective actions, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in needs assessments.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against programme coverage.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in public health departments and community settings.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: health inequalities widened.
- Never let this happen: outbreak signals missed.
- Never let this happen: misinformation unaddressed.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a public health practitioner; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to public health departments and community settings's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
