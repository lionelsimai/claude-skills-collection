# Safety Protocol — Healthcare Administration

Work safely in hospital and clinic administration offices: a practical safety protocol for a healthcare administrator and patients, clinical teams and service managers. The deliverable is a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions.
This skill is written for a healthcare administrator working with patients, clinical teams and service managers in hospital and clinic administration offices.

## When to use
- You are a healthcare administrator preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in hospital and clinic administration offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Waiting list | the record of patients awaiting an appointment or procedure |
| Utilisation | how fully booked capacity is used |
| Triage category | the priority assigned to a referral |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Identify the hazards present in hospital and clinic administration offices for staff, patients, clinical teams and service managers and visitors, starting with waiting list errors
2. **Step 2** - Write controls in order: eliminate, substitute, engineer, administrate, then protective equipment such as patient administration systems
3. **Step 3** - Set pre-start checks and stop-work authority — anyone can halt work that looks unsafe, without penalty
4. **Step 4** - Define what happens when something goes wrong: first response, escalation, recording in clinic schedules
5. **Step 5** - Review the protocol after every incident and at least annually; brief and re-brief everyone it covers

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: waiting list errors.
- Never let this happen: patient data breaches.
- Never let this happen: clinic overbooking.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a healthcare administrator; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to hospital and clinic administration offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
