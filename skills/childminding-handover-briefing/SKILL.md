# Handover Briefing — Childminding

Hand over young children and their parents or active work to another professional without losing critical detail. The deliverable is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
This skill is written for a registered childminder working with young children and their parents in the childminder's home.

## When to use
- You are a registered childminder preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in the childminder's home.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Ratio | the legal limit of children per adult |
| EYFS | the early years foundation stage framework used in England |
| Settling in | helping a child adjust to a new care setting |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Summarise the current position from learning records: what is done, what is in progress, what is waiting
2. **Step 2** - Highlight live risks first — safeguarding concerns missed — and the controls or watchpoints already in place
3. **Step 3** - State the next actions with owners and deadlines, and the results expected for occupancy rates
4. **Step 4** - Walk the receiving professional through the brief, answer questions, and confirm understanding
5. **Step 5** - Record that handover happened, who accepted it, and where the full record lives in the childminder's home

## Profession-Specific Components
| Component | Details for Childminding |
|-----------|-------------------------------|
| Core documentation | learning records; attendance registers; accident records; contracts and policies |
| Core tools & equipment | toys and learning resources; safety equipment; parent communication apps; observation tools |
| People involved | young children and their parents; the registered childminder; colleagues and other services in the childminder's home |
| Primary record | learning records — keep it current, dated and attributable |

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in the childminder's home] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [young children and their parents and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in learning records.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against occupancy rates.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in the childminder's home.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: safeguarding concerns missed.
- Never let this happen: accidents from unsafe environments.
- Never let this happen: ratio breaches.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a registered childminder; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to the childminder's home's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
