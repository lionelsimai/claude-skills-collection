# Intake & Initial Assessment — Bartending

Run a structured intake and initial assessment for bar customers and venue managers in bars, restaurants and event venues. The deliverable is a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
This skill is written for a bartender working with bar customers and venue managers in bars, restaurants and event venues.

## When to use
- You are a bartender preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in bars, restaurants and event venues.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm identity, consent and the reason for referral or engagement, and open the record that will hold stock counts
2. **Step 2** - Gather history and current circumstances from bar customers and venue managers, using the standard intake structure for bars, restaurants and event venues
3. **Step 3** - Record baseline observations and measurements relevant to stock variance, noting anything that changes the priority of the case
4. **Step 4** - Flag immediate concerns — especially over-service of alcohol — and escalate or fast-track anything urgent before proceeding
5. **Step 5** - Summarise findings for bar customers and venue managers in plain language, agree the next step, and file the intake section of stock counts

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: over-service of alcohol.
- Never let this happen: glass injuries.
- Never let this happen: stock theft.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a bartender; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to bars, restaurants and event venues's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
