# Assessment & Findings Report — Forensic Accounting

Assess courts, insurers and businesses investigating financial wrongdoing and write a defensible findings report as a forensic accountant. The deliverable is a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
This skill is written for a forensic accountant working with courts, insurers and businesses investigating financial wrongdoing in an accountancy firm and legal proceedings.

## When to use
- You are a forensic accountant preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in an accountancy firm and legal proceedings.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Review the intake record and investigation reports so the assessment builds on verified information, not assumptions
2. **Step 2** - Carry out the assessment using data analysis software, following the recognised method for an accountancy firm and legal proceedings
3. **Step 3** - Compare findings against the benchmarks behind investigation turnaround and note where evidence is strong, weak or missing
4. **Step 4** - Draft the report: findings first, then interpretation, then recommendations in priority order
5. **Step 5** - Check the draft for accuracy and for evidence contamination; have it reviewed where the profession's standards require a second sign-off

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: evidence contamination.
- Never let this happen: assumptions challenged in court.
- Never let this happen: conflicts of interest.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a forensic accountant; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to an accountancy firm and legal proceedings's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
