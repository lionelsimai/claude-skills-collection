# Tools & Equipment Checklist — Ophthalmology

Select, check and maintain the tools and equipment a ophthalmologist relies on. The deliverable is an equipment checklist with inspection points, maintenance dates and replacement triggers.
This skill is written for a ophthalmologist working with patients with eye conditions and vision problems in an eye hospital clinic and theatre.

## When to use
- You are a ophthalmologist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in an eye hospital clinic and theatre.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an equipment checklist with inspection points, maintenance dates and replacement triggers.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Visual acuity | the sharpness of vision, often recorded as a fraction |
| OCT | a scan showing cross-sections of the retina |
| Glaucoma | optic nerve damage often linked to raised eye pressure |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - List the essential kit for work in an eye hospital clinic and theatre, centred on slit lamps, with what each item is used for
2. **Step 2** - Define pre-use checks: condition, calibration or charge status, cleanliness and safety features
3. **Step 3** - Set maintenance and replacement intervals based on usage and manufacturer guidance, and log them against visual acuity charts
4. **Step 4** - Quarantine faulty items immediately — faulty kit is a direct route to missed glaucoma progression
5. **Step 5** - Review the kit list quarterly against the work actually done and retire or add items deliberately

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in an eye hospital clinic and theatre] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients with eye conditions and vision problems and other stakeholders]

The finished output is an equipment checklist with inspection points, maintenance dates and replacement triggers, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed glaucoma progression.
- Never let this happen: endophthalmitis after surgery.
- Never let this happen: wrong lens implant.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a ophthalmologist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to an eye hospital clinic and theatre's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
