# Session Delivery Guide — Forensic Science

Prepare for and deliver a professional session or appointment for police, courts and legal teams in laboratories and crime scenes. The deliverable is a session plan, delivery checklist and post-session record.
This skill is written for a forensic scientist working with police, courts and legal teams in laboratories and crime scenes.

## When to use
- You are a forensic scientist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in laboratories and crime scenes.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a session plan, delivery checklist and post-session record.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Chain of custody | the documented movement of evidence |
| Exhibit | an item of evidence in an investigation |
| Proficiency testing | external checks of a scientist's accuracy |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Review examination records before the session so you know the history, goals and any cautions such as evidence contamination
2. **Step 2** - Prepare the space and laboratory instruments; check everything works and is safe before police, courts and legal teams arrive
3. **Step 3** - Open the session by agreeing the focus and what a good outcome looks like, linked to report turnaround
4. **Step 4** - Deliver the session, adapting in the moment while staying inside the agreed plan and professional scope
5. **Step 5** - Close by summarising what was covered, confirming follow-ups, and completing the session record the same day

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in laboratories and crime scenes] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [police, courts and legal teams and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a session plan, delivery checklist and post-session record, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: evidence contamination.
- Never let this happen: interpretation overstated.
- Never let this happen: chain of custody gaps.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a forensic scientist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to laboratories and crime scenes's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
