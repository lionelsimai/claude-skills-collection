# Service Plan Design — Oncology

Design a realistic, measurable service plan for patients diagnosed with cancer and their families as a oncologist. The deliverable is a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates.
This skill is written for a oncologist working with patients diagnosed with cancer and their families in a cancer centre and oncology day unit.

## When to use
- You are a oncologist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a cancer centre and oncology day unit.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Staging | classifying how far a cancer has spread |
| Adjuvant therapy | treatment given after primary therapy to reduce recurrence risk |
| Neutropenia | abnormally low neutrophil count raising infection risk |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Start from the assessment findings and agree goals with patients diagnosed with cancer and their families — specific enough to measure against time from diagnosis to first treatment
2. **Step 2** - Break each goal into actions sequenced for a cancer centre and oncology day unit, naming who does what and by when
3. **Step 3** - Build the plan around staging reports so progress, changes and decisions are recorded in one place
4. **Step 4** - Price or resource the plan honestly: time, infusion pumps and materials, with contingencies for delayed diagnosis
5. **Step 5** - Set the review date and the trigger points that would change the plan, and confirm the plan with all parties

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a cancer centre and oncology day unit] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients diagnosed with cancer and their families and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in staging reports.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against time from diagnosis to first treatment.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in a cancer centre and oncology day unit.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: delayed diagnosis.
- Never let this happen: chemotherapy prescribing errors.
- Never let this happen: unmanaged treatment side-effects.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a oncologist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a cancer centre and oncology day unit's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
