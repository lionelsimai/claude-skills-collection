# Metrics & Outcomes Review — Oil & Gas Operations

Track, interpret and act on the outcome metrics that matter for a petroleum operations engineer. The deliverable is a metrics dashboard definition and an interpretation/action review.
This skill is written for a petroleum operations engineer working with energy companies and regulators in offshore platforms and onshore terminals.

## When to use
- You are a petroleum operations engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in offshore platforms and onshore terminals.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a metrics dashboard definition and an interpretation/action review.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Permit to work | a formal authorisation for hazardous work |
| Well control | preventing uncontrolled flow from a well |
| Shutdown | a planned or emergency stop of production |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Define each metric precisely — start with production rates — including source data in production reports and collection method
2. **Step 2** - Set review cadence and comparison baselines so trends, not single data points, drive decisions
3. **Step 3** - Investigate movements before celebrating or panicking: check data quality, case mix and one-off causes
4. **Step 4** - Link metric movements to practice changes and to risks such as hydrocarbon releases; test one change at a time where possible
5. **Step 5** - Report results honestly to stakeholders in offshore platforms and onshore terminals, including where outcomes fell short and what changes

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in offshore platforms and onshore terminals] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [energy companies and regulators and other stakeholders]

2. Detail, following the workflow above

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: hydrocarbon releases.
- Never let this happen: well control incidents.
- Never let this happen: permit breaches.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a petroleum operations engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to offshore platforms and onshore terminals's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
