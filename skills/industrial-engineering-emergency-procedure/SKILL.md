# Emergency Response Procedure — Industrial Engineering

Respond to emergencies in factories, warehouses and service operations calmly, in role and in the right order. The deliverable is an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
This skill is written for a industrial engineer working with manufacturing and service organisations in factories, warehouses and service operations.

## When to use
- You are a industrial engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in factories, warehouses and service operations.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Recognise the emergency early: know the presentations that matter for manufacturing and service organisations, especially changes disrupting production
2. **Step 2** - Take the immediate actions a industrial engineer is trained and authorised for, using simulation software as drilled
3. **Step 3** - Call for help with a structured message: location in factories, warehouses and service operations, situation, what has been done, what is needed
4. **Step 4** - Keep manufacturing and service organisations and bystanders informed and safe while help arrives; hand over with a concise summary
5. **Step 5** - Record the event fully in process maps afterwards, debrief the team, and update the procedure with lessons learned

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: changes disrupting production.
- Never let this happen: worker strain from poor design.
- Never let this happen: unmeasured bottlenecks.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a industrial engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to factories, warehouses and service operations's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
