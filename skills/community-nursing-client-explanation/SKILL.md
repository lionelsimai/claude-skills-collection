# Client Explanation & Communication Guide — Community Nursing

Explain complex community nurse matters to patients receiving care in their own homes in plain, honest language. The deliverable is a communication pack: explanation script, FAQs and a written summary.
This skill is written for a community nurse working with patients receiving care in their own homes in patients' homes and community clinics.

## When to use
- You are a community nurse preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in patients' homes and community clinics.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a communication pack: explanation script, FAQs and a written summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Caseload | the group of patients a nurse is responsible for |
| Lone working | working alone without direct supervision |
| Tissue viability | the health and integrity of skin and underlying tissue |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Identify what patients receiving care in their own homes actually need to decide or understand, and the misconceptions to clear up first
2. **Step 2** - Translate the technical content — including terms behind care plans — into plain language without losing accuracy
3. **Step 3** - Structure the explanation: what we found, what it means, the options, the recommendation and the risks such as lone working incidents
4. **Step 4** - Check understanding by asking patients receiving care in their own homes to play it back in their own words; correct gaps kindly
5. **Step 5** - Follow up with a written summary the same day and record the conversation in the file

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: lone working incidents.
- Never let this happen: missed deterioration at home.
- Never let this happen: medication errors.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a community nurse; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to patients' homes and community clinics's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
