# Cost Estimates & Quotes — Secondary Science Education

Build transparent, defensible cost estimates and quotes as a secondary science teacher. The deliverable is an itemised estimate with assumptions, exclusions and validity terms.
This skill is written for a secondary science teacher working with secondary school students in a school science department and laboratories.

## When to use
- You are a secondary science teacher preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a school science department and laboratories.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an itemised estimate with assumptions, exclusions and validity terms.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Risk assessment | evaluating hazards in practical work before it happens |
| Hypothesis | a testable prediction |
| Controlled variable | a factor kept constant in an investigation |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Scope the work precisely from the assessment and schemes of work; list what is included and excluded
2. **Step 2** - Cost each element: professional time, laboratory equipment usage, materials, travel and third-party fees
3. **Step 3** - Add contingency proportionate to uncertainty — and say so openly rather than burying it, which protects against practical safety incidents
4. **Step 4** - Present the quote with options where genuine choices exist, and relate value to outcomes such as examination results
5. **Step 5** - Record acceptance, variations and final costs against the estimate so future quotes get more accurate

## Output Format

2. Detail, following the workflow above

4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: practical safety incidents.
- Never let this happen: misconceptions in core concepts.
- Never let this happen: equipment faults.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a secondary science teacher; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a school science department and laboratories's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
