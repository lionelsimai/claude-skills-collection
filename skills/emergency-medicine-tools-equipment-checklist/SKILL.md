# Tools & Equipment Checklist — Emergency Medicine

Select, check and maintain the tools and equipment a emergency physician relies on. The deliverable is an equipment checklist with inspection points, maintenance dates and replacement triggers.
This skill is written for a emergency physician working with patients arriving with acute illness or injury in a hospital emergency department.

## When to use
- You are a emergency physician preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a hospital emergency department.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an equipment checklist with inspection points, maintenance dates and replacement triggers.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Triage | sorting patients by urgency of need |
| Resuscitation | emergency treatment to restore circulation or breathing |
| Sepsis | a life-threatening response to infection |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - List the essential kit for work in a hospital emergency department, centred on defibrillators, with what each item is used for
2. **Step 2** - Define pre-use checks: condition, calibration or charge status, cleanliness and safety features
3. **Step 3** - Set maintenance and replacement intervals based on usage and manufacturer guidance, and log them against triage records
4. **Step 4** - Quarantine faulty items immediately — faulty kit is a direct route to missed time-critical diagnoses
5. **Step 5** - Review the kit list quarterly against the work actually done and retire or add items deliberately

## Profession-Specific Components
| Component | Details for Emergency Medicine |
|-----------|-------------------------------|

| Primary record | triage records — keep it current, dated and attributable |

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a hospital emergency department] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients arriving with acute illness or injury and other stakeholders]

The finished output is an equipment checklist with inspection points, maintenance dates and replacement triggers, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in triage records.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against triage-to-clinician time.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in a hospital emergency department.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed time-critical diagnoses.
- Never let this happen: triage under-scoring.
- Never let this happen: crowding-related delays.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a emergency physician; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a hospital emergency department's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
