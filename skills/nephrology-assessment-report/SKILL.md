# Assessment & Findings Report — Nephrology

Assess patients with kidney disease, including dialysis patients and write a defensible findings report as a nephrologist. The deliverable is a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
This skill is written for a nephrologist working with patients with kidney disease, including dialysis patients in a renal unit and dialysis centre.

## When to use
- You are a nephrologist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a renal unit and dialysis centre.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Dialysis | artificial filtering of the blood when kidneys fail |
| GFR | glomerular filtration rate, a measure of kidney function |
| Fistula | a surgically created connection used for dialysis access |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Review the intake record and dialysis prescriptions so the assessment builds on verified information, not assumptions
2. **Step 2** - Carry out the assessment using haemodialysis machines, following the recognised method for a renal unit and dialysis centre
3. **Step 3** - Compare findings against the benchmarks behind dialysis adequacy scores and note where evidence is strong, weak or missing
4. **Step 4** - Draft the report: findings first, then interpretation, then recommendations in priority order
5. **Step 5** - Check the draft for accuracy and for missed hyperkalaemia; have it reviewed where the profession's standards require a second sign-off

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a renal unit and dialysis centre] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients with kidney disease, including dialysis patients and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed hyperkalaemia.
- Never let this happen: dialysis access infection.
- Never let this happen: fluid overload.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a nephrologist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a renal unit and dialysis centre's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
