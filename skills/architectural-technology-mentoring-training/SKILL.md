# Mentoring & Training Junior Staff — Architectural Technology

Train and mentor junior colleagues toward competent, safe architectural technologist practice. The deliverable is a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
This skill is written for a architectural technologist working with architects, developers and homeowners in design studios and construction sites.

## When to use
- You are a architectural technologist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in design studios and construction sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Building regulations | legal standards for design and construction work |
| Detailing | the precise drawings of how building elements join |
| Clash detection | finding conflicts between building systems in a model |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Assess the trainee's current level honestly against the competencies required in design studios and construction sites
2. **Step 2** - Sequence learning from observation to supervised practice to independent work, using CAD and BIM software under supervision first
3. **Step 3** - Set explicit sign-off criteria for high-risk tasks — especially anything touching regulation non-compliance — and never sign off early
4. **Step 4** - Give feedback from direct observation and from technical drawings, specific and timely, with strengths named too
5. **Step 5** - Record progress, keep the training file current, and escalate persistent gaps through supervision, not gossip

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: regulation non-compliance.
- Never let this happen: detailing errors causing site problems.
- Never let this happen: clash detection missed.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a architectural technologist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to design studios and construction sites's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
