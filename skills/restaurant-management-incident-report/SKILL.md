# Incident Reporting & Response — Restaurant Management

Respond to, record and learn from incidents involving diners, kitchen and front-of-house teams in restaurants and cafes. The deliverable is a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list.
This skill is written for a restaurant manager working with diners, kitchen and front-of-house teams in restaurants and cafes.

## When to use
- You are a restaurant manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in restaurants and cafes.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Make the situation safe first; attend to people before paperwork, and escalate if food hygiene failures is still live
2. **Step 2** - Record facts while they are fresh: what happened, when, who was involved, what was observed — in menus and costing sheets
3. **Step 3** - Notify the people and bodies who must know, within the timeframes that apply in restaurants and cafes
4. **Step 4** - Support those affected, including honest communication with diners, kitchen and front-of-house teams about what happened and what happens next
5. **Step 5** - Analyse causes without blame, agree preventive actions with owners and dates, and track them to closure

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in restaurants and cafes] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [diners, kitchen and front-of-house teams and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: food hygiene failures.
- Never let this happen: allergen incidents.
- Never let this happen: cash handling discrepancies.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a restaurant manager; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to restaurants and cafes's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
