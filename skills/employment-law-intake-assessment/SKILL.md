# Intake & Initial Assessment — Employment Law

Run a structured intake and initial assessment for employees, employers and HR teams in a law firm and employment tribunals. The deliverable is a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
This skill is written for a employment solicitor working with employees, employers and HR teams in a law firm and employment tribunals.

## When to use
- You are a employment solicitor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a law firm and employment tribunals.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Settlement agreement | a binding agreement ending employment and waiving claims |
| Unfair dismissal | termination of employment without a fair reason or process |
| Tribunal | the court that hears employment disputes |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm identity, consent and the reason for referral or engagement, and open the record that will hold contracts of employment
2. **Step 2** - Gather history and current circumstances from employees, employers and HR teams, using the standard intake structure for a law firm and employment tribunals
3. **Step 3** - Record baseline observations and measurements relevant to claim resolution times, noting anything that changes the priority of the case
4. **Step 4** - Flag immediate concerns — especially tribunal deadlines missed — and escalate or fast-track anything urgent before proceeding
5. **Step 5** - Summarise findings for employees, employers and HR teams in plain language, agree the next step, and file the intake section of contracts of employment

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: tribunal deadlines missed.
- Never let this happen: discrimination risks unadvised.
- Never let this happen: settlement terms misunderstood.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a employment solicitor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a law firm and employment tribunals's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
