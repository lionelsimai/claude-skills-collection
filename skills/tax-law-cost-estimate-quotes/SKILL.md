# Cost Estimates & Quotes — Tax Law

Build transparent, defensible cost estimates and quotes as a tax solicitor. The deliverable is an itemised estimate with assumptions, exclusions and validity terms.
This skill is written for a tax solicitor working with individuals, businesses and charities with tax questions in a law firm and tax tribunals.

## When to use
- You are a tax solicitor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a law firm and tax tribunals.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an itemised estimate with assumptions, exclusions and validity terms.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Clearance | advance confirmation from a tax authority about treatment of a transaction |
| Disclosure | informing the tax authority of relevant facts |
| Penalty mitigation | reducing penalties through cooperation and care |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Scope the work precisely from the assessment and tax opinions; list what is included and excluded
2. **Step 2** - Cost each element: professional time, tax research databases usage, materials, travel and third-party fees
3. **Step 3** - Add contingency proportionate to uncertainty — and say so openly rather than burying it, which protects against filing deadlines missed
4. **Step 4** - Present the quote with options where genuine choices exist, and relate value to outcomes such as opinion turnaround
5. **Step 5** - Record acceptance, variations and final costs against the estimate so future quotes get more accurate

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: filing deadlines missed.
- Never let this happen: penalties from wrong advice.
- Never let this happen: privilege mishandled.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a tax solicitor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a law firm and tax tribunals's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
