# Handover Briefing — Dermatology

Hand over patients with skin, hair and nail conditions or active work to another professional without losing critical detail. The deliverable is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
This skill is written for a dermatologist working with patients with skin, hair and nail conditions in a dermatology outpatient clinic and minor surgery suite.

## When to use
- You are a dermatologist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a dermatology outpatient clinic and minor surgery suite.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Dermatoscope | a handheld magnifier for examining skin lesions |
| Biopsy | removing a small tissue sample for laboratory analysis |
| Eczema | a group of conditions causing inflamed, itchy skin |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Summarise the current position from skin examination records: what is done, what is in progress, what is waiting
2. **Step 2** - Highlight live risks first — melanoma missed at triage — and the controls or watchpoints already in place
3. **Step 3** - State the next actions with owners and deadlines, and the results expected for urgent suspected-cancer referral timeliness
4. **Step 4** - Walk the receiving professional through the brief, answer questions, and confirm understanding
5. **Step 5** - Record that handover happened, who accepted it, and where the full record lives in a dermatology outpatient clinic and minor surgery suite

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a dermatology outpatient clinic and minor surgery suite] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients with skin, hair and nail conditions and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: melanoma missed at triage.
- Never let this happen: inappropriate long-term steroid use.
- Never let this happen: infection after minor surgery.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a dermatologist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a dermatology outpatient clinic and minor surgery suite's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
