# Stakeholder Presentation — Anaesthesiology

Present anaesthetist findings and proposals to decision-makers and stakeholders. The deliverable is a presentation structure with evidence summary and decision asks.
This skill is written for a anaesthetist working with patients undergoing surgery or painful procedures in operating theatres and recovery units.

## When to use
- You are a anaesthetist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in operating theatres and recovery units.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a presentation structure with evidence summary and decision asks.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Airway | the passage through which a patient breathes under anaesthesia |
| Induction | the phase of starting anaesthesia |
| PONV | post-operative nausea and vomiting |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Open with the decision or conclusion, then the evidence — busy stakeholders should not wait for the point
2. **Step 2** - Support claims with data from anaesthetic charts and movement in pre-operative assessment completion, with sources and limits stated
3. **Step 3** - Address the hard parts directly, including airway emergencies and what it would take to reduce it
4. **Step 4** - Make the ask specific: what decision, by when, with what resources for work in operating theatres and recovery units
5. **Step 5** - Follow up in writing the same day: decisions made, actions agreed, owners and dates

| Component | Details for Anaesthesiology |

| Core documentation | anaesthetic charts; pre-operative assessments; airway plans; pain management plans |
| Core tools & equipment | anaesthetic machines; ventilators; airway devices; patient monitors |
| People involved | patients undergoing surgery or painful procedures; the anaesthetist; colleagues and other services in operating theatres and recovery units |
| Primary record | anaesthetic charts — keep it current, dated and attributable |

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in operating theatres and recovery units] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients undergoing surgery or painful procedures and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a presentation structure with evidence summary and decision asks, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in anaesthetic charts.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against pre-operative assessment completion.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in operating theatres and recovery units.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: airway emergencies.
- Never let this happen: drug errors under pressure.
- Never let this happen: post-operative respiratory depression.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a anaesthetist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to operating theatres and recovery units's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
