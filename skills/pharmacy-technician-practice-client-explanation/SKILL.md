# Client Explanation & Communication Guide — Pharmacy Practice

Explain complex pharmacy technician matters to patients and customers collecting medicines and advice in plain, honest language. The deliverable is a communication pack: explanation script, FAQs and a written summary.
This skill is written for a pharmacy technician working with patients and customers collecting medicines and advice in a community pharmacy.

## When to use
- You are a pharmacy technician preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a community pharmacy.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a communication pack: explanation script, FAQs and a written summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Dispensing | preparing and supplying medicines against a prescription |
| Controlled drug | a medicine subject to strict legal controls |
| SOP | standard operating procedure |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Identify what patients and customers collecting medicines and advice actually need to decide or understand, and the misconceptions to clear up first
2. **Step 2** - Translate the technical content — including terms behind dispensing records — into plain language without losing accuracy
3. **Step 3** - Structure the explanation: what we found, what it means, the options, the recommendation and the risks such as dispensing errors
4. **Step 4** - Check understanding by asking patients and customers collecting medicines and advice to play it back in their own words; correct gaps kindly
5. **Step 5** - Follow up with a written summary the same day and record the conversation in the file

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a community pharmacy] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients and customers collecting medicines and advice and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a communication pack: explanation script, FAQs and a written summary, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: dispensing errors.
- Never let this happen: controlled drug discrepancies.
- Never let this happen: expired stock supplied.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a pharmacy technician; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a community pharmacy's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
