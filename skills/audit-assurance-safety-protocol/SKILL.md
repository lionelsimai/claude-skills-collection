# Safety Protocol — Audit & Assurance

Work safely in an accountancy firm and client sites: a practical safety protocol for a external auditor and companies, charities and public bodies requiring an audit. The deliverable is a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions.
This skill is written for a external auditor working with companies, charities and public bodies requiring an audit in an accountancy firm and client sites.

## When to use
- You are a external auditor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in an accountancy firm and client sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Materiality | the size at which errors could influence decisions |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Identify the hazards present in an accountancy firm and client sites for staff, companies, charities and public bodies requiring an audit and visitors, starting with material misstatements missed
2. **Step 2** - Write controls in order: eliminate, substitute, engineer, administrate, then protective equipment such as audit software
3. **Step 3** - Set pre-start checks and stop-work authority — anyone can halt work that looks unsafe, without penalty
4. **Step 4** - Define what happens when something goes wrong: first response, escalation, recording in audit plans
5. **Step 5** - Review the protocol after every incident and at least annually; brief and re-brief everyone it covers

1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: material misstatements missed.
- Never let this happen: independence threats.
- Never let this happen: deadline overruns.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a external auditor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to an accountancy firm and client sites's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
