# Client Explanation & Communication Guide — Environmental Law

Explain complex environmental solicitor matters to developers, businesses and community groups in plain, honest language. The deliverable is a communication pack: explanation script, FAQs and a written summary.
This skill is written for a environmental solicitor working with developers, businesses and community groups in a law firm and planning inquiries.

## When to use
- You are a environmental solicitor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a law firm and planning inquiries.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a communication pack: explanation script, FAQs and a written summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Environmental permit | official permission for activities affecting the environment |
| EIA | environmental impact assessment of a proposed development |
| Contaminated land | land polluted by previous use |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Identify what developers, businesses and community groups actually need to decide or understand, and the misconceptions to clear up first
2. **Step 2** - Translate the technical content — including terms behind permit applications — into plain language without losing accuracy
3. **Step 3** - Structure the explanation: what we found, what it means, the options, the recommendation and the risks such as permit breaches unadvised
4. **Step 4** - Check understanding by asking developers, businesses and community groups to play it back in their own words; correct gaps kindly
5. **Step 5** - Follow up with a written summary the same day and record the conversation in the file

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: permit breaches unadvised.
- Never let this happen: consultation deadlines missed.
- Never let this happen: contaminated land liabilities.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a environmental solicitor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a law firm and planning inquiries's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
