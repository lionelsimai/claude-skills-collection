# Metrics & Outcomes Review — Welding

Track, interpret and act on the outcome metrics that matter for a welder. The deliverable is a metrics dashboard definition and an interpretation/action review.
This skill is written for a welder working with fabrication companies, construction and repair clients in workshops, shipyards and construction sites.

## When to use
- You are a welder preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in workshops, shipyards and construction sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a metrics dashboard definition and an interpretation/action review.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| WPS | a weld procedure specification defining how a weld is made |
| Penetration | how deeply a weld fuses into the base metal |
| Slag | a residue that must be removed from some welds |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Define each metric precisely — start with weld defect rates — including source data in weld procedure specifications and collection method
2. **Step 2** - Set review cadence and comparison baselines so trends, not single data points, drive decisions
3. **Step 3** - Investigate movements before celebrating or panicking: check data quality, case mix and one-off causes
4. **Step 4** - Link metric movements to practice changes and to risks such as weld failures in service; test one change at a time where possible
5. **Step 5** - Report results honestly to stakeholders in workshops, shipyards and construction sites, including where outcomes fell short and what changes

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: weld failures in service.
- Never let this happen: eye and skin burns.
- Never let this happen: fumes exposure.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a welder; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to workshops, shipyards and construction sites's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
