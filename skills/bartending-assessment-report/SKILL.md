# Assessment & Findings Report — Bartending

Assess bar customers and venue managers and write a defensible findings report as a bartender. The deliverable is a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
This skill is written for a bartender working with bar customers and venue managers in bars, restaurants and event venues.

## When to use
- You are a bartender preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in bars, restaurants and event venues.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Stock variance | the difference between recorded and actual stock |
| Free pouring | pouring measures without a measure, requiring training |
| Cellar management | storing and rotating drinks stock correctly |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Review the intake record and stock counts so the assessment builds on verified information, not assumptions
2. **Step 2** - Carry out the assessment using point of sale systems, following the recognised method for bars, restaurants and event venues
3. **Step 3** - Compare findings against the benchmarks behind stock variance and note where evidence is strong, weak or missing
4. **Step 4** - Draft the report: findings first, then interpretation, then recommendations in priority order
5. **Step 5** - Check the draft for accuracy and for over-service of alcohol; have it reviewed where the profession's standards require a second sign-off

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: over-service of alcohol.
- Never let this happen: glass injuries.
- Never let this happen: stock theft.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a bartender; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to bars, restaurants and event venues's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
