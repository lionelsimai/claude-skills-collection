# Emergency Response Procedure — Bartending

Respond to emergencies in bars, restaurants and event venues calmly, in role and in the right order. The deliverable is an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
This skill is written for a bartender working with bar customers and venue managers in bars, restaurants and event venues.

## When to use
- You are a bartender preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in bars, restaurants and event venues.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Stock variance | the difference between recorded and actual stock |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Recognise the emergency early: know the presentations that matter for bar customers and venue managers, especially over-service of alcohol
2. **Step 2** - Take the immediate actions a bartender is trained and authorised for, using point of sale systems as drilled
3. **Step 3** - Call for help with a structured message: location in bars, restaurants and event venues, situation, what has been done, what is needed
4. **Step 4** - Keep bar customers and venue managers and bystanders informed and safe while help arrives; hand over with a concise summary
5. **Step 5** - Record the event fully in stock counts afterwards, debrief the team, and update the procedure with lessons learned

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: over-service of alcohol.
- Never let this happen: glass injuries.
- Never let this happen: stock theft.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a bartender; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to bars, restaurants and event venues's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
