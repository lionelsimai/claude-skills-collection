# Quality Audit — Commercial Piloting

Audit the quality of commercial pilot work in cockpits and flight operations and drive measurable improvement. The deliverable is an audit report with scores, evidence and an improvement plan.
This skill is written for a commercial pilot working with airlines, passengers and cargo customers in cockpits and flight operations.

## When to use
- You are a commercial pilot preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in cockpits and flight operations.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an audit report with scores, evidence and an improvement plan.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Flight plan | the planned route and details filed before a flight |
| Type rating | qualification to fly a specific aircraft type |
| Sterile cockpit | periods when only flight-critical conversation is allowed |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Define the audit sample and criteria up front, anchored to training check pass rates and the standards for cockpits and flight operations
2. **Step 2** - Review a sample of flight plans and observe practice where permitted, scoring consistently
3. **Step 3** - Separate isolated errors from patterns — patterns point to system fixes, not individual blame
4. **Step 4** - Report findings with evidence, and agree improvement actions that address the root causes, including fatigue risks
5. **Step 5** - Re-audit after the agreed interval and publish the change in scores so improvement is visible

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: fatigue risks.
- Never let this happen: airspace infringements.
- Never let this happen: documentation errors.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a commercial pilot; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to cockpits and flight operations's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
