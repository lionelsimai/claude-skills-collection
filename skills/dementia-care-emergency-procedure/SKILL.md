# Emergency Response Procedure — Dementia Care

Respond to emergencies in care homes, hospitals and homes calmly, in role and in the right order. The deliverable is an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
This skill is written for a dementia care specialist working with people living with dementia and their families in care homes, hospitals and homes.

## When to use
- You are a dementia care specialist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in care homes, hospitals and homes.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Life story work | gathering a person's history to personalise care |
| Capacity | a person's ability to make a specific decision |
| Reminiscence | using memories and familiar items to support wellbeing |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Recognise the emergency early: know the presentations that matter for people living with dementia and their families, especially distress misread as behaviour problems
2. **Step 2** - Take the immediate actions a dementia care specialist is trained and authorised for, using memory aids as drilled
3. **Step 3** - Call for help with a structured message: location in care homes, hospitals and homes, situation, what has been done, what is needed
4. **Step 4** - Keep people living with dementia and their families and bystanders informed and safe while help arrives; hand over with a concise summary
5. **Step 5** - Record the event fully in life story documents afterwards, debrief the team, and update the procedure with lessons learned

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in care homes, hospitals and homes] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [people living with dementia and their families and other stakeholders]

The finished output is an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan

2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against distress incident rates.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in care homes, hospitals and homes.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: distress misread as behaviour problems.
- Never let this happen: unsafe wandering.
- Never let this happen: families not involved.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a dementia care specialist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to care homes, hospitals and homes's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
