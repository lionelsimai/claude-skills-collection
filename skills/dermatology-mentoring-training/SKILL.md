# Mentoring & Training Junior Staff — Dermatology

Train and mentor junior colleagues toward competent, safe dermatologist practice. The deliverable is a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
This skill is written for a dermatologist working with patients with skin, hair and nail conditions in a dermatology outpatient clinic and minor surgery suite.

## When to use
- You are a dermatologist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a dermatology outpatient clinic and minor surgery suite.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Dermatoscope | a handheld magnifier for examining skin lesions |
| Biopsy | removing a small tissue sample for laboratory analysis |
| Eczema | a group of conditions causing inflamed, itchy skin |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Assess the trainee's current level honestly against the competencies required in a dermatology outpatient clinic and minor surgery suite
2. **Step 2** - Sequence learning from observation to supervised practice to independent work, using dermatoscopes under supervision first
3. **Step 3** - Set explicit sign-off criteria for high-risk tasks — especially anything touching melanoma missed at triage — and never sign off early
4. **Step 4** - Give feedback from direct observation and from skin examination records, specific and timely, with strengths named too
5. **Step 5** - Record progress, keep the training file current, and escalate persistent gaps through supervision, not gossip

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a dermatology outpatient clinic and minor surgery suite] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients with skin, hair and nail conditions and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in skin examination records.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against urgent suspected-cancer referral timeliness.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in a dermatology outpatient clinic and minor surgery suite.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: melanoma missed at triage.
- Never let this happen: inappropriate long-term steroid use.
- Never let this happen: infection after minor surgery.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a dermatologist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a dermatology outpatient clinic and minor surgery suite's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
