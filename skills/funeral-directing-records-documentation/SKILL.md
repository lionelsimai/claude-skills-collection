# Records & Documentation — Funeral Directing

Create and maintain accurate, auditable records for work with bereaved families. The deliverable is a documentation standard, templates and a completed record set.
This skill is written for a funeral director working with bereaved families in funeral homes, crematoria and cemeteries.

## When to use
- You are a funeral director preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in funeral homes, crematoria and cemeteries.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a documentation standard, templates and a completed record set.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Arrangement | the meeting where a funeral is planned |
| Embalming | preserving and preparing a deceased person |
| Cortege | the procession of vehicles at a funeral |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Use funeral arrangements as the single source of truth; record facts, observations and decisions — not speculation
2. **Step 2** - Write entries contemporaneously where possible, dated and attributable, in the format funeral homes, crematoria and cemeteries requires
3. **Step 3** - Record measurements and outcomes that feed arrangement accuracy exactly as observed, with units and method noted
4. **Step 4** - Store and share records under the confidentiality and retention rules that apply in funeral homes, crematoria and cemeteries, guarding against wrong details in services
5. **Step 5** - Audit a sample of records monthly for completeness and correct anything missing while the work is still current

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: wrong details in services.
- Never let this happen: documentation errors delaying funerals.
- Never let this happen: identification mistakes.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a funeral director; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to funeral homes, crematoria and cemeteries's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
