# Quality Audit — Dermatology

Audit the quality of dermatologist work in a dermatology outpatient clinic and minor surgery suite and drive measurable improvement. The deliverable is an audit report with scores, evidence and an improvement plan.
This skill is written for a dermatologist working with patients with skin, hair and nail conditions in a dermatology outpatient clinic and minor surgery suite.

## When to use
- You are a dermatologist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a dermatology outpatient clinic and minor surgery suite.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an audit report with scores, evidence and an improvement plan.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Dermatoscope | a handheld magnifier for examining skin lesions |
| Biopsy | removing a small tissue sample for laboratory analysis |
| Eczema | a group of conditions causing inflamed, itchy skin |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Define the audit sample and criteria up front, anchored to urgent suspected-cancer referral timeliness and the standards for a dermatology outpatient clinic and minor surgery suite
2. **Step 2** - Review a sample of skin examination records and observe practice where permitted, scoring consistently
3. **Step 3** - Separate isolated errors from patterns — patterns point to system fixes, not individual blame
4. **Step 4** - Report findings with evidence, and agree improvement actions that address the root causes, including melanoma missed at triage
5. **Step 5** - Re-audit after the agreed interval and publish the change in scores so improvement is visible

2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: melanoma missed at triage.
- Never let this happen: inappropriate long-term steroid use.
- Never let this happen: infection after minor surgery.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a dermatologist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a dermatology outpatient clinic and minor surgery suite's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
