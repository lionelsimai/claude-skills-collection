# Stakeholder Presentation — Healthcare Administration

Present healthcare administrator findings and proposals to decision-makers and stakeholders. The deliverable is a presentation structure with evidence summary and decision asks.
This skill is written for a healthcare administrator working with patients, clinical teams and service managers in hospital and clinic administration offices.

## When to use
- You are a healthcare administrator preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in hospital and clinic administration offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a presentation structure with evidence summary and decision asks.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Waiting list | the record of patients awaiting an appointment or procedure |
| Utilisation | how fully booked capacity is used |
| Triage category | the priority assigned to a referral |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Open with the decision or conclusion, then the evidence — busy stakeholders should not wait for the point
2. **Step 2** - Support claims with data from clinic schedules and movement in waiting list accuracy, with sources and limits stated
3. **Step 3** - Address the hard parts directly, including waiting list errors and what it would take to reduce it
4. **Step 4** - Make the ask specific: what decision, by when, with what resources for work in hospital and clinic administration offices
5. **Step 5** - Follow up in writing the same day: decisions made, actions agreed, owners and dates

| Component | Details for Healthcare Administration |
|-----------|-------------------------------|
| Core documentation | clinic schedules; waiting list records; referral logs; complaint files |
| Core tools & equipment | patient administration systems; scheduling software; telephony systems; reporting dashboards |
| People involved | patients, clinical teams and service managers; the healthcare administrator; colleagues and other services in hospital and clinic administration offices |
| Primary record | clinic schedules — keep it current, dated and attributable |

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in hospital and clinic administration offices] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients, clinical teams and service managers and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a presentation structure with evidence summary and decision asks, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in clinic schedules.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against waiting list accuracy.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in hospital and clinic administration offices.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: waiting list errors.
- Never let this happen: patient data breaches.
- Never let this happen: clinic overbooking.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a healthcare administrator; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to hospital and clinic administration offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
