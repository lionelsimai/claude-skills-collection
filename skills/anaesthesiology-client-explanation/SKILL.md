# Client Explanation & Communication Guide — Anaesthesiology

Explain complex anaesthetist matters to patients undergoing surgery or painful procedures in plain, honest language. The deliverable is a communication pack: explanation script, FAQs and a written summary.
This skill is written for a anaesthetist working with patients undergoing surgery or painful procedures in operating theatres and recovery units.

## When to use
- You are a anaesthetist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in operating theatres and recovery units.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a communication pack: explanation script, FAQs and a written summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Airway | the passage through which a patient breathes under anaesthesia |
| Induction | the phase of starting anaesthesia |
| PONV | post-operative nausea and vomiting |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Identify what patients undergoing surgery or painful procedures actually need to decide or understand, and the misconceptions to clear up first
2. **Step 2** - Translate the technical content — including terms behind anaesthetic charts — into plain language without losing accuracy
3. **Step 3** - Structure the explanation: what we found, what it means, the options, the recommendation and the risks such as airway emergencies
4. **Step 4** - Check understanding by asking patients undergoing surgery or painful procedures to play it back in their own words; correct gaps kindly
5. **Step 5** - Follow up with a written summary the same day and record the conversation in the file

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in operating theatres and recovery units] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients undergoing surgery or painful procedures and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a communication pack: explanation script, FAQs and a written summary, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: airway emergencies.
- Never let this happen: drug errors under pressure.
- Never let this happen: post-operative respiratory depression.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a anaesthetist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to operating theatres and recovery units's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
