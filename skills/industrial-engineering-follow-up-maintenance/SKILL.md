# Follow-Up & Maintenance Schedule — Industrial Engineering

Plan follow-up and maintenance so gains for manufacturing and service organisations are checked and sustained. The deliverable is a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers.
This skill is written for a industrial engineer working with manufacturing and service organisations in factories, warehouses and service operations.

## When to use
- You are a industrial engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in factories, warehouses and service operations.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Throughput | the rate at which a system produces output |
| Bottleneck | the stage limiting a whole process's capacity |
| Lean | a method focused on removing waste from processes |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Set follow-up intervals from the plan and from how throughput improvement is expected to move over time
2. **Step 2** - Define what is checked at each contact, using simulation software where measurement is needed, and record it in process maps
3. **Step 3** - Give manufacturing and service organisations clear guidance for the interval: what to do, what to watch for, when to make contact sooner
4. **Step 4** - Act early on warning signs — deterioration linked to changes disrupting production shortens the interval, never lengthens it
5. **Step 5** - Close the follow-up cycle formally when goals are met, with a summary and re-entry route if needs return

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in factories, warehouses and service operations] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [manufacturing and service organisations and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in process maps.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against throughput improvement.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in factories, warehouses and service operations.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: changes disrupting production.
- Never let this happen: worker strain from poor design.
- Never let this happen: unmeasured bottlenecks.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a industrial engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to factories, warehouses and service operations's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
