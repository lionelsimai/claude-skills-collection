# Intake & Initial Assessment — Healthcare Administration

Run a structured intake and initial assessment for patients, clinical teams and service managers in hospital and clinic administration offices. The deliverable is a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
This skill is written for a healthcare administrator working with patients, clinical teams and service managers in hospital and clinic administration offices.

## When to use
- You are a healthcare administrator preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in hospital and clinic administration offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Waiting list | the record of patients awaiting an appointment or procedure |
| Utilisation | how fully booked capacity is used |
| Triage category | the priority assigned to a referral |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm identity, consent and the reason for referral or engagement, and open the record that will hold clinic schedules
2. **Step 2** - Gather history and current circumstances from patients, clinical teams and service managers, using the standard intake structure for hospital and clinic administration offices
3. **Step 3** - Record baseline observations and measurements relevant to waiting list accuracy, noting anything that changes the priority of the case
4. **Step 4** - Flag immediate concerns — especially waiting list errors — and escalate or fast-track anything urgent before proceeding
5. **Step 5** - Summarise findings for patients, clinical teams and service managers in plain language, agree the next step, and file the intake section of clinic schedules

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: waiting list errors.
- Never let this happen: patient data breaches.
- Never let this happen: clinic overbooking.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a healthcare administrator; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to hospital and clinic administration offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
