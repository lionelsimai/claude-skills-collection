# Scheduling & Workload Management — Veterinary Nursing

Plan schedules, caseloads and turnaround times for a veterinary nurse in a veterinary practice. The deliverable is a workable schedule template with priorities, buffers and escalation rules.
This skill is written for a veterinary nurse working with animals in care and their owners in a veterinary practice.

## When to use
- You are a veterinary nurse preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a veterinary practice.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a workable schedule template with priorities, buffers and escalation rules.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Hospitalisation chart | a record of an inpatient animal's care and observations |
| Analgesia | pain relief |
| Triage | prioritising patients by urgency in practice |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Map demand: appointment or job types, durations, and the deadlines that drive patient monitoring compliance
2. **Step 2** - Build the schedule around fixed commitments in a veterinary practice first, then fit flexible work around them
3. **Step 3** - Protect buffer time for overruns, urgent cases and the admin behind nursing care plans — schedules without buffers fail by week two
4. **Step 4** - Define triage rules so urgent work (for example anything involving unrecognised pain in animals) displaces routine work predictably
5. **Step 5** - Review the schedule weekly against actuals and adjust booking rules, staffing or monitoring equipment availability

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: unrecognised pain in animals.
- Never let this happen: fluid therapy errors.
- Never let this happen: escape or injury in kennels.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a veterinary nurse; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a veterinary practice's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
