# Incident Reporting & Response — Ophthalmology

Respond to, record and learn from incidents involving patients with eye conditions and vision problems in an eye hospital clinic and theatre. The deliverable is a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list.
This skill is written for a ophthalmologist working with patients with eye conditions and vision problems in an eye hospital clinic and theatre.

## When to use
- You are a ophthalmologist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in an eye hospital clinic and theatre.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Visual acuity | the sharpness of vision, often recorded as a fraction |
| OCT | a scan showing cross-sections of the retina |
| Glaucoma | optic nerve damage often linked to raised eye pressure |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Make the situation safe first; attend to people before paperwork, and escalate if missed glaucoma progression is still live
2. **Step 2** - Record facts while they are fresh: what happened, when, who was involved, what was observed — in visual acuity charts
3. **Step 3** - Notify the people and bodies who must know, within the timeframes that apply in an eye hospital clinic and theatre
4. **Step 4** - Support those affected, including honest communication with patients with eye conditions and vision problems about what happened and what happens next
5. **Step 5** - Analyse causes without blame, agree preventive actions with owners and dates, and track them to closure

## Profession-Specific Components
| Component | Details for Ophthalmology |
|-----------|-------------------------------|
| Core documentation | visual acuity charts; OCT scans; operation notes; refraction records |
| Core tools & equipment | slit lamps; OCT scanners; phacoemulsification machines; visual field analysers |
| People involved | patients with eye conditions and vision problems; the ophthalmologist; colleagues and other services in an eye hospital clinic and theatre |
| Primary record | visual acuity charts — keep it current, dated and attributable |

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in an eye hospital clinic and theatre] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients with eye conditions and vision problems and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in visual acuity charts.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against cataract surgery outcomes.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in an eye hospital clinic and theatre.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed glaucoma progression.
- Never let this happen: endophthalmitis after surgery.
- Never let this happen: wrong lens implant.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a ophthalmologist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to an eye hospital clinic and theatre's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
