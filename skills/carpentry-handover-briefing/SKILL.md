# Handover Briefing — Carpentry

Hand over homeowners, builders and commercial clients or active work to another professional without losing critical detail. The deliverable is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
This skill is written for a carpenter working with homeowners, builders and commercial clients in workshops and construction sites.

## When to use
- You are a carpenter preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in workshops and construction sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Snagging | fixing small defects at the end of a job |
| Mitred joint | a joint cut at an angle, typically 45 degrees |
| Stud wall | a framed internal wall of timber uprights |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Summarise the current position from cutting lists: what is done, what is in progress, what is waiting
2. **Step 2** - Highlight live risks first — machinery injuries — and the controls or watchpoints already in place
3. **Step 3** - State the next actions with owners and deadlines, and the results expected for job completion times
4. **Step 4** - Walk the receiving professional through the brief, answer questions, and confirm understanding
5. **Step 5** - Record that handover happened, who accepted it, and where the full record lives in workshops and construction sites

| Core tools & equipment | power saws; routers; measuring tools; workshop machinery |

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in workshops and construction sites] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [homeowners, builders and commercial clients and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in cutting lists.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against job completion times.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in workshops and construction sites.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: machinery injuries.
- Never let this happen: measurement errors wasting material.
- Never let this happen: structural work done wrong.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a carpenter; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to workshops and construction sites's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
