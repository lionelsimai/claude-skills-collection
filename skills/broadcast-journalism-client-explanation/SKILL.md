# Client Explanation & Communication Guide — Broadcast Journalism

Explain complex broadcast journalist matters to audiences, contributors and news editors in plain, honest language. The deliverable is a communication pack: explanation script, FAQs and a written summary.
This skill is written for a broadcast journalist working with audiences, contributors and news editors in newsrooms and outside broadcasts.

## When to use
- You are a broadcast journalist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in newsrooms and outside broadcasts.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a communication pack: explanation script, FAQs and a written summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Running order | the planned sequence of items in a programme |
| Compliance | checking content against broadcast rules and law |
| Package | a pre-recorded report within a programme |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Identify what audiences, contributors and news editors actually need to decide or understand, and the misconceptions to clear up first
2. **Step 2** - Translate the technical content — including terms behind running orders — into plain language without losing accuracy
3. **Step 3** - Structure the explanation: what we found, what it means, the options, the recommendation and the risks such as inaccurate reporting broadcast
4. **Step 4** - Check understanding by asking audiences, contributors and news editors to play it back in their own words; correct gaps kindly
5. **Step 5** - Follow up with a written summary the same day and record the conversation in the file

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in newsrooms and outside broadcasts] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [audiences, contributors and news editors and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a communication pack: explanation script, FAQs and a written summary, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: inaccurate reporting broadcast.
- Never let this happen: contributor harm.
- Never let this happen: legal risks in reports.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a broadcast journalist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to newsrooms and outside broadcasts's rules.
