# Continuing Professional Development Plan — Intellectual Property Law

Plan and evidence continuing professional development for a practising intellectual property solicitor. The deliverable is a CPD plan mapped to competency gaps, with evidence and reflection records.
This skill is written for a intellectual property solicitor working with creators, brands and technology companies in a law firm and intellectual property offices.

## When to use
- You are a intellectual property solicitor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a law firm and intellectual property offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a CPD plan mapped to competency gaps, with evidence and reflection records.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Trade mark | a sign distinguishing one business's goods or services |
| Infringement | unauthorised use of protected intellectual property |
| Licence | permission to use intellectual property on agreed terms |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Audit current competence against the standards for a law firm and intellectual property offices and the evidence in your recent trade mark applications
2. **Step 2** - Choose development priorities that close real gaps — including staying current on missed renewal deadlines — not just easy hours
3. **Step 3** - Mix formats: courses, supervised practice, case review, reading and teaching others
4. **Step 4** - After each activity, write a short reflection: what changed in your practice and what it means for filing success rates
5. **Step 5** - Keep the CPD record inspection-ready and review the plan at appraisal or revalidation time

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed renewal deadlines.
- Never let this happen: infringing use unnoticed.
- Never let this happen: ownership chains unclear.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a intellectual property solicitor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a law firm and intellectual property offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
