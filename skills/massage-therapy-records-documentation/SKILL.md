# Records & Documentation — Massage Therapy

Create and maintain accurate, auditable records for work with clients seeking treatment for tension, pain or relaxation. The deliverable is a documentation standard, templates and a completed record set.
This skill is written for a massage therapist working with clients seeking treatment for tension, pain or relaxation in clinics, spas and sports settings.

## When to use
- You are a massage therapist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in clinics, spas and sports settings.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a documentation standard, templates and a completed record set.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Contraindication | a condition making a treatment unsafe or requiring adaptation |
| Deep tissue | massage working on deeper layers of muscle |
| Aftercare | advice given to a client following treatment |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Use client health forms as the single source of truth; record facts, observations and decisions — not speculation
2. **Step 2** - Write entries contemporaneously where possible, dated and attributable, in the format clinics, spas and sports settings requires
3. **Step 3** - Record measurements and outcomes that feed client rebooking rates exactly as observed, with units and method noted
4. **Step 4** - Store and share records under the confidentiality and retention rules that apply in clinics, spas and sports settings, guarding against contraindications missed causing harm
5. **Step 5** - Audit a sample of records monthly for completeness and correct anything missing while the work is still current

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: contraindications missed causing harm.
- Never let this happen: boundary issues.
- Never let this happen: injuries aggravated.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a massage therapist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to clinics, spas and sports settings's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
