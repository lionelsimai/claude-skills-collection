# Risk Assessment — Industrial Engineering

Identify, score and control the risks in work for manufacturing and service organisations in factories, warehouses and service operations. The deliverable is a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners.
This skill is written for a industrial engineer working with manufacturing and service organisations in factories, warehouses and service operations.

## When to use
- You are a industrial engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in factories, warehouses and service operations.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Throughput | the rate at which a system produces output |
| Bottleneck | the stage limiting a whole process's capacity |
| Lean | a method focused on removing waste from processes |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Map the activity step by step and list what could go wrong at each stage for manufacturing and service organisations and staff
2. **Step 2** - Score each risk for likelihood and impact, giving special weight to changes disrupting production
3. **Step 3** - For every high risk, define a control that removes, reduces or contains it, using simulation software where relevant
4. **Step 4** - Record the assessment in process maps, assign an owner to each control, and set a review date
5. **Step 5** - Brief everyone involved before work starts; stop and reassess if conditions in factories, warehouses and service operations change

## Output Format

2. Detail, following the workflow above

4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: changes disrupting production.
- Never let this happen: worker strain from poor design.
- Never let this happen: unmeasured bottlenecks.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a industrial engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to factories, warehouses and service operations's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
