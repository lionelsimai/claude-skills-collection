# Formal Report Writing — Commercial Piloting

Write formal reports as a commercial pilot that stand up to scrutiny from any reader. The deliverable is a report template with structure, evidence standards and review steps.
This skill is written for a commercial pilot working with airlines, passengers and cargo customers in cockpits and flight operations.

## When to use
- You are a commercial pilot preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in cockpits and flight operations.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a report template with structure, evidence standards and review steps.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Flight plan | the planned route and details filed before a flight |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Establish the report's purpose, audience and deadline before writing; gather source material from flight plans
2. **Step 2** - Structure: summary, background, method, findings, analysis, recommendations — in that order
3. **Step 3** - Write findings factually and separately from opinion; label professional judgement as judgement
4. **Step 4** - Support key points with evidence and with training check pass rates where outcomes are claimed; acknowledge limitations openly
5. **Step 5** - Proofread for accuracy and tone, check names, dates and figures twice, and consider fatigue risks before release

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in cockpits and flight operations] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [airlines, passengers and cargo customers and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a report template with structure, evidence standards and review steps, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: fatigue risks.
- Never let this happen: airspace infringements.
- Never let this happen: documentation errors.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a commercial pilot; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to cockpits and flight operations's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
