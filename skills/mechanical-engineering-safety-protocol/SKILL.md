# Safety Protocol — Mechanical Engineering

Work safely in design offices, factories and test facilities: a practical safety protocol for a mechanical engineer and manufacturing and product clients. The deliverable is a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions.
This skill is written for a mechanical engineer working with manufacturing and product clients in design offices, factories and test facilities.

## When to use
- You are a mechanical engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in design offices, factories and test facilities.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Tolerance | the permitted variation in a dimension |
| Fatigue | weakening of material from repeated loading |
| Prototype | an early version built to test a design |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Identify the hazards present in design offices, factories and test facilities for staff, manufacturing and product clients and visitors, starting with component failures
2. **Step 2** - Write controls in order: eliminate, substitute, engineer, administrate, then protective equipment such as CAD software
3. **Step 3** - Set pre-start checks and stop-work authority — anyone can halt work that looks unsafe, without penalty
4. **Step 4** - Define what happens when something goes wrong: first response, escalation, recording in design files
5. **Step 5** - Review the protocol after every incident and at least annually; brief and re-brief everyone it covers
