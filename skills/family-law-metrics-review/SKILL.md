# Metrics & Outcomes Review — Family Law

Track, interpret and act on the outcome metrics that matter for a family law solicitor. The deliverable is a metrics dashboard definition and an interpretation/action review.
This skill is written for a family law solicitor working with clients dealing with divorce, children and family finances in a law firm and family court.

## When to use
- You are a family law solicitor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a law firm and family court.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a metrics dashboard definition and an interpretation/action review.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Disclosure | the formal exchange of financial information in family cases |
| Consent order | an agreed financial settlement approved by the court |
| Non-molestation order | a court order protecting a person from abuse |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Define each metric precisely — start with case progression times — including source data in client care letters and collection method
2. **Step 2** - Set review cadence and comparison baselines so trends, not single data points, drive decisions
3. **Step 3** - Investigate movements before celebrating or panicking: check data quality, case mix and one-off causes
4. **Step 4** - Link metric movements to practice changes and to risks such as missed court deadlines; test one change at a time where possible
5. **Step 5** - Report results honestly to stakeholders in a law firm and family court, including where outcomes fell short and what changes

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed court deadlines.
- Never let this happen: safeguarding issues unreported.
- Never let this happen: conflicts of interest.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a family law solicitor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a law firm and family court's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
