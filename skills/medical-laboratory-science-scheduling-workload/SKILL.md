# Scheduling & Workload Management — Medical Laboratory Science

Plan schedules, caseloads and turnaround times for a biomedical scientist in a hospital pathology laboratory. The deliverable is a workable schedule template with priorities, buffers and escalation rules.
This skill is written for a biomedical scientist working with patients whose diagnosis depends on laboratory results, via clinical teams in a hospital pathology laboratory.

## When to use
- You are a biomedical scientist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a hospital pathology laboratory.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a workable schedule template with priorities, buffers and escalation rules.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Quality control | checks that a test system is performing correctly |
| Turnaround time | time from sample receipt to result |
| Haemolysis | breakdown of red blood cells that can spoil a sample |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Map demand: appointment or job types, durations, and the deadlines that drive result turnaround time
2. **Step 2** - Build the schedule around fixed commitments in a hospital pathology laboratory first, then fit flexible work around them
3. **Step 3** - Protect buffer time for overruns, urgent cases and the admin behind test requests — schedules without buffers fail by week two
4. **Step 4** - Define triage rules so urgent work (for example anything involving sample mislabelling) displaces routine work predictably
5. **Step 5** - Review the schedule weekly against actuals and adjust booking rules, staffing or laboratory analysers availability

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: sample mislabelling.
- Never let this happen: delayed critical results.
- Never let this happen: analyser faults unnoticed.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a biomedical scientist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a hospital pathology laboratory's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
