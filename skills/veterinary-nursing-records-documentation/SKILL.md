# Records & Documentation — Veterinary Nursing

Create and maintain accurate, auditable records for work with animals in care and their owners. The deliverable is a documentation standard, templates and a completed record set.
This skill is written for a veterinary nurse working with animals in care and their owners in a veterinary practice.

## When to use
- You are a veterinary nurse preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a veterinary practice.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a documentation standard, templates and a completed record set.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Hospitalisation chart | a record of an inpatient animal's care and observations |
| Analgesia | pain relief |
| Triage | prioritising patients by urgency in practice |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Use nursing care plans as the single source of truth; record facts, observations and decisions — not speculation
2. **Step 2** - Write entries contemporaneously where possible, dated and attributable, in the format a veterinary practice requires
3. **Step 3** - Record measurements and outcomes that feed patient monitoring compliance exactly as observed, with units and method noted
4. **Step 4** - Store and share records under the confidentiality and retention rules that apply in a veterinary practice, guarding against unrecognised pain in animals
5. **Step 5** - Audit a sample of records monthly for completeness and correct anything missing while the work is still current

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: unrecognised pain in animals.
- Never let this happen: fluid therapy errors.
- Never let this happen: escape or injury in kennels.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a veterinary nurse; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a veterinary practice's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
