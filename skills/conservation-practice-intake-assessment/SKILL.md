# Intake & Initial Assessment — Conservation Practice

Run a structured intake and initial assessment for landowners, visitors and conservation charities in nature reserves and countryside sites. The deliverable is a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
This skill is written for a conservation officer working with landowners, visitors and conservation charities in nature reserves and countryside sites.

## When to use
- You are a conservation officer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in nature reserves and countryside sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Habitat | the natural home of a species |
| Biodiversity | the variety of living things in an area |
| Invasive species | a non-native species harming the environment |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm identity, consent and the reason for referral or engagement, and open the record that will hold management plans
2. **Step 2** - Gather history and current circumstances from landowners, visitors and conservation charities, using the standard intake structure for nature reserves and countryside sites
3. **Step 3** - Record baseline observations and measurements relevant to habitat condition scores, noting anything that changes the priority of the case
4. **Step 4** - Flag immediate concerns — especially habitat damage from works — and escalate or fast-track anything urgent before proceeding
5. **Step 5** - Summarise findings for landowners, visitors and conservation charities in plain language, agree the next step, and file the intake section of management plans

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: habitat damage from works.
- Never let this happen: invasive species spread.
- Never let this happen: public safety incidents.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a conservation officer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to nature reserves and countryside sites's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
