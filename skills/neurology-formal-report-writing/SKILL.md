# Formal Report Writing — Neurology

Write formal reports as a neurologist that stand up to scrutiny from any reader. The deliverable is a report template with structure, evidence standards and review steps.
This skill is written for a neurologist working with patients with brain, spinal cord and nerve disorders in a neurology department and outpatient clinic.

## When to use
- You are a neurologist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a neurology department and outpatient clinic.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a report template with structure, evidence standards and review steps.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| EEG | a recording of electrical activity in the brain |
| Lesion | an area of damaged tissue |
| Aura | warning symptoms some patients get before a seizure or migraine |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Establish the report's purpose, audience and deadline before writing; gather source material from neurological examination records
2. **Step 2** - Structure: summary, background, method, findings, analysis, recommendations — in that order
3. **Step 3** - Write findings factually and separately from opinion; label professional judgement as judgement
4. **Step 4** - Support key points with evidence and with stroke assessment timeliness where outcomes are claimed; acknowledge limitations openly
5. **Step 5** - Proofread for accuracy and tone, check names, dates and figures twice, and consider missed stroke presentations before release

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a neurology department and outpatient clinic] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients with brain, spinal cord and nerve disorders and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a report template with structure, evidence standards and review steps, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed stroke presentations.
- Never let this happen: status epilepticus delays.
- Never let this happen: medication interactions.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a neurologist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a neurology department and outpatient clinic's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
