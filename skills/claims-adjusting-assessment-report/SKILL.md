# Assessment & Findings Report — Claims Adjusting

Assess policyholders, insurers and repairers and write a defensible findings report as a claims adjuster. The deliverable is a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
This skill is written for a claims adjuster working with policyholders, insurers and repairers in insurance offices and loss sites.

## When to use
- You are a claims adjuster preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in insurance offices and loss sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Reserve | money set aside for the expected cost of a claim |
| Leakage | claim payments higher than they should be |
| Indemnity | restoring the insured to their pre-loss position |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Review the intake record and claims files so the assessment builds on verified information, not assumptions
2. **Step 2** - Carry out the assessment using claims systems, following the recognised method for insurance offices and loss sites
3. **Step 3** - Compare findings against the benchmarks behind claims cycle time and note where evidence is strong, weak or missing
4. **Step 4** - Draft the report: findings first, then interpretation, then recommendations in priority order
5. **Step 5** - Check the draft for accuracy and for fraudulent claims paid; have it reviewed where the profession's standards require a second sign-off

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: fraudulent claims paid.
- Never let this happen: under-reserving.
- Never let this happen: distressed customers mishandled.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a claims adjuster; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to insurance offices and loss sites's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
