# Referral & Coordination — Cold Chain Logistics

Refer food, pharmaceutical and healthcare shippers onward and coordinate with other professionals and services. The deliverable is a referral pack and a coordination log with responses tracked.
This skill is written for a cold chain manager working with food, pharmaceutical and healthcare shippers in cold stores, vehicles and distribution hubs.

## When to use
- You are a cold chain manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in cold stores, vehicles and distribution hubs.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a referral pack and a coordination log with responses tracked.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Temperature excursion | time spent outside the permitted temperature range |
| Data logger | a device recording temperature through a journey |
| Validation | proving a process consistently meets requirements |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm the referral is appropriate: what food, pharmaceutical and healthcare shippers need that sits outside the cold chain manager scope in cold stores, vehicles and distribution hubs
2. **Step 2** - Prepare the referral from temperature logs — relevant history, current status, the specific question being asked
3. **Step 3** - With consent, send the referral and log who received it and when; chase non-responses on a set schedule
4. **Step 4** - Share incoming findings back into the main record so the plan and temperature compliance rate stay joined up
5. **Step 5** - Close the loop with food, pharmaceutical and healthcare shippers: explain what happens next, who owns it, and what to do if things worsen — mindful of temperature excursions spoiling goods

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: temperature excursions spoiling goods.
- Never let this happen: equipment failures unnoticed.
- Never let this happen: broken chain of custody.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a cold chain manager; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to cold stores, vehicles and distribution hubs's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
