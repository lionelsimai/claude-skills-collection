# Mentoring & Training Junior Staff — Cold Chain Logistics

Train and mentor junior colleagues toward competent, safe cold chain manager practice. The deliverable is a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
This skill is written for a cold chain manager working with food, pharmaceutical and healthcare shippers in cold stores, vehicles and distribution hubs.

## When to use
- You are a cold chain manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in cold stores, vehicles and distribution hubs.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Temperature excursion | time spent outside the permitted temperature range |
| Data logger | a device recording temperature through a journey |
| Validation | proving a process consistently meets requirements |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Assess the trainee's current level honestly against the competencies required in cold stores, vehicles and distribution hubs
2. **Step 2** - Sequence learning from observation to supervised practice to independent work, using refrigerated vehicles under supervision first
3. **Step 3** - Set explicit sign-off criteria for high-risk tasks — especially anything touching temperature excursions spoiling goods — and never sign off early
4. **Step 4** - Give feedback from direct observation and from temperature logs, specific and timely, with strengths named too
5. **Step 5** - Record progress, keep the training file current, and escalate persistent gaps through supervision, not gossip

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: temperature excursions spoiling goods.
- Never let this happen: equipment failures unnoticed.
- Never let this happen: broken chain of custody.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a cold chain manager; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to cold stores, vehicles and distribution hubs's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
