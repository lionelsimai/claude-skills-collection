# Risk Assessment — Wildlife Management

Identify, score and control the risks in work for conservation bodies, landowners and the public in reserves, estates and rural areas. The deliverable is a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners.
This skill is written for a wildlife manager working with conservation bodies, landowners and the public in reserves, estates and rural areas.

## When to use
- You are a wildlife manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in reserves, estates and rural areas.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Protected species | an animal or plant given legal protection |
| Cull | selective reduction of a population for management reasons |
| Telemetry | tracking animals remotely with transmitters |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Map the activity step by step and list what could go wrong at each stage for conservation bodies, landowners and the public and staff
2. **Step 2** - Score each risk for likelihood and impact, giving special weight to harm to protected species
3. **Step 3** - For every high risk, define a control that removes, reduces or contains it, using trapping and tracking equipment where relevant
4. **Step 4** - Record the assessment in population surveys, assign an owner to each control, and set a review date
5. **Step 5** - Brief everyone involved before work starts; stop and reassess if conditions in reserves, estates and rural areas change

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in reserves, estates and rural areas] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [conservation bodies, landowners and the public and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: harm to protected species.
- Never let this happen: public conflict over control.
- Never let this happen: disease spread.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a wildlife manager; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to reserves, estates and rural areas's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
