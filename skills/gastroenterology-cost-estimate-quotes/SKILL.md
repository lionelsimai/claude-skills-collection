# Cost Estimates & Quotes — Gastroenterology

Build transparent, defensible cost estimates and quotes as a gastroenterologist. The deliverable is an itemised estimate with assumptions, exclusions and validity terms.
This skill is written for a gastroenterologist working with patients with digestive system conditions in an endoscopy unit and gastroenterology clinic.

## When to use
- You are a gastroenterologist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in an endoscopy unit and gastroenterology clinic.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an itemised estimate with assumptions, exclusions and validity terms.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Endoscopy | examining the digestive tract with a flexible camera |
| Cirrhosis | advanced scarring of the liver |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Scope the work precisely from the assessment and endoscopy reports; list what is included and excluded
2. **Step 2** - Cost each element: professional time, endoscopes usage, materials, travel and third-party fees
3. **Step 3** - Add contingency proportionate to uncertainty — and say so openly rather than burying it, which protects against missed gastrointestinal bleeding
4. **Step 4** - Present the quote with options where genuine choices exist, and relate value to outcomes such as endoscopy waiting times
5. **Step 5** - Record acceptance, variations and final costs against the estimate so future quotes get more accurate

2. Detail, following the workflow above

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed gastrointestinal bleeding.
- Never let this happen: sedation complications.
- Never let this happen: delayed cancer pathway.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a gastroenterologist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to an endoscopy unit and gastroenterology clinic's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
