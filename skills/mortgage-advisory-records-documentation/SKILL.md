# Records & Documentation — Mortgage Advisory

Create and maintain accurate, auditable records for work with home buyers and homeowners. The deliverable is a documentation standard, templates and a completed record set.
This skill is written for a mortgage adviser working with home buyers and homeowners in advice firms, branches and online.

## When to use
- You are a mortgage adviser preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in advice firms, branches and online.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a documentation standard, templates and a completed record set.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Affordability | assessment of whether a borrower can sustain repayments |
| Loan to value | the loan as a percentage of the property's value |
| Offer | the lender's formal agreement to lend |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Use fact finds as the single source of truth; record facts, observations and decisions — not speculation
2. **Step 2** - Write entries contemporaneously where possible, dated and attributable, in the format advice firms, branches and online requires
3. **Step 3** - Record measurements and outcomes that feed application-to-offer times exactly as observed, with units and method noted
4. **Step 4** - Store and share records under the confidentiality and retention rules that apply in advice firms, branches and online, guarding against unsuitable product recommendations
5. **Step 5** - Audit a sample of records monthly for completeness and correct anything missing while the work is still current

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: unsuitable product recommendations.
- Never let this happen: affordability miscalculated.
- Never let this happen: deadlines missed losing purchases.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a mortgage adviser; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to advice firms, branches and online's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
