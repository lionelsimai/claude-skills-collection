# Safety Protocol — Anaesthesiology

Work safely in operating theatres and recovery units: a practical safety protocol for a anaesthetist and patients undergoing surgery or painful procedures. The deliverable is a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions.
This skill is written for a anaesthetist working with patients undergoing surgery or painful procedures in operating theatres and recovery units.

## When to use
- You are a anaesthetist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in operating theatres and recovery units.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Airway | the passage through which a patient breathes under anaesthesia |
| Induction | the phase of starting anaesthesia |
| PONV | post-operative nausea and vomiting |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Identify the hazards present in operating theatres and recovery units for staff, patients undergoing surgery or painful procedures and visitors, starting with airway emergencies
2. **Step 2** - Write controls in order: eliminate, substitute, engineer, administrate, then protective equipment such as anaesthetic machines
3. **Step 3** - Set pre-start checks and stop-work authority — anyone can halt work that looks unsafe, without penalty
4. **Step 4** - Define what happens when something goes wrong: first response, escalation, recording in anaesthetic charts
5. **Step 5** - Review the protocol after every incident and at least annually; brief and re-brief everyone it covers

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in operating theatres and recovery units] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients undergoing surgery or painful procedures and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against pre-operative assessment completion.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: airway emergencies.
- Never let this happen: drug errors under pressure.
- Never let this happen: post-operative respiratory depression.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a anaesthetist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to operating theatres and recovery units's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
