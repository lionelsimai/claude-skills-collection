# Metrics & Outcomes Review — Tax Advisory

Track, interpret and act on the outcome metrics that matter for a tax adviser. The deliverable is a metrics dashboard definition and an interpretation/action review.
This skill is written for a tax adviser working with individuals and businesses planning their tax affairs in an accountancy or advisory firm.

## When to use
- You are a tax adviser preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in an accountancy or advisory firm.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a metrics dashboard definition and an interpretation/action review.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Tax relief | a legal reduction in tax payable |
| Enquiry | an investigation by the tax authority into a return |
| Residence | the country where a person is treated as living for tax |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Define each metric precisely — start with filing deadline compliance — including source data in tax computations and collection method
2. **Step 2** - Set review cadence and comparison baselines so trends, not single data points, drive decisions
3. **Step 3** - Investigate movements before celebrating or panicking: check data quality, case mix and one-off causes
4. **Step 4** - Link metric movements to practice changes and to risks such as missed filing deadlines; test one change at a time where possible
5. **Step 5** - Report results honestly to stakeholders in an accountancy or advisory firm, including where outcomes fell short and what changes

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed filing deadlines.
- Never let this happen: aggressive schemes challenged.
- Never let this happen: relief claims overlooked.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a tax adviser; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to an accountancy or advisory firm's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
