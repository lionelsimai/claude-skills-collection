# Cost Estimates & Quotes — Pharmacy Practice

Build transparent, defensible cost estimates and quotes as a pharmacy technician. The deliverable is an itemised estimate with assumptions, exclusions and validity terms.
This skill is written for a pharmacy technician working with patients and customers collecting medicines and advice in a community pharmacy.

## When to use
- You are a pharmacy technician preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a community pharmacy.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an itemised estimate with assumptions, exclusions and validity terms.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Dispensing | preparing and supplying medicines against a prescription |
| Controlled drug | a medicine subject to strict legal controls |
| SOP | standard operating procedure |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Scope the work precisely from the assessment and dispensing records; list what is included and excluded
2. **Step 2** - Cost each element: professional time, dispensing systems usage, materials, travel and third-party fees
3. **Step 3** - Add contingency proportionate to uncertainty — and say so openly rather than burying it, which protects against dispensing errors
4. **Step 4** - Present the quote with options where genuine choices exist, and relate value to outcomes such as dispensing accuracy rate
5. **Step 5** - Record acceptance, variations and final costs against the estimate so future quotes get more accurate

1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: dispensing errors.
- Never let this happen: controlled drug discrepancies.
- Never let this happen: expired stock supplied.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a pharmacy technician; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a community pharmacy's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
