# Metrics & Outcomes Review — Landscape Architecture

Track, interpret and act on the outcome metrics that matter for a landscape architect. The deliverable is a metrics dashboard definition and an interpretation/action review.
This skill is written for a landscape architect working with developers, councils and private clients in design studios and outdoor sites.

## When to use
- You are a landscape architect preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in design studios and outdoor sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a metrics dashboard definition and an interpretation/action review.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Planting plan | the layout and specification of plants for a site |
| SuDS | sustainable drainage systems managing rainwater naturally |
| Hard landscape | the built elements of a landscape, such as paving |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Define each metric precisely — start with design approval rates — including source data in design concepts and collection method
2. **Step 2** - Set review cadence and comparison baselines so trends, not single data points, drive decisions
3. **Step 3** - Investigate movements before celebrating or panicking: check data quality, case mix and one-off causes
4. **Step 4** - Link metric movements to practice changes and to risks such as planting failures; test one change at a time where possible
5. **Step 5** - Report results honestly to stakeholders in design studios and outdoor sites, including where outcomes fell short and what changes

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: planting failures.
- Never let this happen: drainage problems overlooked.
- Never let this happen: inaccessible designs.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a landscape architect; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to design studios and outdoor sites's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
