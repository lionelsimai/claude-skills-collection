# Scheduling & Workload Management — Emergency Medicine

Plan schedules, caseloads and turnaround times for a emergency physician in a hospital emergency department. The deliverable is a workable schedule template with priorities, buffers and escalation rules.
This skill is written for a emergency physician working with patients arriving with acute illness or injury in a hospital emergency department.

## When to use
- You are a emergency physician preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a hospital emergency department.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a workable schedule template with priorities, buffers and escalation rules.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Triage | sorting patients by urgency of need |
| Resuscitation | emergency treatment to restore circulation or breathing |
| Sepsis | a life-threatening response to infection |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Map demand: appointment or job types, durations, and the deadlines that drive triage-to-clinician time
2. **Step 2** - Build the schedule around fixed commitments in a hospital emergency department first, then fit flexible work around them
3. **Step 3** - Protect buffer time for overruns, urgent cases and the admin behind triage records — schedules without buffers fail by week two
4. **Step 4** - Define triage rules so urgent work (for example anything involving missed time-critical diagnoses) displaces routine work predictably
5. **Step 5** - Review the schedule weekly against actuals and adjust booking rules, staffing or defibrillators availability

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a hospital emergency department] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients arriving with acute illness or injury and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a workable schedule template with priorities, buffers and escalation rules, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in triage records.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against triage-to-clinician time.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in a hospital emergency department.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed time-critical diagnoses.
- Never let this happen: triage under-scoring.
- Never let this happen: crowding-related delays.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a emergency physician; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a hospital emergency department's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
