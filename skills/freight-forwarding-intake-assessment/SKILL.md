# Intake & Initial Assessment — Freight Forwarding

Run a structured intake and initial assessment for importers, exporters and carriers in freight offices, ports and airports. The deliverable is a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
This skill is written for a freight forwarder working with importers, exporters and carriers in freight offices, ports and airports.

## When to use
- You are a freight forwarder preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in freight offices, ports and airports.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm identity, consent and the reason for referral or engagement, and open the record that will hold shipping documents
2. **Step 2** - Gather history and current circumstances from importers, exporters and carriers, using the standard intake structure for freight offices, ports and airports
3. **Step 3** - Record baseline observations and measurements relevant to shipment on-time rate, noting anything that changes the priority of the case
4. **Step 4** - Flag immediate concerns — especially customs delays from bad documents — and escalate or fast-track anything urgent before proceeding
5. **Step 5** - Summarise findings for importers, exporters and carriers in plain language, agree the next step, and file the intake section of shipping documents

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: customs delays from bad documents.
- Never let this happen: cargo damage.
- Never let this happen: missed vessel cut-offs.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a freight forwarder; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to freight offices, ports and airports's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
