# Metrics & Outcomes Review — Livestock Farming

Track, interpret and act on the outcome metrics that matter for a livestock farmer. The deliverable is a metrics dashboard definition and an interpretation/action review.
This skill is written for a livestock farmer working with meat, milk and egg buyers and the food chain in farms and livestock units.

## When to use
- You are a livestock farmer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in farms and livestock units.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a metrics dashboard definition and an interpretation/action review.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Biosecurity | measures preventing disease entering or spreading on a farm |
| Feed conversion | how efficiently feed becomes meat, milk or eggs |
| Husbandry | the care and management of farm animals |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Define each metric precisely — start with animal health scores — including source data in animal health records and collection method
2. **Step 2** - Set review cadence and comparison baselines so trends, not single data points, drive decisions
3. **Step 3** - Investigate movements before celebrating or panicking: check data quality, case mix and one-off causes
4. **Step 4** - Link metric movements to practice changes and to risks such as disease outbreaks; test one change at a time where possible
5. **Step 5** - Report results honestly to stakeholders in farms and livestock units, including where outcomes fell short and what changes

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: disease outbreaks.
- Never let this happen: animal welfare failures.
- Never let this happen: medicine record errors.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a livestock farmer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to farms and livestock units's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
