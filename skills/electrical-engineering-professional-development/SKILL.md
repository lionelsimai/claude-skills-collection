# Continuing Professional Development Plan — Electrical Engineering

Plan and evidence continuing professional development for a practising electrical engineer. The deliverable is a CPD plan mapped to competency gaps, with evidence and reflection records.
This skill is written for a electrical engineer working with energy, manufacturing and building clients in design offices, plants and substations.

## When to use
- You are a electrical engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in design offices, plants and substations.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a CPD plan mapped to competency gaps, with evidence and reflection records.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Load | the power drawn by a system or device |
| Protection relay | a device that detects faults and isolates circuits |
| Earthing | connecting equipment to earth for safety |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Audit current competence against the standards for design offices, plants and substations and the evidence in your recent circuit designs
2. **Step 2** - Choose development priorities that close real gaps — including staying current on electric shock risks — not just easy hours
3. **Step 3** - Mix formats: courses, supervised practice, case review, reading and teaching others
4. **Step 4** - After each activity, write a short reflection: what changed in your practice and what it means for test pass rates
5. **Step 5** - Keep the CPD record inspection-ready and review the plan at appraisal or revalidation time

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in design offices, plants and substations] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [energy, manufacturing and building clients and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a CPD plan mapped to competency gaps, with evidence and reflection records, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: electric shock risks.
- Never let this happen: protection failures.
- Never let this happen: overheating equipment.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a electrical engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to design offices, plants and substations's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
