# Emergency Response Procedure — Air Traffic Control

Respond to emergencies in control towers and radar centres calmly, in role and in the right order. The deliverable is an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
This skill is written for a air traffic controller working with pilots, airlines and airport operators in control towers and radar centres.

## When to use
- You are a air traffic controller preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in control towers and radar centres.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Separation | the safe distance maintained between aircraft |
| Handover | transferring responsibility for traffic between controllers |
| Squawk | the transponder code identifying an aircraft on radar |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Recognise the emergency early: know the presentations that matter for pilots, airlines and airport operators, especially loss of separation
2. **Step 2** - Take the immediate actions a air traffic controller is trained and authorised for, using radar systems as drilled
3. **Step 3** - Call for help with a structured message: location in control towers and radar centres, situation, what has been done, what is needed
4. **Step 4** - Keep pilots, airlines and airport operators and bystanders informed and safe while help arrives; hand over with a concise summary
5. **Step 5** - Record the event fully in traffic logs afterwards, debrief the team, and update the procedure with lessons learned

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in control towers and radar centres] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [pilots, airlines and airport operators and other stakeholders]

The finished output is an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: loss of separation.
- Never let this happen: communication misunderstandings.
- Never let this happen: fatigue on shift.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a air traffic controller; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to control towers and radar centres's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
