# Cost Estimates & Quotes — Freight Forwarding

Build transparent, defensible cost estimates and quotes as a freight forwarder. The deliverable is an itemised estimate with assumptions, exclusions and validity terms.
This skill is written for a freight forwarder working with importers, exporters and carriers in freight offices, ports and airports.

## When to use
- You are a freight forwarder preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in freight offices, ports and airports.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an itemised estimate with assumptions, exclusions and validity terms.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Bill of lading | the transport document evidencing the carriage contract |
| Cut-off | the deadline for delivering cargo for a sailing or flight |
| Demurrage | charges for keeping containers beyond free time |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Scope the work precisely from the assessment and shipping documents; list what is included and excluded
2. **Step 2** - Cost each element: professional time, freight management systems usage, materials, travel and third-party fees
3. **Step 3** - Add contingency proportionate to uncertainty — and say so openly rather than burying it, which protects against customs delays from bad documents
4. **Step 4** - Present the quote with options where genuine choices exist, and relate value to outcomes such as shipment on-time rate
5. **Step 5** - Record acceptance, variations and final costs against the estimate so future quotes get more accurate

- Never let this happen: customs delays from bad documents.
- Never let this happen: cargo damage.
- Never let this happen: missed vessel cut-offs.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a freight forwarder; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to freight offices, ports and airports's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
