# Safety Protocol — Sustainability Management

Work safely in organisations across all sectors: a practical safety protocol for a sustainability manager and businesses, investors and regulators. The deliverable is a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions.
This skill is written for a sustainability manager working with businesses, investors and regulators in organisations across all sectors.

## When to use
- You are a sustainability manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in organisations across all sectors.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

| Term | Meaning |

| Scope 3 | indirect emissions in a value chain |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Identify the hazards present in organisations across all sectors for staff, businesses, investors and regulators and visitors, starting with greenwashing accusations
2. **Step 2** - Write controls in order: eliminate, substitute, engineer, administrate, then protective equipment such as carbon accounting software
3. **Step 3** - Set pre-start checks and stop-work authority — anyone can halt work that looks unsafe, without penalty
4. **Step 4** - Define what happens when something goes wrong: first response, escalation, recording in carbon inventories
5. **Step 5** - Review the protocol after every incident and at least annually; brief and re-brief everyone it covers

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: greenwashing accusations.
- Never let this happen: data gaps in reporting.
- Never let this happen: missed regulatory deadlines.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a sustainability manager; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to organisations across all sectors's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
