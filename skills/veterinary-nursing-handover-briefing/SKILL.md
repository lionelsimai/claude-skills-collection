# Handover Briefing — Veterinary Nursing

Hand over animals in care and their owners or active work to another professional without losing critical detail. The deliverable is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
This skill is written for a veterinary nurse working with animals in care and their owners in a veterinary practice.

## When to use
- You are a veterinary nurse preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a veterinary practice.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Hospitalisation chart | a record of an inpatient animal's care and observations |
| Analgesia | pain relief |
| Triage | prioritising patients by urgency in practice |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Summarise the current position from nursing care plans: what is done, what is in progress, what is waiting
2. **Step 2** - Highlight live risks first — unrecognised pain in animals — and the controls or watchpoints already in place
3. **Step 3** - State the next actions with owners and deadlines, and the results expected for patient monitoring compliance
4. **Step 4** - Walk the receiving professional through the brief, answer questions, and confirm understanding
5. **Step 5** - Record that handover happened, who accepted it, and where the full record lives in a veterinary practice

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a veterinary practice] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [animals in care and their owners and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in nursing care plans.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against patient monitoring compliance.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in a veterinary practice.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: unrecognised pain in animals.
- Never let this happen: fluid therapy errors.
- Never let this happen: escape or injury in kennels.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a veterinary nurse; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a veterinary practice's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
