# Records & Documentation — Conservation Architecture

Create and maintain accurate, auditable records for work with heritage bodies, owners of historic buildings and councils. The deliverable is a documentation standard, templates and a completed record set.
This skill is written for a conservation architect working with heritage bodies, owners of historic buildings and councils in historic buildings and design studios.

## When to use
- You are a conservation architect preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in historic buildings and design studios.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a documentation standard, templates and a completed record set.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Listed building | a building protected by law for its heritage value |

| Reversibility | repairs that can be undone without harming original material |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Use condition surveys as the single source of truth; record facts, observations and decisions — not speculation
2. **Step 2** - Write entries contemporaneously where possible, dated and attributable, in the format historic buildings and design studios requires
3. **Step 3** - Record measurements and outcomes that feed survey turnaround exactly as observed, with units and method noted
4. **Step 4** - Store and share records under the confidentiality and retention rules that apply in historic buildings and design studios, guarding against inappropriate repairs damaging fabric
5. **Step 5** - Audit a sample of records monthly for completeness and correct anything missing while the work is still current

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: inappropriate repairs damaging fabric.
- Never let this happen: consent refusals.
- Never let this happen: hidden defects.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a conservation architect; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to historic buildings and design studios's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
