# Emergency Response Procedure — Psychiatry

Respond to emergencies in a mental health unit and community mental health team base calmly, in role and in the right order. The deliverable is an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
This skill is written for a psychiatrist working with people experiencing mental illness and psychological distress in a mental health unit and community mental health team base.

## When to use
- You are a psychiatrist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a mental health unit and community mental health team base.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Recognise the emergency early: know the presentations that matter for people experiencing mental illness and psychological distress, especially suicide risk not recognised
2. **Step 2** - Take the immediate actions a psychiatrist is trained and authorised for, using observation level charts as drilled
3. **Step 3** - Call for help with a structured message: location in a mental health unit and community mental health team base, situation, what has been done, what is needed
4. **Step 4** - Keep people experiencing mental illness and psychological distress and bystanders informed and safe while help arrives; hand over with a concise summary
5. **Step 5** - Record the event fully in psychiatric assessments afterwards, debrief the team, and update the procedure with lessons learned

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a mental health unit and community mental health team base] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [people experiencing mental illness and psychological distress and other stakeholders]

The finished output is an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: suicide risk not recognised.
- Never let this happen: absconding from the unit.
- Never let this happen: medication side-effects unmanaged.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a psychiatrist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a mental health unit and community mental health team base's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
