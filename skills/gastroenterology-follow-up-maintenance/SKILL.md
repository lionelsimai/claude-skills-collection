# Follow-Up & Maintenance Schedule — Gastroenterology

Plan follow-up and maintenance so gains for patients with digestive system conditions are checked and sustained. The deliverable is a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers.
This skill is written for a gastroenterologist working with patients with digestive system conditions in an endoscopy unit and gastroenterology clinic.

## When to use
- You are a gastroenterologist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in an endoscopy unit and gastroenterology clinic.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Endoscopy | examining the digestive tract with a flexible camera |
| Cirrhosis | advanced scarring of the liver |
| IBD | inflammatory bowel disease, mainly Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Set follow-up intervals from the plan and from how endoscopy waiting times is expected to move over time
2. **Step 2** - Define what is checked at each contact, using endoscopes where measurement is needed, and record it in endoscopy reports
3. **Step 3** - Give patients with digestive system conditions clear guidance for the interval: what to do, what to watch for, when to make contact sooner
4. **Step 4** - Act early on warning signs — deterioration linked to missed gastrointestinal bleeding shortens the interval, never lengthens it
5. **Step 5** - Close the follow-up cycle formally when goals are met, with a summary and re-entry route if needs return

## Output Format

The finished output is a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed gastrointestinal bleeding.
- Never let this happen: sedation complications.
- Never let this happen: delayed cancer pathway.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a gastroenterologist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to an endoscopy unit and gastroenterology clinic's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
