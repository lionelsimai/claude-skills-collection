# Records & Documentation — Forensic Accounting

Create and maintain accurate, auditable records for work with courts, insurers and businesses investigating financial wrongdoing. The deliverable is a documentation standard, templates and a completed record set.
This skill is written for a forensic accountant working with courts, insurers and businesses investigating financial wrongdoing in an accountancy firm and legal proceedings.

## When to use
- You are a forensic accountant preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in an accountancy firm and legal proceedings.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a documentation standard, templates and a completed record set.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Use investigation reports as the single source of truth; record facts, observations and decisions — not speculation
2. **Step 2** - Write entries contemporaneously where possible, dated and attributable, in the format an accountancy firm and legal proceedings requires
3. **Step 3** - Record measurements and outcomes that feed investigation turnaround exactly as observed, with units and method noted
4. **Step 4** - Store and share records under the confidentiality and retention rules that apply in an accountancy firm and legal proceedings, guarding against evidence contamination
5. **Step 5** - Audit a sample of records monthly for completeness and correct anything missing while the work is still current

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: evidence contamination.
- Never let this happen: assumptions challenged in court.
- Never let this happen: conflicts of interest.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a forensic accountant; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to an accountancy firm and legal proceedings's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
