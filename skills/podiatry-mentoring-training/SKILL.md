# Mentoring & Training Junior Staff — Podiatry

Train and mentor junior colleagues toward competent, safe podiatrist practice. The deliverable is a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
This skill is written for a podiatrist working with patients with foot and lower limb conditions in a podiatry clinic and community settings.

## When to use
- You are a podiatrist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a podiatry clinic and community settings.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Ulcer | an open sore that is slow to heal |
| Biomechanics | how the body moves and bears load |
| Neuropathy | nerve damage reducing sensation, common in diabetes |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Assess the trainee's current level honestly against the competencies required in a podiatry clinic and community settings
2. **Step 2** - Sequence learning from observation to supervised practice to independent work, using surgical instruments under supervision first
3. **Step 3** - Set explicit sign-off criteria for high-risk tasks — especially anything touching diabetic foot ulcers missed — and never sign off early
4. **Step 4** - Give feedback from direct observation and from foot assessments, specific and timely, with strengths named too
5. **Step 5** - Record progress, keep the training file current, and escalate persistent gaps through supervision, not gossip

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a podiatry clinic and community settings] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients with foot and lower limb conditions and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in foot assessments.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against ulcer healing rates.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in a podiatry clinic and community settings.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: diabetic foot ulcers missed.
- Never let this happen: infection after nail surgery.
- Never let this happen: vascular problems unrecognised.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a podiatrist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a podiatry clinic and community settings's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
