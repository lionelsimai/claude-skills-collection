# Formal Report Writing — Chemical Engineering

Write formal reports as a chemical engineer that stand up to scrutiny from any reader. The deliverable is a report template with structure, evidence standards and review steps.
This skill is written for a chemical engineer working with process plants and manufacturing clients in chemical plants and design offices.

## When to use
- You are a chemical engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in chemical plants and design offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a report template with structure, evidence standards and review steps.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| HAZOP | a systematic study of hazards and operability problems |
| Yield | the amount of desired product obtained from a process |
| Mass balance | accounting for all material entering and leaving a process |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Establish the report's purpose, audience and deadline before writing; gather source material from process flow diagrams
2. **Step 2** - Structure: summary, background, method, findings, analysis, recommendations — in that order
3. **Step 3** - Write findings factually and separately from opinion; label professional judgement as judgement
4. **Step 4** - Support key points with evidence and with plant uptime where outcomes are claimed; acknowledge limitations openly
5. **Step 5** - Proofread for accuracy and tone, check names, dates and figures twice, and consider process safety incidents before release

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: process safety incidents.
- Never let this happen: runaway reactions.
- Never let this happen: environmental releases.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a chemical engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to chemical plants and design offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
