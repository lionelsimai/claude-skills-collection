# Quality Audit — Construction Site Management

Audit the quality of site manager work in construction sites and drive measurable improvement. The deliverable is an audit report with scores, evidence and an improvement plan.
This skill is written for a site manager working with clients, contractors and construction teams in construction sites.

## When to use
- You are a site manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in construction sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an audit report with scores, evidence and an improvement plan.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Programme | the schedule of construction activities |
| Snagging list | defects to fix before handover |
| Toolbox talk | a short safety briefing for site workers |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Define the audit sample and criteria up front, anchored to programme adherence and the standards for construction sites
2. **Step 2** - Review a sample of site diaries and observe practice where permitted, scoring consistently
3. **Step 3** - Separate isolated errors from patterns — patterns point to system fixes, not individual blame
4. **Step 4** - Report findings with evidence, and agree improvement actions that address the root causes, including site accidents
5. **Step 5** - Re-audit after the agreed interval and publish the change in scores so improvement is visible

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: site accidents.
- Never let this happen: programme slippage hidden.
- Never let this happen: quality defects at handover.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a site manager; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to construction sites's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
