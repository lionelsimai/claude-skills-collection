# Tools & Equipment Checklist — Geological Surveying

Select, check and maintain the tools and equipment a engineering geologist and surveyor relies on. The deliverable is an equipment checklist with inspection points, maintenance dates and replacement triggers.
This skill is written for a engineering geologist and surveyor working with mining, construction and environmental clients in field sites and laboratories.

## When to use
- You are a engineering geologist and surveyor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in field sites and laboratories.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an equipment checklist with inspection points, maintenance dates and replacement triggers.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Core sample | a cylinder of rock extracted by drilling |
| Overburden | material lying above the resource of interest |
| Fault | a fracture in rock along which movement has occurred |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - List the essential kit for work in field sites and laboratories, centred on drilling equipment, with what each item is used for
2. **Step 2** - Define pre-use checks: condition, calibration or charge status, cleanliness and safety features
3. **Step 3** - Set maintenance and replacement intervals based on usage and manufacturer guidance, and log them against survey data
4. **Step 4** - Quarantine faulty items immediately — faulty kit is a direct route to field safety incidents
5. **Step 5** - Review the kit list quarterly against the work actually done and retire or add items deliberately

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: field safety incidents.
- Never let this happen: sample mix-ups.
- Never let this happen: misinterpreted ground conditions.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a engineering geologist and surveyor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to field sites and laboratories's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
