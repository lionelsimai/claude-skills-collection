# Intake & Initial Assessment — Cardiology

Run a structured intake and initial assessment for patients with heart and circulation conditions in a hospital cardiology department and outpatient clinic. The deliverable is a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
This skill is written for a cardiologist working with patients with heart and circulation conditions in a hospital cardiology department and outpatient clinic.

## When to use
- You are a cardiologist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a hospital cardiology department and outpatient clinic.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| ECG | a tracing of the heart's electrical activity |
| Ejection fraction | the percentage of blood the left ventricle pumps out each beat |
| Angioplasty | a procedure to widen narrowed coronary arteries |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm identity, consent and the reason for referral or engagement, and open the record that will hold ECG reports
2. **Step 2** - Gather history and current circumstances from patients with heart and circulation conditions, using the standard intake structure for a hospital cardiology department and outpatient clinic
3. **Step 3** - Record baseline observations and measurements relevant to chest-pain triage time, noting anything that changes the priority of the case
4. **Step 4** - Flag immediate concerns — especially missed myocardial infarction — and escalate or fast-track anything urgent before proceeding
5. **Step 5** - Summarise findings for patients with heart and circulation conditions in plain language, agree the next step, and file the intake section of ECG reports

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed myocardial infarction.
- Never let this happen: anticoagulant dosing errors.
- Never let this happen: contrast-induced kidney injury.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a cardiologist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a hospital cardiology department and outpatient clinic's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
