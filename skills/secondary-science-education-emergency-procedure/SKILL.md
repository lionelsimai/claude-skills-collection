# Emergency Response Procedure — Secondary Science Education

Respond to emergencies in a school science department and laboratories calmly, in role and in the right order. The deliverable is an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
This skill is written for a secondary science teacher working with secondary school students in a school science department and laboratories.

## When to use
- You are a secondary science teacher preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a school science department and laboratories.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Recognise the emergency early: know the presentations that matter for secondary school students, especially practical safety incidents
2. **Step 2** - Take the immediate actions a secondary science teacher is trained and authorised for, using laboratory equipment as drilled
3. **Step 3** - Call for help with a structured message: location in a school science department and laboratories, situation, what has been done, what is needed
4. **Step 4** - Keep secondary school students and bystanders informed and safe while help arrives; hand over with a concise summary
5. **Step 5** - Record the event fully in schemes of work afterwards, debrief the team, and update the procedure with lessons learned

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: practical safety incidents.
- Never let this happen: misconceptions in core concepts.
- Never let this happen: equipment faults.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a secondary science teacher; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a school science department and laboratories's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
