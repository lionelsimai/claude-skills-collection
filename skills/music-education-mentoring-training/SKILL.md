# Mentoring & Training Junior Staff — Music Education

Train and mentor junior colleagues toward competent, safe music teacher practice. The deliverable is a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
This skill is written for a music teacher working with music students of all ages and their parents in music services, schools and private studios.

## When to use
- You are a music teacher preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in music services, schools and private studios.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Sight-reading | playing music at first sight without rehearsal |
| Ensemble | a group of musicians performing together |
| Graded exams | staged practical examinations in instrumental performance |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Assess the trainee's current level honestly against the competencies required in music services, schools and private studios
2. **Step 2** - Sequence learning from observation to supervised practice to independent work, using instruments under supervision first
3. **Step 3** - Set explicit sign-off criteria for high-risk tasks — especially anything touching hearing damage from loud practice — and never sign off early
4. **Step 4** - Give feedback from direct observation and from lesson plans, specific and timely, with strengths named too
5. **Step 5** - Record progress, keep the training file current, and escalate persistent gaps through supervision, not gossip

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: hearing damage from loud practice.
- Never let this happen: exam entry errors.
- Never let this happen: safeguarding at one-to-one lessons.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a music teacher; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to music services, schools and private studios's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
