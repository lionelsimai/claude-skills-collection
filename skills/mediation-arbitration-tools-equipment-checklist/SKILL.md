# Tools & Equipment Checklist — Mediation & Arbitration

Select, check and maintain the tools and equipment a mediator and arbitrator relies on. The deliverable is an equipment checklist with inspection points, maintenance dates and replacement triggers.
This skill is written for a mediator and arbitrator working with parties in dispute and their representatives in mediation venues and hearing rooms.

## When to use
- You are a mediator and arbitrator preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in mediation venues and hearing rooms.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an equipment checklist with inspection points, maintenance dates and replacement triggers.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Mediation | a voluntary process where a neutral helps parties settle |
| Arbitration | private dispute resolution producing a binding award |
| Without prejudice | communications that cannot be used in court if settlement fails |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - List the essential kit for work in mediation venues and hearing rooms, centred on hearing platforms, with what each item is used for
2. **Step 2** - Define pre-use checks: condition, calibration or charge status, cleanliness and safety features
3. **Step 3** - Set maintenance and replacement intervals based on usage and manufacturer guidance, and log them against mediation agreements
4. **Step 4** - Quarantine faulty items immediately — faulty kit is a direct route to bias perceived or actual
5. **Step 5** - Review the kit list quarterly against the work actually done and retire or add items deliberately

## Output Format

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: bias perceived or actual.
- Never let this happen: settlement terms unenforceable.
- Never let this happen: confidentiality breached.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a mediator and arbitrator; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to mediation venues and hearing rooms's rules.
