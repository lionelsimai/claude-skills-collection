# Referral & Coordination — Orthopaedics

Refer patients with bone, joint and muscle injuries or conditions onward and coordinate with other professionals and services. The deliverable is a referral pack and a coordination log with responses tracked.
This skill is written for a orthopaedic surgeon working with patients with bone, joint and muscle injuries or conditions in an orthopaedic theatre and fracture clinic.

## When to use
- You are a orthopaedic surgeon preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in an orthopaedic theatre and fracture clinic.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a referral pack and a coordination log with responses tracked.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm the referral is appropriate: what patients with bone, joint and muscle injuries or conditions need that sits outside the orthopaedic surgeon scope in an orthopaedic theatre and fracture clinic
2. **Step 2** - Prepare the referral from operation notes — relevant history, current status, the specific question being asked
3. **Step 3** - With consent, send the referral and log who received it and when; chase non-responses on a set schedule
4. **Step 4** - Share incoming findings back into the main record so the plan and surgical site infection rate stay joined up
5. **Step 5** - Close the loop with patients with bone, joint and muscle injuries or conditions: explain what happens next, who owns it, and what to do if things worsen — mindful of wrong-site surgery

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: wrong-site surgery.
- Never let this happen: compartment syndrome missed.
- Never let this happen: post-operative infection.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a orthopaedic surgeon; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to an orthopaedic theatre and fracture clinic's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
