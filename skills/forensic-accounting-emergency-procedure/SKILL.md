# Emergency Response Procedure — Forensic Accounting

Respond to emergencies in an accountancy firm and legal proceedings calmly, in role and in the right order. The deliverable is an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
This skill is written for a forensic accountant working with courts, insurers and businesses investigating financial wrongdoing in an accountancy firm and legal proceedings.

## When to use
- You are a forensic accountant preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in an accountancy firm and legal proceedings.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Recognise the emergency early: know the presentations that matter for courts, insurers and businesses investigating financial wrongdoing, especially evidence contamination
2. **Step 2** - Take the immediate actions a forensic accountant is trained and authorised for, using data analysis software as drilled
3. **Step 3** - Call for help with a structured message: location in an accountancy firm and legal proceedings, situation, what has been done, what is needed
4. **Step 4** - Keep courts, insurers and businesses investigating financial wrongdoing and bystanders informed and safe while help arrives; hand over with a concise summary
5. **Step 5** - Record the event fully in investigation reports afterwards, debrief the team, and update the procedure with lessons learned

## Output Format

2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: evidence contamination.
- Never let this happen: assumptions challenged in court.
- Never let this happen: conflicts of interest.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a forensic accountant; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to an accountancy firm and legal proceedings's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
