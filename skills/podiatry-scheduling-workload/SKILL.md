# Scheduling & Workload Management — Podiatry

Plan schedules, caseloads and turnaround times for a podiatrist in a podiatry clinic and community settings. The deliverable is a workable schedule template with priorities, buffers and escalation rules.
This skill is written for a podiatrist working with patients with foot and lower limb conditions in a podiatry clinic and community settings.

## When to use
- You are a podiatrist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a podiatry clinic and community settings.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a workable schedule template with priorities, buffers and escalation rules.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Ulcer | an open sore that is slow to heal |
| Biomechanics | how the body moves and bears load |
| Neuropathy | nerve damage reducing sensation, common in diabetes |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Map demand: appointment or job types, durations, and the deadlines that drive ulcer healing rates
2. **Step 2** - Build the schedule around fixed commitments in a podiatry clinic and community settings first, then fit flexible work around them
3. **Step 3** - Protect buffer time for overruns, urgent cases and the admin behind foot assessments — schedules without buffers fail by week two
4. **Step 4** - Define triage rules so urgent work (for example anything involving diabetic foot ulcers missed) displaces routine work predictably
5. **Step 5** - Review the schedule weekly against actuals and adjust booking rules, staffing or surgical instruments availability

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: diabetic foot ulcers missed.
- Never let this happen: infection after nail surgery.
- Never let this happen: vascular problems unrecognised.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a podiatrist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a podiatry clinic and community settings's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
