# Session Delivery Guide — Podiatry

Prepare for and deliver a professional session or appointment for patients with foot and lower limb conditions in a podiatry clinic and community settings. The deliverable is a session plan, delivery checklist and post-session record.
This skill is written for a podiatrist working with patients with foot and lower limb conditions in a podiatry clinic and community settings.

## When to use
- You are a podiatrist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a podiatry clinic and community settings.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a session plan, delivery checklist and post-session record.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Ulcer | an open sore that is slow to heal |
| Biomechanics | how the body moves and bears load |
| Neuropathy | nerve damage reducing sensation, common in diabetes |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Review foot assessments before the session so you know the history, goals and any cautions such as diabetic foot ulcers missed
2. **Step 2** - Prepare the space and surgical instruments; check everything works and is safe before patients with foot and lower limb conditions arrive
3. **Step 3** - Open the session by agreeing the focus and what a good outcome looks like, linked to ulcer healing rates
4. **Step 4** - Deliver the session, adapting in the moment while staying inside the agreed plan and professional scope
5. **Step 5** - Close by summarising what was covered, confirming follow-ups, and completing the session record the same day

## Output Format

1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: diabetic foot ulcers missed.
- Never let this happen: infection after nail surgery.
- Never let this happen: vascular problems unrecognised.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a podiatrist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a podiatry clinic and community settings's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
