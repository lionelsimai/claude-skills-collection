# Mentoring & Training Junior Staff — Port Operations

Train and mentor junior colleagues toward competent, safe port operations manager practice. The deliverable is a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
This skill is written for a port operations manager working with shipping lines, hauliers and cargo owners in ports and container terminals.

## When to use
- You are a port operations manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in ports and container terminals.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Berth | the place where a vessel moors to load or unload |
| TEU | a standard unit equal to a twenty-foot container |
| Turnaround | the time a vessel or truck spends in port |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Assess the trainee's current level honestly against the competencies required in ports and container terminals
2. **Step 2** - Sequence learning from observation to supervised practice to independent work, using terminal operating systems under supervision first
3. **Step 3** - Set explicit sign-off criteria for high-risk tasks — especially anything touching crane accidents — and never sign off early
4. **Step 4** - Give feedback from direct observation and from berth plans, specific and timely, with strengths named too
5. **Step 5** - Record progress, keep the training file current, and escalate persistent gaps through supervision, not gossip

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in ports and container terminals] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [shipping lines, hauliers and cargo owners and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against berth utilisation.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: crane accidents.
- Never let this happen: cargo damage.
- Never let this happen: berth congestion.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a port operations manager; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to ports and container terminals's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
