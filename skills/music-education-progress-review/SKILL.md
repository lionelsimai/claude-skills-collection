# Progress Review & Adjustment — Music Education

Review progress for music students of all ages and their parents against their plan and adjust it with evidence. The deliverable is a progress review summary with decisions and an updated plan.
This skill is written for a music teacher working with music students of all ages and their parents in music services, schools and private studios.

## When to use
- You are a music teacher preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in music services, schools and private studios.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a progress review summary with decisions and an updated plan.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Sight-reading | playing music at first sight without rehearsal |
| Ensemble | a group of musicians performing together |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Pull together lesson plans and the latest results for examination results since the last review
2. **Step 2** - Compare actual progress with the plan's goals and timelines; identify what moved, what stalled and why
3. **Step 3** - Discuss the evidence with music students of all ages and their parents honestly, including where the original plan no longer fits
4. **Step 4** - Agree adjustments: continue, intensify, change approach or close — and record the reasoning
5. **Step 5** - Update the plan and review date, and communicate changes to everyone involved in music services, schools and private studios

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: hearing damage from loud practice.
- Never let this happen: exam entry errors.
- Never let this happen: safeguarding at one-to-one lessons.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a music teacher; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to music services, schools and private studios's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
