# Records & Documentation — Freight Forwarding

Create and maintain accurate, auditable records for work with importers, exporters and carriers. The deliverable is a documentation standard, templates and a completed record set.
This skill is written for a freight forwarder working with importers, exporters and carriers in freight offices, ports and airports.

## When to use
- You are a freight forwarder preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in freight offices, ports and airports.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a documentation standard, templates and a completed record set.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Bill of lading | the transport document evidencing the carriage contract |
| Cut-off | the deadline for delivering cargo for a sailing or flight |
| Demurrage | charges for keeping containers beyond free time |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Use shipping documents as the single source of truth; record facts, observations and decisions — not speculation
2. **Step 2** - Write entries contemporaneously where possible, dated and attributable, in the format freight offices, ports and airports requires
3. **Step 3** - Record measurements and outcomes that feed shipment on-time rate exactly as observed, with units and method noted
4. **Step 4** - Store and share records under the confidentiality and retention rules that apply in freight offices, ports and airports, guarding against customs delays from bad documents
5. **Step 5** - Audit a sample of records monthly for completeness and correct anything missing while the work is still current

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: customs delays from bad documents.
- Never let this happen: cargo damage.
- Never let this happen: missed vessel cut-offs.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a freight forwarder; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to freight offices, ports and airports's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
