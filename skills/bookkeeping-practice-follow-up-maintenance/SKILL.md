# Follow-Up & Maintenance Schedule — Bookkeeping Practice

Plan follow-up and maintenance so gains for small businesses, sole traders and accountants are checked and sustained. The deliverable is a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers.
This skill is written for a bookkeeper working with small businesses, sole traders and accountants in a bookkeeping practice and client premises.

## When to use
- You are a bookkeeper preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a bookkeeping practice and client premises.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Reconciliation | matching records against bank statements to find differences |
| Ledger | the record of transactions by category |
| Accruals | recognising income or costs in the period they relate to |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Set follow-up intervals from the plan and from how reconciliation timeliness is expected to move over time
2. **Step 2** - Define what is checked at each contact, using cloud accounting software where measurement is needed, and record it in bank reconciliations
3. **Step 3** - Give small businesses, sole traders and accountants clear guidance for the interval: what to do, what to watch for, when to make contact sooner
4. **Step 4** - Act early on warning signs — deterioration linked to miscoded transactions shortens the interval, never lengthens it
5. **Step 5** - Close the follow-up cycle formally when goals are met, with a summary and re-entry route if needs return

## Output Format

The finished output is a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: miscoded transactions.
- Never let this happen: missing source documents.
- Never let this happen: VAT errors.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a bookkeeper; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a bookkeeping practice and client premises's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
