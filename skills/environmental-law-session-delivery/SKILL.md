# Session Delivery Guide — Environmental Law

Prepare for and deliver a professional session or appointment for developers, businesses and community groups in a law firm and planning inquiries. The deliverable is a session plan, delivery checklist and post-session record.
This skill is written for a environmental solicitor working with developers, businesses and community groups in a law firm and planning inquiries.

## When to use
- You are a environmental solicitor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a law firm and planning inquiries.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a session plan, delivery checklist and post-session record.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Environmental permit | official permission for activities affecting the environment |
| EIA | environmental impact assessment of a proposed development |
| Contaminated land | land polluted by previous use |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Review permit applications before the session so you know the history, goals and any cautions such as permit breaches unadvised
2. **Step 2** - Prepare the space and planning portals; check everything works and is safe before developers, businesses and community groups arrive
3. **Step 3** - Open the session by agreeing the focus and what a good outcome looks like, linked to permit success rates
4. **Step 4** - Deliver the session, adapting in the moment while staying inside the agreed plan and professional scope
5. **Step 5** - Close by summarising what was covered, confirming follow-ups, and completing the session record the same day

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: permit breaches unadvised.
- Never let this happen: consultation deadlines missed.
- Never let this happen: contaminated land liabilities.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a environmental solicitor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a law firm and planning inquiries's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
