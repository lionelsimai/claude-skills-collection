# Progress Review & Adjustment — Respiratory Therapy

Review progress for patients with acute and chronic breathing disorders against their plan and adjust it with evidence. The deliverable is a progress review summary with decisions and an updated plan.
This skill is written for a respiratory therapist working with patients with acute and chronic breathing disorders in hospital wards, intensive care and clinics.

## When to use
- You are a respiratory therapist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in hospital wards, intensive care and clinics.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a progress review summary with decisions and an updated plan.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Blood gas | a blood test measuring oxygen, carbon dioxide and acidity |
| Extubation | removing a breathing tube |
| Oxygen saturation | the percentage of haemoglobin carrying oxygen |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Pull together ventilation charts and the latest results for blood gas turnaround since the last review
2. **Step 2** - Compare actual progress with the plan's goals and timelines; identify what moved, what stalled and why
3. **Step 3** - Discuss the evidence with patients with acute and chronic breathing disorders honestly, including where the original plan no longer fits
4. **Step 4** - Agree adjustments: continue, intensify, change approach or close — and record the reasoning
5. **Step 5** - Update the plan and review date, and communicate changes to everyone involved in hospital wards, intensive care and clinics

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in hospital wards, intensive care and clinics] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients with acute and chronic breathing disorders and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a progress review summary with decisions and an updated plan, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in ventilation charts.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against blood gas turnaround.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in hospital wards, intensive care and clinics.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: ventilator alarms ignored.
- Never let this happen: incorrect oxygen targets.
- Never let this happen: tube displacement unnoticed.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a respiratory therapist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to hospital wards, intensive care and clinics's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
