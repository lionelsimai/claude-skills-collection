# New Client Onboarding Pack — Learning & Development

Onboard employees and organisational leaders smoothly into a learning and development specialist's service in organisations across all sectors. The deliverable is an onboarding pack: welcome information, expectations, first steps and contacts.
This skill is written for a learning and development specialist working with employees and organisational leaders in organisations across all sectors.

## When to use
- You are a learning and development specialist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in organisations across all sectors.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an onboarding pack: welcome information, expectations, first steps and contacts.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Needs analysis | identifying the gap between current and required skills |
| Blended learning | mixing formats such as workshops, online and coaching |
| Kirkpatrick | a model evaluating training from reaction to results |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Send a welcome pack before the first appointment: what the service does, what happens first, what to bring from training needs analyses
2. **Step 2** - Set expectations honestly — timescales, costs where relevant, and how progress on training completion will be shared
3. **Step 3** - Cover rights and responsibilities on both sides, including confidentiality limits and how to raise concerns
4. **Step 4** - Make the first contact easy: named contact, directions to organisations across all sectors, and what to do if plans change
5. **Step 5** - Check in after the first session or milestone and fix friction early, before it becomes disengagement or training that changes nothing

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: training that changes nothing.
- Never let this happen: accessibility overlooked.
- Never let this happen: evaluation skipped.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a learning and development specialist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to organisations across all sectors's rules.
