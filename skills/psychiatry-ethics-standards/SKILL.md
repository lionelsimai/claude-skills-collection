# Ethics & Professional Standards — Psychiatry

Apply the ethical codes and professional standards that govern a psychiatrist. The deliverable is an ethics guide with principles, common dilemmas and decision steps.
This skill is written for a psychiatrist working with people experiencing mental illness and psychological distress in a mental health unit and community mental health team base.

## When to use
- You are a psychiatrist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a mental health unit and community mental health team base.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an ethics guide with principles, common dilemmas and decision steps.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Liaison psychiatry | psychiatric care for patients in general hospitals |
| Formulation | an explanation of how a person's difficulties developed and are maintained |
| Sectioning | detaining a person under mental health law for assessment or treatment |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Know the code that applies in a mental health unit and community mental health team base: consent, confidentiality, honesty, competence and conflicts of interest
2. **Step 2** - Work through dilemmas with a framework: facts, stakeholders, options, code provisions, then decide and record
3. **Step 3** - Protect people experiencing mental illness and psychological distress first where duties conflict, and be alert to how suicide risk not recognised can arise from ethical shortcuts
4. **Step 4** - Declare conflicts of interest early and manage them transparently rather than hoping they stay invisible
5. **Step 5** - Raise concerns about others' practice through the proper channels, with evidence from psychiatric assessments, and support colleagues who do the same

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: suicide risk not recognised.
- Never let this happen: absconding from the unit.
- Never let this happen: medication side-effects unmanaged.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a psychiatrist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a mental health unit and community mental health team base's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
