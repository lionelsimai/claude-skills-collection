# Incident Reporting & Response — Family Law

Respond to, record and learn from incidents involving clients dealing with divorce, children and family finances in a law firm and family court. The deliverable is a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list.
This skill is written for a family law solicitor working with clients dealing with divorce, children and family finances in a law firm and family court.

## When to use
- You are a family law solicitor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a law firm and family court.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Disclosure | the formal exchange of financial information in family cases |

| Non-molestation order | a court order protecting a person from abuse |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Make the situation safe first; attend to people before paperwork, and escalate if missed court deadlines is still live
2. **Step 2** - Record facts while they are fresh: what happened, when, who was involved, what was observed — in client care letters
3. **Step 3** - Notify the people and bodies who must know, within the timeframes that apply in a law firm and family court
4. **Step 4** - Support those affected, including honest communication with clients dealing with divorce, children and family finances about what happened and what happens next
5. **Step 5** - Analyse causes without blame, agree preventive actions with owners and dates, and track them to closure

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a law firm and family court] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [clients dealing with divorce, children and family finances and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed court deadlines.
- Never let this happen: safeguarding issues unreported.
- Never let this happen: conflicts of interest.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a family law solicitor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a law firm and family court's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
