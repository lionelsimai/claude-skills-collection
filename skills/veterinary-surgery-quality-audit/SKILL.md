# Quality Audit — Veterinary Surgery

Audit the quality of veterinary surgeon work in a veterinary practice and animal hospital and drive measurable improvement. The deliverable is an audit report with scores, evidence and an improvement plan.
This skill is written for a veterinary surgeon working with animals and their owners in a veterinary practice and animal hospital.

## When to use
- You are a veterinary surgeon preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a veterinary practice and animal hospital.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an audit report with scores, evidence and an improvement plan.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Define the audit sample and criteria up front, anchored to appointment waiting times and the standards for a veterinary practice and animal hospital
2. **Step 2** - Review a sample of clinical records and observe practice where permitted, scoring consistently
3. **Step 3** - Separate isolated errors from patterns — patterns point to system fixes, not individual blame
4. **Step 4** - Report findings with evidence, and agree improvement actions that address the root causes, including anaesthetic incidents
5. **Step 5** - Re-audit after the agreed interval and publish the change in scores so improvement is visible

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: anaesthetic incidents.
- Never let this happen: zoonotic infection risk.
- Never let this happen: missed diagnoses from limited history.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a veterinary surgeon; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a veterinary practice and animal hospital's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
