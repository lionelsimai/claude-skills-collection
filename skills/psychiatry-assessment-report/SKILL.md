# Assessment & Findings Report — Psychiatry

Assess people experiencing mental illness and psychological distress and write a defensible findings report as a psychiatrist. The deliverable is a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
This skill is written for a psychiatrist working with people experiencing mental illness and psychological distress in a mental health unit and community mental health team base.

## When to use
- You are a psychiatrist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a mental health unit and community mental health team base.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Liaison psychiatry | psychiatric care for patients in general hospitals |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Review the intake record and psychiatric assessments so the assessment builds on verified information, not assumptions
2. **Step 2** - Carry out the assessment using observation level charts, following the recognised method for a mental health unit and community mental health team base
3. **Step 3** - Compare findings against the benchmarks behind risk assessment completion and note where evidence is strong, weak or missing
4. **Step 4** - Draft the report: findings first, then interpretation, then recommendations in priority order
5. **Step 5** - Check the draft for accuracy and for suicide risk not recognised; have it reviewed where the profession's standards require a second sign-off

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a mental health unit and community mental health team base] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [people experiencing mental illness and psychological distress and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: suicide risk not recognised.
- Never let this happen: absconding from the unit.
- Never let this happen: medication side-effects unmanaged.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a psychiatrist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a mental health unit and community mental health team base's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
