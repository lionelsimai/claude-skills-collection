# Risk Assessment — Environmental Consulting

Identify, score and control the risks in work for developers, industries and regulators in client sites and consultancy offices. The deliverable is a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners.
This skill is written for a environmental consultant working with developers, industries and regulators in client sites and consultancy offices.

## When to use
- You are a environmental consultant preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in client sites and consultancy offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Baseline | conditions measured before a change, for comparison |
| Receptor | a person, habitat or resource that could be harmed |
| Mitigation | actions reducing an environmental impact |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Map the activity step by step and list what could go wrong at each stage for developers, industries and regulators and staff
2. **Step 2** - Score each risk for likelihood and impact, giving special weight to missed contamination
3. **Step 3** - For every high risk, define a control that removes, reduces or contains it, using sampling equipment where relevant
4. **Step 4** - Record the assessment in assessment reports, assign an owner to each control, and set a review date
5. **Step 5** - Brief everyone involved before work starts; stop and reassess if conditions in client sites and consultancy offices change

2. Detail, following the workflow above

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed contamination.
- Never let this happen: sampling errors.
- Never let this happen: missed permit deadlines.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a environmental consultant; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to client sites and consultancy offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
