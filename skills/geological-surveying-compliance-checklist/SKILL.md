# Compliance & Standards Checklist — Geological Surveying

Check work in field sites and laboratories against the laws, standards and codes that govern a engineering geologist and surveyor. The deliverable is a completed compliance checklist with gaps and corrective actions.
This skill is written for a engineering geologist and surveyor working with mining, construction and environmental clients in field sites and laboratories.

## When to use
- You are a engineering geologist and surveyor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in field sites and laboratories.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed compliance checklist with gaps and corrective actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Core sample | a cylinder of rock extracted by drilling |
| Overburden | material lying above the resource of interest |
| Fault | a fracture in rock along which movement has occurred |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - List the obligations that apply: legislation, regulator guidance, professional codes and local policy in field sites and laboratories
2. **Step 2** - Work through each obligation against current practice and survey data, marking compliant, partial or missing
3. **Step 3** - Investigate every gap — especially anything touching field safety incidents — and record the evidence for the judgement
4. **Step 4** - Turn gaps into corrective actions with owners and deadlines, proportionate to the risk
5. **Step 5** - Re-check completed actions, file the checklist, and diarise the next compliance review

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: field safety incidents.
- Never let this happen: sample mix-ups.
- Never let this happen: misinterpreted ground conditions.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a engineering geologist and surveyor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to field sites and laboratories's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
