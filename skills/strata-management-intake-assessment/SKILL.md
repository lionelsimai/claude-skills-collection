# Intake & Initial Assessment — Strata Management

Run a structured intake and initial assessment for lot owners, owners corporations and residents in residential complexes and management offices. The deliverable is a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
This skill is written for a strata manager working with lot owners, owners corporations and residents in residential complexes and management offices.

## When to use
- You are a strata manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in residential complexes and management offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm identity, consent and the reason for referral or engagement, and open the record that will hold meeting minutes
2. **Step 2** - Gather history and current circumstances from lot owners, owners corporations and residents, using the standard intake structure for residential complexes and management offices
3. **Step 3** - Record baseline observations and measurements relevant to levy collection rates, noting anything that changes the priority of the case
4. **Step 4** - Flag immediate concerns — especially fund mismanagement — and escalate or fast-track anything urgent before proceeding
5. **Step 5** - Summarise findings for lot owners, owners corporations and residents in plain language, agree the next step, and file the intake section of meeting minutes

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: fund mismanagement.
- Never let this happen: meeting procedure errors.
- Never let this happen: by-law disputes escalated.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a strata manager; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to residential complexes and management offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
