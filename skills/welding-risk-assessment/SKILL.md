# Risk Assessment — Welding

Identify, score and control the risks in work for fabrication companies, construction and repair clients in workshops, shipyards and construction sites. The deliverable is a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners.
This skill is written for a welder working with fabrication companies, construction and repair clients in workshops, shipyards and construction sites.

## When to use
- You are a welder preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in workshops, shipyards and construction sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| WPS | a weld procedure specification defining how a weld is made |
| Penetration | how deeply a weld fuses into the base metal |
| Slag | a residue that must be removed from some welds |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Map the activity step by step and list what could go wrong at each stage for fabrication companies, construction and repair clients and staff
2. **Step 2** - Score each risk for likelihood and impact, giving special weight to weld failures in service
3. **Step 3** - For every high risk, define a control that removes, reduces or contains it, using welding machines where relevant
4. **Step 4** - Record the assessment in weld procedure specifications, assign an owner to each control, and set a review date
5. **Step 5** - Brief everyone involved before work starts; stop and reassess if conditions in workshops, shipyards and construction sites change

- Never let this happen: fumes exposure.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a welder; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to workshops, shipyards and construction sites's rules.
