# Mentoring & Training Junior Staff — Electrical Engineering

Train and mentor junior colleagues toward competent, safe electrical engineer practice. The deliverable is a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
This skill is written for a electrical engineer working with energy, manufacturing and building clients in design offices, plants and substations.

## When to use
- You are a electrical engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in design offices, plants and substations.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Load | the power drawn by a system or device |
| Protection relay | a device that detects faults and isolates circuits |
| Earthing | connecting equipment to earth for safety |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Assess the trainee's current level honestly against the competencies required in design offices, plants and substations
2. **Step 2** - Sequence learning from observation to supervised practice to independent work, using design software under supervision first
3. **Step 3** - Set explicit sign-off criteria for high-risk tasks — especially anything touching electric shock risks — and never sign off early
4. **Step 4** - Give feedback from direct observation and from circuit designs, specific and timely, with strengths named too
5. **Step 5** - Record progress, keep the training file current, and escalate persistent gaps through supervision, not gossip

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: electric shock risks.
- Never let this happen: protection failures.
- Never let this happen: overheating equipment.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a electrical engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to design offices, plants and substations's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
