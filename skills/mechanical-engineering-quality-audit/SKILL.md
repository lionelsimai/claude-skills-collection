# Quality Audit — Mechanical Engineering

Audit the quality of mechanical engineer work in design offices, factories and test facilities and drive measurable improvement. The deliverable is an audit report with scores, evidence and an improvement plan.
This skill is written for a mechanical engineer working with manufacturing and product clients in design offices, factories and test facilities.

## When to use
- You are a mechanical engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in design offices, factories and test facilities.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an audit report with scores, evidence and an improvement plan.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Tolerance | the permitted variation in a dimension |
| Fatigue | weakening of material from repeated loading |
| Prototype | an early version built to test a design |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Define the audit sample and criteria up front, anchored to prototype test pass rates and the standards for design offices, factories and test facilities
2. **Step 2** - Review a sample of design files and observe practice where permitted, scoring consistently
3. **Step 3** - Separate isolated errors from patterns — patterns point to system fixes, not individual blame
4. **Step 4** - Report findings with evidence, and agree improvement actions that address the root causes, including component failures
5. **Step 5** - Re-audit after the agreed interval and publish the change in scores so improvement is visible

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: component failures.
- Never let this happen: test safety incidents.
- Never let this happen: design tolerance errors.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a mechanical engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to design offices, factories and test facilities's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
