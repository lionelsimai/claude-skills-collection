# Risk Assessment — Higher Education Lecturing

Identify, score and control the risks in work for undergraduate and postgraduate students in a university department. The deliverable is a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners.
This skill is written for a university lecturer working with undergraduate and postgraduate students in a university department.

## When to use
- You are a university lecturer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a university department.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Module | a self-contained unit of study within a degree |
| Formative feedback | comments that help students improve before final assessment |
| External examiner | an independent academic who checks standards |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Map the activity step by step and list what could go wrong at each stage for undergraduate and postgraduate students and staff
2. **Step 2** - Score each risk for likelihood and impact, giving special weight to marking delays
3. **Step 3** - For every high risk, define a control that removes, reduces or contains it, using virtual learning environments where relevant
4. **Step 4** - Record the assessment in module outlines, assign an owner to each control, and set a review date
5. **Step 5** - Brief everyone involved before work starts; stop and reassess if conditions in a university department change

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a university department] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [undergraduate and postgraduate students and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: marking delays.
- Never let this happen: assessment design flaws.
- Never let this happen: student disengagement unnoticed.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a university lecturer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a university department's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
