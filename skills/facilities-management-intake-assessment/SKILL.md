# Intake & Initial Assessment — Facilities Management

Run a structured intake and initial assessment for building occupants, owners and service teams in commercial buildings and campuses. The deliverable is a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
This skill is written for a facilities manager working with building occupants, owners and service teams in commercial buildings and campuses.

## When to use
- You are a facilities manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in commercial buildings and campuses.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm identity, consent and the reason for referral or engagement, and open the record that will hold maintenance schedules
2. **Step 2** - Gather history and current circumstances from building occupants, owners and service teams, using the standard intake structure for commercial buildings and campuses
3. **Step 3** - Record baseline observations and measurements relevant to work order completion, noting anything that changes the priority of the case
4. **Step 4** - Flag immediate concerns — especially life-safety systems unmaintained — and escalate or fast-track anything urgent before proceeding
5. **Step 5** - Summarise findings for building occupants, owners and service teams in plain language, agree the next step, and file the intake section of maintenance schedules

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: life-safety systems unmaintained.
- Never let this happen: contractor failures.
- Never let this happen: energy waste.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a facilities manager; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to commercial buildings and campuses's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
