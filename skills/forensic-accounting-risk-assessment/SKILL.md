# Risk Assessment — Forensic Accounting

Identify, score and control the risks in work for courts, insurers and businesses investigating financial wrongdoing in an accountancy firm and legal proceedings. The deliverable is a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners.
This skill is written for a forensic accountant working with courts, insurers and businesses investigating financial wrongdoing in an accountancy firm and legal proceedings.

## When to use
- You are a forensic accountant preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in an accountancy firm and legal proceedings.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Map the activity step by step and list what could go wrong at each stage for courts, insurers and businesses investigating financial wrongdoing and staff
2. **Step 2** - Score each risk for likelihood and impact, giving special weight to evidence contamination
3. **Step 3** - For every high risk, define a control that removes, reduces or contains it, using data analysis software where relevant
4. **Step 4** - Record the assessment in investigation reports, assign an owner to each control, and set a review date
5. **Step 5** - Brief everyone involved before work starts; stop and reassess if conditions in an accountancy firm and legal proceedings change

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: evidence contamination.
- Never let this happen: assumptions challenged in court.
- Never let this happen: conflicts of interest.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a forensic accountant; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to an accountancy firm and legal proceedings's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
