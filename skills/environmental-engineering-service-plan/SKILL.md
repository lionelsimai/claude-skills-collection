# Service Plan Design — Environmental Engineering

Design a realistic, measurable service plan for water companies, industries and regulators as a environmental engineer. The deliverable is a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates.
This skill is written for a environmental engineer working with water companies, industries and regulators in treatment plants, client sites and design offices.

## When to use
- You are a environmental engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in treatment plants, client sites and design offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Consent | permission to discharge treated water or waste |
| Effluent | liquid waste discharged from a process |
| Remediation | cleaning up contaminated land or water |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Start from the assessment findings and agree goals with water companies, industries and regulators — specific enough to measure against permit compliance
2. **Step 2** - Break each goal into actions sequenced for treatment plants, client sites and design offices, naming who does what and by when
3. **Step 3** - Build the plan around impact assessments so progress, changes and decisions are recorded in one place
4. **Step 4** - Price or resource the plan honestly: time, modelling software and materials, with contingencies for pollution incidents
5. **Step 5** - Set the review date and the trigger points that would change the plan, and confirm the plan with all parties

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in treatment plants, client sites and design offices] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [water companies, industries and regulators and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in impact assessments.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against permit compliance.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in treatment plants, client sites and design offices.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: pollution incidents.
- Never let this happen: consent breaches.
- Never let this happen: design underperformance.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a environmental engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to treatment plants, client sites and design offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
