# Follow-Up & Maintenance Schedule — Food Processing

Plan follow-up and maintenance so gains for retailers, wholesalers and consumers are checked and sustained. The deliverable is a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers.
This skill is written for a food production manager working with retailers, wholesalers and consumers in food factories and processing plants.

## When to use
- You are a food production manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in food factories and processing plants.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Traceability | following a product through every production stage |
| Shelf life | how long a product remains safe and acceptable |
| Critical control point | a step where control prevents a food safety hazard |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Set follow-up intervals from the plan and from how production yield is expected to move over time
2. **Step 2** - Define what is checked at each contact, using processing machinery where measurement is needed, and record it in production plans
3. **Step 3** - Give retailers, wholesalers and consumers clear guidance for the interval: what to do, what to watch for, when to make contact sooner
4. **Step 4** - Act early on warning signs — deterioration linked to contamination incidents shortens the interval, never lengthens it
5. **Step 5** - Close the follow-up cycle formally when goals are met, with a summary and re-entry route if needs return

2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

- Never let this happen: labelling errors.
