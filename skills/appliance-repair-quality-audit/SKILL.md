# Quality Audit — Appliance Repair

Audit the quality of appliance repair technician work in customers' homes and workshops and drive measurable improvement. The deliverable is an audit report with scores, evidence and an improvement plan.
This skill is written for a appliance repair technician working with homeowners, landlords and retailers in customers' homes and workshops.

## When to use
- You are a appliance repair technician preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in customers' homes and workshops.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an audit report with scores, evidence and an improvement plan.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Diagnostic code | a fault code displayed by an appliance's controls |
| PAT testing | checking portable appliances for electrical safety |
| Planned obsolescence | designing products with a limited lifespan |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Define the audit sample and criteria up front, anchored to first-time fix rate and the standards for customers' homes and workshops
2. **Step 2** - Review a sample of job cards and observe practice where permitted, scoring consistently
3. **Step 3** - Separate isolated errors from patterns — patterns point to system fixes, not individual blame
4. **Step 4** - Report findings with evidence, and agree improvement actions that address the root causes, including electric shock
5. **Step 5** - Re-audit after the agreed interval and publish the change in scores so improvement is visible

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: electric shock.
- Never let this happen: gas appliance risks.
- Never let this happen: repeat faults.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a appliance repair technician; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to customers' homes and workshops's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
