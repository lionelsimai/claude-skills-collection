# Risk Assessment — Quantity Surveying

Identify, score and control the risks in work for developers, contractors and public clients in construction projects and offices. The deliverable is a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners.
This skill is written for a quantity surveyor working with developers, contractors and public clients in construction projects and offices.

## When to use
- You are a quantity surveyor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in construction projects and offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Bill of quantities | an itemised list of materials and work for pricing |
| Valuation | an assessment of work completed for interim payment |
| Final account | the agreed total cost at a project's end |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Map the activity step by step and list what could go wrong at each stage for developers, contractors and public clients and staff
2. **Step 2** - Score each risk for likelihood and impact, giving special weight to cost overruns unreported
3. **Step 3** - For every high risk, define a control that removes, reduces or contains it, using measurement software where relevant
4. **Step 4** - Record the assessment in bills of quantities, assign an owner to each control, and set a review date
5. **Step 5** - Brief everyone involved before work starts; stop and reassess if conditions in construction projects and offices change

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: cost overruns unreported.
- Never let this happen: measurement errors.
- Never let this happen: contractual deadlines missed.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a quantity surveyor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to construction projects and offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
