# Incident Reporting & Response — Radiology

Respond to, record and learn from incidents involving patients referred for diagnostic imaging and referring clinicians in a hospital imaging department. The deliverable is a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list.
This skill is written for a radiologist working with patients referred for diagnostic imaging and referring clinicians in a hospital imaging department.

## When to use
- You are a radiologist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a hospital imaging department.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| PACS | the system storing and displaying medical images |
| Contrast | a substance given to make structures visible on scans |
| Modality | a type of imaging, such as CT, MRI or ultrasound |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Make the situation safe first; attend to people before paperwork, and escalate if missed findings on imaging is still live
2. **Step 2** - Record facts while they are fresh: what happened, when, who was involved, what was observed — in imaging reports
3. **Step 3** - Notify the people and bodies who must know, within the timeframes that apply in a hospital imaging department
4. **Step 4** - Support those affected, including honest communication with patients referred for diagnostic imaging and referring clinicians about what happened and what happens next
5. **Step 5** - Analyse causes without blame, agree preventive actions with owners and dates, and track them to closure

## Profession-Specific Components
| Component | Details for Radiology |
|-----------|-------------------------------|
| Core documentation | imaging reports; scan protocols; contrast consent forms; radiation dose records |
| Core tools & equipment | CT scanners; MRI scanners; ultrasound systems; PACS workstations |
| People involved | patients referred for diagnostic imaging and referring clinicians; the radiologist; colleagues and other services in a hospital imaging department |
| Primary record | imaging reports — keep it current, dated and attributable |

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a hospital imaging department] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients referred for diagnostic imaging and referring clinicians and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in imaging reports.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against report turnaround time.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in a hospital imaging department.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed findings on imaging.
- Never let this happen: radiation overexposure.
- Never let this happen: contrast reactions.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a radiologist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a hospital imaging department's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
