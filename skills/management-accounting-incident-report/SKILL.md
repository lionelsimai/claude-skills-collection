# Incident Reporting & Response — Management Accounting

Respond to, record and learn from incidents involving business managers and leadership teams in a finance department. The deliverable is a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list.
This skill is written for a management accountant working with business managers and leadership teams in a finance department.

## When to use
- You are a management accountant preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a finance department.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Variance | the difference between budgeted and actual figures |
| Forecast | a prediction of future financial performance |
| Cost centre | a unit of a business tracked for its costs |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Make the situation safe first; attend to people before paperwork, and escalate if misleading forecasts is still live
2. **Step 2** - Record facts while they are fresh: what happened, when, who was involved, what was observed — in management accounts
3. **Step 3** - Notify the people and bodies who must know, within the timeframes that apply in a finance department
4. **Step 4** - Support those affected, including honest communication with business managers and leadership teams about what happened and what happens next
5. **Step 5** - Analyse causes without blame, agree preventive actions with owners and dates, and track them to closure

## Profession-Specific Components
| Component | Details for Management Accounting |
|-----------|-------------------------------|
| Core documentation | management accounts; budgets and forecasts; variance reports; costing models |
| Core tools & equipment | ERP systems; forecasting software; dashboards; spreadsheet models |
| People involved | business managers and leadership teams; the management accountant; colleagues and other services in a finance department |
| Primary record | management accounts — keep it current, dated and attributable |

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a finance department] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [business managers and leadership teams and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in management accounts.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against report delivery timeliness.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in a finance department.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: misleading forecasts.
- Never let this happen: cost allocations distorted.
- Never let this happen: deadline slippage.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a management accountant; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a finance department's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
