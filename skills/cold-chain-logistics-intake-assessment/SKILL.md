# Intake & Initial Assessment — Cold Chain Logistics

Run a structured intake and initial assessment for food, pharmaceutical and healthcare shippers in cold stores, vehicles and distribution hubs. The deliverable is a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
This skill is written for a cold chain manager working with food, pharmaceutical and healthcare shippers in cold stores, vehicles and distribution hubs.

## When to use
- You are a cold chain manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in cold stores, vehicles and distribution hubs.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm identity, consent and the reason for referral or engagement, and open the record that will hold temperature logs
2. **Step 2** - Gather history and current circumstances from food, pharmaceutical and healthcare shippers, using the standard intake structure for cold stores, vehicles and distribution hubs
3. **Step 3** - Record baseline observations and measurements relevant to temperature compliance rate, noting anything that changes the priority of the case
4. **Step 4** - Flag immediate concerns — especially temperature excursions spoiling goods — and escalate or fast-track anything urgent before proceeding
5. **Step 5** - Summarise findings for food, pharmaceutical and healthcare shippers in plain language, agree the next step, and file the intake section of temperature logs

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: temperature excursions spoiling goods.
- Never let this happen: equipment failures unnoticed.
- Never let this happen: broken chain of custody.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a cold chain manager; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to cold stores, vehicles and distribution hubs's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
