# Service Plan Design — Disability Support Work

Design a realistic, measurable service plan for people with disabilities and their families as a disability support worker. The deliverable is a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates.
This skill is written for a disability support worker working with people with disabilities and their families in homes, day services and community settings.

## When to use
- You are a disability support worker preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in homes, day services and community settings.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Person-centred | support designed around the individual's wishes |
| Communication passport | a document explaining how a person communicates |
| Positive behaviour support | understanding behaviour to reduce distress and restriction |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Start from the assessment findings and agree goals with people with disabilities and their families — specific enough to measure against goal progress
2. **Step 2** - Break each goal into actions sequenced for homes, day services and community settings, naming who does what and by when
3. **Step 3** - Build the plan around support plans so progress, changes and decisions are recorded in one place
4. **Step 4** - Price or resource the plan honestly: time, assistive technology and materials, with contingencies for personal care done unsafely
5. **Step 5** - Set the review date and the trigger points that would change the plan, and confirm the plan with all parties

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in homes, day services and community settings] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [people with disabilities and their families and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in support plans.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against goal progress.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in homes, day services and community settings.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: personal care done unsafely.
- Never let this happen: communication needs ignored.
- Never let this happen: restrictive practices misused.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a disability support worker; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to homes, day services and community settings's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
