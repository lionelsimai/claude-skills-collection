# Safety Protocol — Air Traffic Control

Work safely in control towers and radar centres: a practical safety protocol for a air traffic controller and pilots, airlines and airport operators. The deliverable is a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions.
This skill is written for a air traffic controller working with pilots, airlines and airport operators in control towers and radar centres.

## When to use
- You are a air traffic controller preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in control towers and radar centres.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Separation | the safe distance maintained between aircraft |
| Handover | transferring responsibility for traffic between controllers |
| Squawk | the transponder code identifying an aircraft on radar |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Identify the hazards present in control towers and radar centres for staff, pilots, airlines and airport operators and visitors, starting with loss of separation
2. **Step 2** - Write controls in order: eliminate, substitute, engineer, administrate, then protective equipment such as radar systems
3. **Step 3** - Set pre-start checks and stop-work authority — anyone can halt work that looks unsafe, without penalty
4. **Step 4** - Define what happens when something goes wrong: first response, escalation, recording in traffic logs
5. **Step 5** - Review the protocol after every incident and at least annually; brief and re-brief everyone it covers

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: loss of separation.
- Never let this happen: communication misunderstandings.
- Never let this happen: fatigue on shift.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a air traffic controller; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to control towers and radar centres's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
