# Cost Estimates & Quotes — Environmental Consulting

Build transparent, defensible cost estimates and quotes as a environmental consultant. The deliverable is an itemised estimate with assumptions, exclusions and validity terms.
This skill is written for a environmental consultant working with developers, industries and regulators in client sites and consultancy offices.

## When to use
- You are a environmental consultant preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in client sites and consultancy offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an itemised estimate with assumptions, exclusions and validity terms.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Baseline | conditions measured before a change, for comparison |
| Receptor | a person, habitat or resource that could be harmed |
| Mitigation | actions reducing an environmental impact |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Scope the work precisely from the assessment and assessment reports; list what is included and excluded
2. **Step 2** - Cost each element: professional time, sampling equipment usage, materials, travel and third-party fees
3. **Step 3** - Add contingency proportionate to uncertainty — and say so openly rather than burying it, which protects against missed contamination
4. **Step 4** - Present the quote with options where genuine choices exist, and relate value to outcomes such as report turnaround
5. **Step 5** - Record acceptance, variations and final costs against the estimate so future quotes get more accurate

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed contamination.
- Never let this happen: sampling errors.
- Never let this happen: missed permit deadlines.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a environmental consultant; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to client sites and consultancy offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
