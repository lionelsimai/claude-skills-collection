# Service Plan Design — Electrical Engineering

Design a realistic, measurable service plan for energy, manufacturing and building clients as a electrical engineer. The deliverable is a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates.
This skill is written for a electrical engineer working with energy, manufacturing and building clients in design offices, plants and substations.

## When to use
- You are a electrical engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in design offices, plants and substations.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Load | the power drawn by a system or device |
| Protection relay | a device that detects faults and isolates circuits |
| Earthing | connecting equipment to earth for safety |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Start from the assessment findings and agree goals with energy, manufacturing and building clients — specific enough to measure against test pass rates
2. **Step 2** - Break each goal into actions sequenced for design offices, plants and substations, naming who does what and by when
3. **Step 3** - Build the plan around circuit designs so progress, changes and decisions are recorded in one place
4. **Step 4** - Price or resource the plan honestly: time, design software and materials, with contingencies for electric shock risks
5. **Step 5** - Set the review date and the trigger points that would change the plan, and confirm the plan with all parties

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in design offices, plants and substations] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [energy, manufacturing and building clients and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route
