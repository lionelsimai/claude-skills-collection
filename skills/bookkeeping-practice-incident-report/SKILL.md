# Incident Reporting & Response — Bookkeeping Practice

Respond to, record and learn from incidents involving small businesses, sole traders and accountants in a bookkeeping practice and client premises. The deliverable is a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list.
This skill is written for a bookkeeper working with small businesses, sole traders and accountants in a bookkeeping practice and client premises.

## When to use
- You are a bookkeeper preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a bookkeeping practice and client premises.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Reconciliation | matching records against bank statements to find differences |
| Ledger | the record of transactions by category |
| Accruals | recognising income or costs in the period they relate to |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Make the situation safe first; attend to people before paperwork, and escalate if miscoded transactions is still live
2. **Step 2** - Record facts while they are fresh: what happened, when, who was involved, what was observed — in bank reconciliations
3. **Step 3** - Notify the people and bodies who must know, within the timeframes that apply in a bookkeeping practice and client premises
4. **Step 4** - Support those affected, including honest communication with small businesses, sole traders and accountants about what happened and what happens next
5. **Step 5** - Analyse causes without blame, agree preventive actions with owners and dates, and track them to closure

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a bookkeeping practice and client premises] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [small businesses, sole traders and accountants and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in bank reconciliations.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against reconciliation timeliness.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in a bookkeeping practice and client premises.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: miscoded transactions.
- Never let this happen: missing source documents.
- Never let this happen: VAT errors.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a bookkeeper; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a bookkeeping practice and client premises's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
