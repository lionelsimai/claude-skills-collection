# Emergency Response Procedure — Employment Law

Respond to emergencies in a law firm and employment tribunals calmly, in role and in the right order. The deliverable is an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
This skill is written for a employment solicitor working with employees, employers and HR teams in a law firm and employment tribunals.

## When to use
- You are a employment solicitor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a law firm and employment tribunals.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Settlement agreement | a binding agreement ending employment and waiving claims |
| Unfair dismissal | termination of employment without a fair reason or process |
| Tribunal | the court that hears employment disputes |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Recognise the emergency early: know the presentations that matter for employees, employers and HR teams, especially tribunal deadlines missed
2. **Step 2** - Take the immediate actions a employment solicitor is trained and authorised for, using HR information systems as drilled
3. **Step 3** - Call for help with a structured message: location in a law firm and employment tribunals, situation, what has been done, what is needed
4. **Step 4** - Keep employees, employers and HR teams and bystanders informed and safe while help arrives; hand over with a concise summary
5. **Step 5** - Record the event fully in contracts of employment afterwards, debrief the team, and update the procedure with lessons learned

## Output Format

1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: tribunal deadlines missed.
- Never let this happen: discrimination risks unadvised.
- Never let this happen: settlement terms misunderstood.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a employment solicitor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a law firm and employment tribunals's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
