# Troubleshooting & Problem Solving — Clinical Research

Diagnose and resolve common problems a clinical research practitioner meets in hospitals and research sites. The deliverable is a troubleshooting guide: symptoms, likely causes, checks and fixes.
This skill is written for a clinical research practitioner working with trial participants, sponsors and investigators in hospitals and research sites.

## When to use
- You are a clinical research practitioner preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in hospitals and research sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a troubleshooting guide: symptoms, likely causes, checks and fixes.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Protocol | the detailed plan for a clinical trial |
| Informed consent | a participant's voluntary agreement after full explanation |
| Adverse event | an untoward medical occurrence in a participant |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Describe the problem precisely from the record in protocols — when it started, what changed, who it affects
2. **Step 2** - Work from most likely and most dangerous causes first; rule out anything connected to consent failures early
3. **Step 3** - Run checks in a logical order using electronic data capture, changing one variable at a time so the cause can be isolated
4. **Step 4** - Apply the fix, then verify against recruitment to target that the problem is actually resolved, not just quieter
5. **Step 5** - Document the diagnosis and fix so the next occurrence is faster, and flag recurring problems for root-cause work

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in hospitals and research sites] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [trial participants, sponsors and investigators and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a troubleshooting guide: symptoms, likely causes, checks and fixes, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in protocols.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against recruitment to target.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in hospitals and research sites.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: consent failures.
- Never let this happen: protocol deviations unreported.
- Never let this happen: participant safety signals missed.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a clinical research practitioner; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to hospitals and research sites's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
