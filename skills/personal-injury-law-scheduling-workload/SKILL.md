# Scheduling & Workload Management — Personal Injury Law

Plan schedules, caseloads and turnaround times for a personal injury solicitor in a law firm. The deliverable is a workable schedule template with priorities, buffers and escalation rules.
This skill is written for a personal injury solicitor working with clients injured in accidents or through negligence in a law firm.

## When to use
- You are a personal injury solicitor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a law firm.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a workable schedule template with priorities, buffers and escalation rules.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Limitation | the legal deadline for starting a claim |
| Schedule of loss | a document listing the financial losses claimed |
| Liability | legal responsibility for the injury |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Map demand: appointment or job types, durations, and the deadlines that drive claim settlement values
2. **Step 2** - Build the schedule around fixed commitments in a law firm first, then fit flexible work around them
3. **Step 3** - Protect buffer time for overruns, urgent cases and the admin behind claim files — schedules without buffers fail by week two
4. **Step 4** - Define triage rules so urgent work (for example anything involving limitation deadlines missed) displaces routine work predictably
5. **Step 5** - Review the schedule weekly against actuals and adjust booking rules, staffing or case management systems availability

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: limitation deadlines missed.
- Never let this happen: evidence lost.
- Never let this happen: under-settlement of claims.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a personal injury solicitor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a law firm's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
