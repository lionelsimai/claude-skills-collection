# Follow-Up and Learning Loop

## Follow up with a reason

- Follow up only when there is a new reason: a relevant event, a launch, a deadline the lead named, or material they asked for.

- Keep follow-ups shorter than the first email. One new fact, one line of proof, one ask.

- Stop after the agreed number of touches. Silence is an answer, and chasing it costs reputation.

## Measure the campaign honestly

- Track per segment: sent, replies, hot leads, meetings booked, spend, and cost per hot lead.

- Count a hot lead only when a reply moves the deal forward. Opens and polite replies are not hot leads.

- Keep a weekly scoreboard the user can audit: partner revenue signed, paid seats, asks made, confirmed opt-ins, and recurring revenue. Registrations and impressions do not count as revenue.

## Scale, pause, or keep testing

- Scale a segment when its cost per hot lead is below the scale threshold and its hot rate is healthy.

- Pause a segment when its cost per hot lead passes the pause threshold after enough volume to mean anything, or when its hot rate stays near zero at volume.

- Keep testing anything in between, and say what number would change the verdict.

- Report every verdict with the numbers behind it, so the user can overrule it with evidence.

## When to use

Use this skill when the task matches its description above and you need a repeatable, checked workflow rather than an improvised answer.

## Guardrails and quality checks

- Never fabricate facts, numbers, clients or results. Use only sources the user provides or verifies.
- Keep drafts as drafts until a human approves any external send, payment or commitment.
- Check the final output against the workflow above before delivering it, and state anything you could not verify.

## Output format and example

Deliver the result in the structure the workflow defines. Example request: "Use the Follow-Up and Learning Loop skill for my situation: [describe the business, audience and goal]." Example output: a completed, checked deliverable following every step above, with open questions listed at the end rather than guessed.
