# Client Explanation & Communication Guide — Radiography

Explain complex diagnostic radiographer matters to patients attending for medical imaging in plain, honest language. The deliverable is a communication pack: explanation script, FAQs and a written summary.
This skill is written for a diagnostic radiographer working with patients attending for medical imaging in an imaging department.

## When to use
- You are a diagnostic radiographer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in an imaging department.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a communication pack: explanation script, FAQs and a written summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Justification | confirming an examination's benefit outweighs its radiation risk |
| Reject analysis | reviewing why images were repeated |
| ALARP | radiation dose kept as low as reasonably practicable |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Identify what patients attending for medical imaging actually need to decide or understand, and the misconceptions to clear up first
2. **Step 2** - Translate the technical content — including terms behind examination requests — into plain language without losing accuracy
3. **Step 3** - Structure the explanation: what we found, what it means, the options, the recommendation and the risks such as excess radiation dose
4. **Step 4** - Check understanding by asking patients attending for medical imaging to play it back in their own words; correct gaps kindly
5. **Step 5** - Follow up with a written summary the same day and record the conversation in the file

| Component | Details for Radiography |

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in an imaging department] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients attending for medical imaging and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a communication pack: explanation script, FAQs and a written summary, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: excess radiation dose.
- Never let this happen: wrong patient imaged.
- Never let this happen: missed pregnancy checks.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a diagnostic radiographer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to an imaging department's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
