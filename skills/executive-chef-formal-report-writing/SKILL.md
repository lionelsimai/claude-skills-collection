# Formal Report Writing — Executive Chef Practice

Write formal reports as a executive chef that stand up to scrutiny from any reader. The deliverable is a report template with structure, evidence standards and review steps.
This skill is written for a executive chef working with diners, kitchen teams and restaurant owners in professional kitchens.

## When to use
- You are a executive chef preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in professional kitchens.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a report template with structure, evidence standards and review steps.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Establish the report's purpose, audience and deadline before writing; gather source material from menu plans
2. **Step 2** - Structure: summary, background, method, findings, analysis, recommendations — in that order
3. **Step 3** - Write findings factually and separately from opinion; label professional judgement as judgement
4. **Step 4** - Support key points with evidence and with food cost control where outcomes are claimed; acknowledge limitations openly
5. **Step 5** - Proofread for accuracy and tone, check names, dates and figures twice, and consider food poisoning risks before release

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: food poisoning risks.
- Never let this happen: cross-contamination.
- Never let this happen: burns and cuts in service.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a executive chef; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to professional kitchens's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
