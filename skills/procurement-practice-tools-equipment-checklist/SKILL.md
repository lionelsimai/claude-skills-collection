# Tools & Equipment Checklist — Procurement Practice

Select, check and maintain the tools and equipment a procurement officer relies on. The deliverable is an equipment checklist with inspection points, maintenance dates and replacement triggers.
This skill is written for a procurement officer working with businesses and public bodies buying goods and services in procurement departments.

## When to use
- You are a procurement officer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in procurement departments.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an equipment checklist with inspection points, maintenance dates and replacement triggers.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Tender | a formal process inviting suppliers to bid |
| Specification | the detailed requirement a supplier must meet |
| Total cost | purchase price plus all associated costs of ownership |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - List the essential kit for work in procurement departments, centred on e-procurement systems, with what each item is used for
2. **Step 2** - Define pre-use checks: condition, calibration or charge status, cleanliness and safety features
3. **Step 3** - Set maintenance and replacement intervals based on usage and manufacturer guidance, and log them against purchase orders
4. **Step 4** - Quarantine faulty items immediately — faulty kit is a direct route to supplier failure
5. **Step 5** - Review the kit list quarterly against the work actually done and retire or add items deliberately

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: supplier failure.
- Never let this happen: fraud in purchasing.
- Never let this happen: contract deadlines missed.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a procurement officer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to procurement departments's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
