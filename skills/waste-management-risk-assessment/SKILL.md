# Risk Assessment — Waste Management

Identify, score and control the risks in work for households, businesses and councils in waste facilities and collection rounds. The deliverable is a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners.
This skill is written for a waste operations manager working with households, businesses and councils in waste facilities and collection rounds.

## When to use
- You are a waste operations manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in waste facilities and collection rounds.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Map the activity step by step and list what could go wrong at each stage for households, businesses and councils and staff
2. **Step 2** - Score each risk for likelihood and impact, giving special weight to hazardous waste mishandling
3. **Step 3** - For every high risk, define a control that removes, reduces or contains it, using collection vehicles where relevant
4. **Step 4** - Record the assessment in waste transfer notes, assign an owner to each control, and set a review date
5. **Step 5** - Brief everyone involved before work starts; stop and reassess if conditions in waste facilities and collection rounds change

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in waste facilities and collection rounds] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [households, businesses and councils and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: hazardous waste mishandling.
- Never let this happen: vehicle accidents.
- Never let this happen: site fires.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a waste operations manager; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to waste facilities and collection rounds's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
