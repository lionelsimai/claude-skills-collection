# Handover Briefing — Forensic Accounting

Hand over courts, insurers and businesses investigating financial wrongdoing or active work to another professional without losing critical detail. The deliverable is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
This skill is written for a forensic accountant working with courts, insurers and businesses investigating financial wrongdoing in an accountancy firm and legal proceedings.

## When to use
- You are a forensic accountant preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in an accountancy firm and legal proceedings.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Summarise the current position from investigation reports: what is done, what is in progress, what is waiting
2. **Step 2** - Highlight live risks first — evidence contamination — and the controls or watchpoints already in place
3. **Step 3** - State the next actions with owners and deadlines, and the results expected for investigation turnaround
4. **Step 4** - Walk the receiving professional through the brief, answer questions, and confirm understanding
5. **Step 5** - Record that handover happened, who accepted it, and where the full record lives in an accountancy firm and legal proceedings

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in an accountancy firm and legal proceedings] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [courts, insurers and businesses investigating financial wrongdoing and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan

2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against investigation turnaround.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in an accountancy firm and legal proceedings.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: evidence contamination.
- Never let this happen: assumptions challenged in court.
- Never let this happen: conflicts of interest.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a forensic accountant; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to an accountancy firm and legal proceedings's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
