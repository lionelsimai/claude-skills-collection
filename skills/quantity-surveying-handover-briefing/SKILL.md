# Handover Briefing — Quantity Surveying

Hand over developers, contractors and public clients or active work to another professional without losing critical detail. The deliverable is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
This skill is written for a quantity surveyor working with developers, contractors and public clients in construction projects and offices.

## When to use
- You are a quantity surveyor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in construction projects and offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Bill of quantities | an itemised list of materials and work for pricing |
| Valuation | an assessment of work completed for interim payment |
| Final account | the agreed total cost at a project's end |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Summarise the current position from bills of quantities: what is done, what is in progress, what is waiting
2. **Step 2** - Highlight live risks first — cost overruns unreported — and the controls or watchpoints already in place
3. **Step 3** - State the next actions with owners and deadlines, and the results expected for valuation timeliness
4. **Step 4** - Walk the receiving professional through the brief, answer questions, and confirm understanding
5. **Step 5** - Record that handover happened, who accepted it, and where the full record lives in construction projects and offices

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in construction projects and offices] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [developers, contractors and public clients and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: cost overruns unreported.
- Never let this happen: measurement errors.
- Never let this happen: contractual deadlines missed.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a quantity surveyor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to construction projects and offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
