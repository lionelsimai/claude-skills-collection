# Scheduling & Workload Management — Audit & Assurance

Plan schedules, caseloads and turnaround times for a external auditor in an accountancy firm and client sites. The deliverable is a workable schedule template with priorities, buffers and escalation rules.
This skill is written for a external auditor working with companies, charities and public bodies requiring an audit in an accountancy firm and client sites.

## When to use
- You are a external auditor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in an accountancy firm and client sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a workable schedule template with priorities, buffers and escalation rules.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Materiality | the size at which errors could influence decisions |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Map demand: appointment or job types, durations, and the deadlines that drive audit completion times
2. **Step 2** - Build the schedule around fixed commitments in an accountancy firm and client sites first, then fit flexible work around them
3. **Step 3** - Protect buffer time for overruns, urgent cases and the admin behind audit plans — schedules without buffers fail by week two
4. **Step 4** - Define triage rules so urgent work (for example anything involving material misstatements missed) displaces routine work predictably
5. **Step 5** - Review the schedule weekly against actuals and adjust booking rules, staffing or audit software availability

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: material misstatements missed.
- Never let this happen: independence threats.
- Never let this happen: deadline overruns.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a external auditor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to an accountancy firm and client sites's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
