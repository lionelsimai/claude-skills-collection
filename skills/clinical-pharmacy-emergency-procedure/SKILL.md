# Emergency Response Procedure — Clinical Pharmacy

Respond to emergencies in hospital wards and clinics calmly, in role and in the right order. The deliverable is an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
This skill is written for a clinical pharmacist working with patients with complex medication needs and prescribing teams in hospital wards and clinics.

## When to use
- You are a clinical pharmacist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in hospital wards and clinics.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Polypharmacy | the use of many medicines by one patient |
| Formulary | the approved list of medicines for an organisation |
| Titration | adjusting a dose gradually to effect |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Recognise the emergency early: know the presentations that matter for patients with complex medication needs and prescribing teams, especially dangerous interactions missed
2. **Step 2** - Take the immediate actions a clinical pharmacist is trained and authorised for, using electronic prescribing systems as drilled
3. **Step 3** - Call for help with a structured message: location in hospital wards and clinics, situation, what has been done, what is needed
4. **Step 4** - Keep patients with complex medication needs and prescribing teams and bystanders informed and safe while help arrives; hand over with a concise summary
5. **Step 5** - Record the event fully in medication reviews afterwards, debrief the team, and update the procedure with lessons learned

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in hospital wards and clinics] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients with complex medication needs and prescribing teams and other stakeholders]

The finished output is an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in medication reviews.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against medication review completion.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in hospital wards and clinics.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: dangerous interactions missed.
- Never let this happen: look-alike medicine confusion.
- Never let this happen: discharge medicines errors.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a clinical pharmacist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to hospital wards and clinics's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
