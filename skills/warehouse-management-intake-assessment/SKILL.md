# Intake & Initial Assessment — Warehouse Management

Run a structured intake and initial assessment for retailers, manufacturers and distribution clients in warehouses and distribution centres. The deliverable is a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
This skill is written for a warehouse manager working with retailers, manufacturers and distribution clients in warehouses and distribution centres.

## When to use
- You are a warehouse manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in warehouses and distribution centres.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Pick accuracy | how often the correct items are picked |
| Goods-in | receiving and checking deliveries |
| Cycle counting | counting sections of stock on a rolling basis |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm identity, consent and the reason for referral or engagement, and open the record that will hold inventory records
2. **Step 2** - Gather history and current circumstances from retailers, manufacturers and distribution clients, using the standard intake structure for warehouses and distribution centres
3. **Step 3** - Record baseline observations and measurements relevant to pick accuracy, noting anything that changes the priority of the case
4. **Step 4** - Flag immediate concerns — especially forklift accidents — and escalate or fast-track anything urgent before proceeding
5. **Step 5** - Summarise findings for retailers, manufacturers and distribution clients in plain language, agree the next step, and file the intake section of inventory records

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: forklift accidents.
- Never let this happen: stock shrinkage.
- Never let this happen: picking errors shipped.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a warehouse manager; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to warehouses and distribution centres's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
