# Metrics & Outcomes Review — Freight Forwarding

Track, interpret and act on the outcome metrics that matter for a freight forwarder. The deliverable is a metrics dashboard definition and an interpretation/action review.
This skill is written for a freight forwarder working with importers, exporters and carriers in freight offices, ports and airports.

## When to use
- You are a freight forwarder preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in freight offices, ports and airports.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a metrics dashboard definition and an interpretation/action review.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Bill of lading | the transport document evidencing the carriage contract |
| Cut-off | the deadline for delivering cargo for a sailing or flight |
| Demurrage | charges for keeping containers beyond free time |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Define each metric precisely — start with shipment on-time rate — including source data in shipping documents and collection method
2. **Step 2** - Set review cadence and comparison baselines so trends, not single data points, drive decisions
3. **Step 3** - Investigate movements before celebrating or panicking: check data quality, case mix and one-off causes
4. **Step 4** - Link metric movements to practice changes and to risks such as customs delays from bad documents; test one change at a time where possible
5. **Step 5** - Report results honestly to stakeholders in freight offices, ports and airports, including where outcomes fell short and what changes

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: customs delays from bad documents.
- Never let this happen: cargo damage.
- Never let this happen: missed vessel cut-offs.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a freight forwarder; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to freight offices, ports and airports's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
