# Continuing Professional Development Plan — Chemical Engineering

Plan and evidence continuing professional development for a practising chemical engineer. The deliverable is a CPD plan mapped to competency gaps, with evidence and reflection records.
This skill is written for a chemical engineer working with process plants and manufacturing clients in chemical plants and design offices.

## When to use
- You are a chemical engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in chemical plants and design offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a CPD plan mapped to competency gaps, with evidence and reflection records.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| HAZOP | a systematic study of hazards and operability problems |
| Yield | the amount of desired product obtained from a process |
| Mass balance | accounting for all material entering and leaving a process |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Audit current competence against the standards for chemical plants and design offices and the evidence in your recent process flow diagrams
2. **Step 2** - Choose development priorities that close real gaps — including staying current on process safety incidents — not just easy hours
3. **Step 3** - Mix formats: courses, supervised practice, case review, reading and teaching others
4. **Step 4** - After each activity, write a short reflection: what changed in your practice and what it means for plant uptime
5. **Step 5** - Keep the CPD record inspection-ready and review the plan at appraisal or revalidation time

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: process safety incidents.
- Never let this happen: runaway reactions.
- Never let this happen: environmental releases.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a chemical engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to chemical plants and design offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
