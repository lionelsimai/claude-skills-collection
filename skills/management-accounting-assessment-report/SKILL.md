# Assessment & Findings Report — Management Accounting

Assess business managers and leadership teams and write a defensible findings report as a management accountant. The deliverable is a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
This skill is written for a management accountant working with business managers and leadership teams in a finance department.

## When to use
- You are a management accountant preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a finance department.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Variance | the difference between budgeted and actual figures |
| Forecast | a prediction of future financial performance |
| Cost centre | a unit of a business tracked for its costs |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Review the intake record and management accounts so the assessment builds on verified information, not assumptions
2. **Step 2** - Carry out the assessment using ERP systems, following the recognised method for a finance department
3. **Step 3** - Compare findings against the benchmarks behind report delivery timeliness and note where evidence is strong, weak or missing
4. **Step 4** - Draft the report: findings first, then interpretation, then recommendations in priority order
5. **Step 5** - Check the draft for accuracy and for misleading forecasts; have it reviewed where the profession's standards require a second sign-off

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: misleading forecasts.
- Never let this happen: cost allocations distorted.
- Never let this happen: deadline slippage.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a management accountant; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a finance department's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
