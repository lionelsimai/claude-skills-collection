# Service Plan Design — Forensic Science

Design a realistic, measurable service plan for police, courts and legal teams as a forensic scientist. The deliverable is a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates.
This skill is written for a forensic scientist working with police, courts and legal teams in laboratories and crime scenes.

## When to use
- You are a forensic scientist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in laboratories and crime scenes.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Chain of custody | the documented movement of evidence |
| Exhibit | an item of evidence in an investigation |
| Proficiency testing | external checks of a scientist's accuracy |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Start from the assessment findings and agree goals with police, courts and legal teams — specific enough to measure against report turnaround
2. **Step 2** - Break each goal into actions sequenced for laboratories and crime scenes, naming who does what and by when
3. **Step 3** - Build the plan around examination records so progress, changes and decisions are recorded in one place
4. **Step 4** - Price or resource the plan honestly: time, laboratory instruments and materials, with contingencies for evidence contamination
5. **Step 5** - Set the review date and the trigger points that would change the plan, and confirm the plan with all parties

| Component | Details for Forensic Science |

| Core documentation | examination records; evidence logs; expert reports; court statements |
| Core tools & equipment | laboratory instruments; comparison microscopes; DNA analysis systems; scene equipment |
| People involved | police, courts and legal teams; the forensic scientist; colleagues and other services in laboratories and crime scenes |

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in laboratories and crime scenes] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [police, courts and legal teams and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in examination records.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against report turnaround.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in laboratories and crime scenes.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: evidence contamination.
- Never let this happen: interpretation overstated.
- Never let this happen: chain of custody gaps.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a forensic scientist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to laboratories and crime scenes's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
