# New Client Onboarding Pack — Mortgage Advisory

Onboard home buyers and homeowners smoothly into a mortgage adviser's service in advice firms, branches and online. The deliverable is an onboarding pack: welcome information, expectations, first steps and contacts.
This skill is written for a mortgage adviser working with home buyers and homeowners in advice firms, branches and online.

## When to use
- You are a mortgage adviser preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in advice firms, branches and online.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an onboarding pack: welcome information, expectations, first steps and contacts.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Affordability | assessment of whether a borrower can sustain repayments |
| Loan to value | the loan as a percentage of the property's value |
| Offer | the lender's formal agreement to lend |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Send a welcome pack before the first appointment: what the service does, what happens first, what to bring from fact finds
2. **Step 2** - Set expectations honestly — timescales, costs where relevant, and how progress on application-to-offer times will be shared
3. **Step 3** - Cover rights and responsibilities on both sides, including confidentiality limits and how to raise concerns
4. **Step 4** - Make the first contact easy: named contact, directions to advice firms, branches and online, and what to do if plans change
5. **Step 5** - Check in after the first session or milestone and fix friction early, before it becomes disengagement or unsuitable product recommendations

2. Detail, following the workflow above

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: unsuitable product recommendations.
- Never let this happen: affordability miscalculated.
- Never let this happen: deadlines missed losing purchases.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a mortgage adviser; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to advice firms, branches and online's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
