# Intake & Initial Assessment — Commercial Diving

Run a structured intake and initial assessment for offshore, construction and salvage clients in underwater work sites and support vessels. The deliverable is a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
This skill is written for a commercial diver working with offshore, construction and salvage clients in underwater work sites and support vessels.

## When to use
- You are a commercial diver preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in underwater work sites and support vessels.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Umbilical | the cable and hoses supplying air and communications to a diver |
| Decompression | controlled ascent stops letting gases leave the body safely |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm identity, consent and the reason for referral or engagement, and open the record that will hold dive plans
2. **Step 2** - Gather history and current circumstances from offshore, construction and salvage clients, using the standard intake structure for underwater work sites and support vessels
3. **Step 3** - Record baseline observations and measurements relevant to dive plan compliance, noting anything that changes the priority of the case
4. **Step 4** - Flag immediate concerns — especially decompression sickness — and escalate or fast-track anything urgent before proceeding
5. **Step 5** - Summarise findings for offshore, construction and salvage clients in plain language, agree the next step, and file the intake section of dive plans

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: decompression sickness.
- Never let this happen: equipment failure underwater.
- Never let this happen: communication loss.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a commercial diver; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to underwater work sites and support vessels's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
