# Stakeholder Presentation — Critical Care Nursing

Present critical care nurse findings and proposals to decision-makers and stakeholders. The deliverable is a presentation structure with evidence summary and decision asks.
This skill is written for a critical care nurse working with critically ill patients and their families in an intensive care unit.

## When to use
- You are a critical care nurse preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in an intensive care unit.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a presentation structure with evidence summary and decision asks.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Sedation score | a scale rating how deeply a patient is sedated |
| Weaning | gradually reducing ventilator support |
| Delirium | acute confusion common in critical illness |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Open with the decision or conclusion, then the evidence — busy stakeholders should not wait for the point
2. **Step 2** - Support claims with data from nursing care plans and movement in observation chart completion, with sources and limits stated
3. **Step 3** - Address the hard parts directly, including unrecognised deterioration and what it would take to reduce it
4. **Step 4** - Make the ask specific: what decision, by when, with what resources for work in an intensive care unit
5. **Step 5** - Follow up in writing the same day: decisions made, actions agreed, owners and dates

## Profession-Specific Components
| Component | Details for Critical Care Nursing |
|-----------|-------------------------------|
| Core documentation | nursing care plans; observation charts; ventilation records; handover notes |
| Core tools & equipment | ventilators; infusion pumps; continuous monitoring; renal replacement machines |
| People involved | critically ill patients and their families; the critical care nurse; colleagues and other services in an intensive care unit |
| Primary record | nursing care plans — keep it current, dated and attributable |

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in an intensive care unit] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [critically ill patients and their families and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a presentation structure with evidence summary and decision asks, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in nursing care plans.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against observation chart completion.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in an intensive care unit.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: unrecognised deterioration.
- Never let this happen: pressure damage.
- Never let this happen: device-associated infection.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a critical care nurse; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to an intensive care unit's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
