# Mentoring & Training Junior Staff — Conservation Architecture

Train and mentor junior colleagues toward competent, safe conservation architect practice. The deliverable is a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
This skill is written for a conservation architect working with heritage bodies, owners of historic buildings and councils in historic buildings and design studios.

## When to use
- You are a conservation architect preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in historic buildings and design studios.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Listed building | a building protected by law for its heritage value |
| Fabric | the physical material of a historic building |
| Reversibility | repairs that can be undone without harming original material |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Assess the trainee's current level honestly against the competencies required in historic buildings and design studios
2. **Step 2** - Sequence learning from observation to supervised practice to independent work, using survey equipment under supervision first
3. **Step 3** - Set explicit sign-off criteria for high-risk tasks — especially anything touching inappropriate repairs damaging fabric — and never sign off early
4. **Step 4** - Give feedback from direct observation and from condition surveys, specific and timely, with strengths named too
5. **Step 5** - Record progress, keep the training file current, and escalate persistent gaps through supervision, not gossip

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: inappropriate repairs damaging fabric.
- Never let this happen: consent refusals.
- Never let this happen: hidden defects.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a conservation architect; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to historic buildings and design studios's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
