# Handover Briefing — Oil & Gas Operations

Hand over energy companies and regulators or active work to another professional without losing critical detail. The deliverable is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
This skill is written for a petroleum operations engineer working with energy companies and regulators in offshore platforms and onshore terminals.

## When to use
- You are a petroleum operations engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in offshore platforms and onshore terminals.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Permit to work | a formal authorisation for hazardous work |
| Well control | preventing uncontrolled flow from a well |
| Shutdown | a planned or emergency stop of production |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Summarise the current position from production reports: what is done, what is in progress, what is waiting
2. **Step 2** - Highlight live risks first — hydrocarbon releases — and the controls or watchpoints already in place
3. **Step 3** - State the next actions with owners and deadlines, and the results expected for production rates
4. **Step 4** - Walk the receiving professional through the brief, answer questions, and confirm understanding
5. **Step 5** - Record that handover happened, who accepted it, and where the full record lives in offshore platforms and onshore terminals

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in offshore platforms and onshore terminals] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [energy companies and regulators and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: hydrocarbon releases.
- Never let this happen: well control incidents.
- Never let this happen: permit breaches.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a petroleum operations engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to offshore platforms and onshore terminals's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
