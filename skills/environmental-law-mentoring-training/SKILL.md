# Mentoring & Training Junior Staff — Environmental Law

Train and mentor junior colleagues toward competent, safe environmental solicitor practice. The deliverable is a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
This skill is written for a environmental solicitor working with developers, businesses and community groups in a law firm and planning inquiries.

## When to use
- You are a environmental solicitor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a law firm and planning inquiries.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Environmental permit | official permission for activities affecting the environment |
| EIA | environmental impact assessment of a proposed development |
| Contaminated land | land polluted by previous use |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Assess the trainee's current level honestly against the competencies required in a law firm and planning inquiries
2. **Step 2** - Sequence learning from observation to supervised practice to independent work, using planning portals under supervision first
3. **Step 3** - Set explicit sign-off criteria for high-risk tasks — especially anything touching permit breaches unadvised — and never sign off early
4. **Step 4** - Give feedback from direct observation and from permit applications, specific and timely, with strengths named too
5. **Step 5** - Record progress, keep the training file current, and escalate persistent gaps through supervision, not gossip

## Output Format

2. Detail, following the workflow above

4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: permit breaches unadvised.
- Never let this happen: consultation deadlines missed.
- Never let this happen: contaminated land liabilities.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a environmental solicitor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a law firm and planning inquiries's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
