# Tools & Equipment Checklist — Gastroenterology

Select, check and maintain the tools and equipment a gastroenterologist relies on. The deliverable is an equipment checklist with inspection points, maintenance dates and replacement triggers.
This skill is written for a gastroenterologist working with patients with digestive system conditions in an endoscopy unit and gastroenterology clinic.

## When to use
- You are a gastroenterologist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in an endoscopy unit and gastroenterology clinic.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an equipment checklist with inspection points, maintenance dates and replacement triggers.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Endoscopy | examining the digestive tract with a flexible camera |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - List the essential kit for work in an endoscopy unit and gastroenterology clinic, centred on endoscopes, with what each item is used for
2. **Step 2** - Define pre-use checks: condition, calibration or charge status, cleanliness and safety features
3. **Step 3** - Set maintenance and replacement intervals based on usage and manufacturer guidance, and log them against endoscopy reports
4. **Step 4** - Quarantine faulty items immediately — faulty kit is a direct route to missed gastrointestinal bleeding
5. **Step 5** - Review the kit list quarterly against the work actually done and retire or add items deliberately

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed gastrointestinal bleeding.
- Never let this happen: sedation complications.
- Never let this happen: delayed cancer pathway.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a gastroenterologist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to an endoscopy unit and gastroenterology clinic's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
