# Ethics & Professional Standards — Primary Education

Apply the ethical codes and professional standards that govern a primary school teacher. The deliverable is an ethics guide with principles, common dilemmas and decision steps.
This skill is written for a primary school teacher working with primary-aged pupils and their parents in a primary school classroom.

## When to use
- You are a primary school teacher preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a primary school classroom.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an ethics guide with principles, common dilemmas and decision steps.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Know the code that applies in a primary school classroom: consent, confidentiality, honesty, competence and conflicts of interest
2. **Step 2** - Work through dilemmas with a framework: facts, stakeholders, options, code provisions, then decide and record
3. **Step 3** - Protect primary-aged pupils and their parents first where duties conflict, and be alert to how safeguarding disclosures mishandled can arise from ethical shortcuts
4. **Step 4** - Declare conflicts of interest early and manage them transparently rather than hoping they stay invisible
5. **Step 5** - Raise concerns about others' practice through the proper channels, with evidence from lesson plans, and support colleagues who do the same

## Output Format

The finished output is an ethics guide with principles, common dilemmas and decision steps, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against pupil progress measures.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: safeguarding disclosures mishandled.
- Never let this happen: gaps in progress unaddressed.
- Never let this happen: workload overload.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a primary school teacher; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a primary school classroom's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
