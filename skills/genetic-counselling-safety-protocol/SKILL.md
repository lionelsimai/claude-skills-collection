# Safety Protocol — Genetic Counselling

Work safely in a clinical genetics service: a practical safety protocol for a genetic counsellor and individuals and families with or at risk of inherited conditions. The deliverable is a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions.
This skill is written for a genetic counsellor working with individuals and families with or at risk of inherited conditions in a clinical genetics service.

## When to use
- You are a genetic counsellor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a clinical genetics service.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a written safety protocol with checks, responsibilities and emergency actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Pedigree | a diagram of a family's medical history across generations |
| Carrier | a person with one copy of a gene variant who may pass it on |
| Variant | a difference in a DNA sequence |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Identify the hazards present in a clinical genetics service for staff, individuals and families with or at risk of inherited conditions and visitors, starting with distress unaddressed after results
2. **Step 2** - Write controls in order: eliminate, substitute, engineer, administrate, then protective equipment such as pedigree drawing tools
3. **Step 3** - Set pre-start checks and stop-work authority — anyone can halt work that looks unsafe, without penalty
4. **Step 4** - Define what happens when something goes wrong: first response, escalation, recording in family histories
5. **Step 5** - Review the protocol after every incident and at least annually; brief and re-brief everyone it covers

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: distress unaddressed after results.
- Never let this happen: family implications missed.
- Never let this happen: consent inadequacies.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a genetic counsellor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a clinical genetics service's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
