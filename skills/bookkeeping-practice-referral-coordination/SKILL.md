# Referral & Coordination — Bookkeeping Practice

Refer small businesses, sole traders and accountants onward and coordinate with other professionals and services. The deliverable is a referral pack and a coordination log with responses tracked.
This skill is written for a bookkeeper working with small businesses, sole traders and accountants in a bookkeeping practice and client premises.

## When to use
- You are a bookkeeper preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a bookkeeping practice and client premises.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a referral pack and a coordination log with responses tracked.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Reconciliation | matching records against bank statements to find differences |
| Ledger | the record of transactions by category |
| Accruals | recognising income or costs in the period they relate to |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm the referral is appropriate: what small businesses, sole traders and accountants need that sits outside the bookkeeper scope in a bookkeeping practice and client premises
2. **Step 2** - Prepare the referral from bank reconciliations — relevant history, current status, the specific question being asked
3. **Step 3** - With consent, send the referral and log who received it and when; chase non-responses on a set schedule
4. **Step 4** - Share incoming findings back into the main record so the plan and reconciliation timeliness stay joined up
5. **Step 5** - Close the loop with small businesses, sole traders and accountants: explain what happens next, who owns it, and what to do if things worsen — mindful of miscoded transactions

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: miscoded transactions.
- Never let this happen: missing source documents.
- Never let this happen: VAT errors.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a bookkeeper; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a bookkeeping practice and client premises's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
