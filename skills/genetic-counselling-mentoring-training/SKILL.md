# Mentoring & Training Junior Staff — Genetic Counselling

Train and mentor junior colleagues toward competent, safe genetic counsellor practice. The deliverable is a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
This skill is written for a genetic counsellor working with individuals and families with or at risk of inherited conditions in a clinical genetics service.

## When to use
- You are a genetic counsellor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a clinical genetics service.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Pedigree | a diagram of a family's medical history across generations |
| Carrier | a person with one copy of a gene variant who may pass it on |
| Variant | a difference in a DNA sequence |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Assess the trainee's current level honestly against the competencies required in a clinical genetics service
2. **Step 2** - Sequence learning from observation to supervised practice to independent work, using pedigree drawing tools under supervision first
3. **Step 3** - Set explicit sign-off criteria for high-risk tasks — especially anything touching distress unaddressed after results — and never sign off early
4. **Step 4** - Give feedback from direct observation and from family histories, specific and timely, with strengths named too
5. **Step 5** - Record progress, keep the training file current, and escalate persistent gaps through supervision, not gossip

## Profession-Specific Components
| Component | Details for Genetic Counselling |
|-----------|-------------------------------|
| Core documentation | family histories; risk assessments; test consent records; counselling notes |
| Core tools & equipment | pedigree drawing tools; genetic test databases; telehealth platforms; sample tracking systems |
| People involved | individuals and families with or at risk of inherited conditions; the genetic counsellor; colleagues and other services in a clinical genetics service |
| Primary record | family histories — keep it current, dated and attributable |

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a clinical genetics service] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [individuals and families with or at risk of inherited conditions and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a training plan with competencies, supervised milestones and sign-off criteria, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in family histories.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against risk assessment accuracy.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in a clinical genetics service.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: distress unaddressed after results.
- Never let this happen: family implications missed.
- Never let this happen: consent inadequacies.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a genetic counsellor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a clinical genetics service's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
