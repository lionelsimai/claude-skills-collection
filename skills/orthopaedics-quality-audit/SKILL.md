# Quality Audit — Orthopaedics

Audit the quality of orthopaedic surgeon work in an orthopaedic theatre and fracture clinic and drive measurable improvement. The deliverable is an audit report with scores, evidence and an improvement plan.
This skill is written for a orthopaedic surgeon working with patients with bone, joint and muscle injuries or conditions in an orthopaedic theatre and fracture clinic.

## When to use
- You are a orthopaedic surgeon preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in an orthopaedic theatre and fracture clinic.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an audit report with scores, evidence and an improvement plan.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Fracture | a break in a bone |
| Arthroplasty | surgical replacement of a joint |
| Compartment syndrome | dangerous pressure build-up within a muscle compartment |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Define the audit sample and criteria up front, anchored to surgical site infection rate and the standards for an orthopaedic theatre and fracture clinic
2. **Step 2** - Review a sample of operation notes and observe practice where permitted, scoring consistently
3. **Step 3** - Separate isolated errors from patterns — patterns point to system fixes, not individual blame
4. **Step 4** - Report findings with evidence, and agree improvement actions that address the root causes, including wrong-site surgery
5. **Step 5** - Re-audit after the agreed interval and publish the change in scores so improvement is visible

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: wrong-site surgery.
- Never let this happen: compartment syndrome missed.
- Never let this happen: post-operative infection.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a orthopaedic surgeon; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to an orthopaedic theatre and fracture clinic's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
