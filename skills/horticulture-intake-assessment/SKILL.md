# Intake & Initial Assessment — Horticulture

Run a structured intake and initial assessment for garden centres, landscapers and the public in nurseries, glasshouses and gardens. The deliverable is a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
This skill is written for a horticulturist and grower working with garden centres, landscapers and the public in nurseries, glasshouses and gardens.

## When to use
- You are a horticulturist and grower preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in nurseries, glasshouses and gardens.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm identity, consent and the reason for referral or engagement, and open the record that will hold planting plans
2. **Step 2** - Gather history and current circumstances from garden centres, landscapers and the public, using the standard intake structure for nurseries, glasshouses and gardens
3. **Step 3** - Record baseline observations and measurements relevant to crop success rates, noting anything that changes the priority of the case
4. **Step 4** - Flag immediate concerns — especially pest outbreaks — and escalate or fast-track anything urgent before proceeding
5. **Step 5** - Summarise findings for garden centres, landscapers and the public in plain language, agree the next step, and file the intake section of planting plans

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: pest outbreaks.
- Never let this happen: plant disease spread.
- Never let this happen: irrigation failures.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a horticulturist and grower; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to nurseries, glasshouses and gardens's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
