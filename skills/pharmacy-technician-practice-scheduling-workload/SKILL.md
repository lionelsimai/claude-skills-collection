# Scheduling & Workload Management — Pharmacy Practice

Plan schedules, caseloads and turnaround times for a pharmacy technician in a community pharmacy. The deliverable is a workable schedule template with priorities, buffers and escalation rules.
This skill is written for a pharmacy technician working with patients and customers collecting medicines and advice in a community pharmacy.

## When to use
- You are a pharmacy technician preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a community pharmacy.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a workable schedule template with priorities, buffers and escalation rules.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Dispensing | preparing and supplying medicines against a prescription |
| Controlled drug | a medicine subject to strict legal controls |
| SOP | standard operating procedure |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Map demand: appointment or job types, durations, and the deadlines that drive dispensing accuracy rate
2. **Step 2** - Build the schedule around fixed commitments in a community pharmacy first, then fit flexible work around them
3. **Step 3** - Protect buffer time for overruns, urgent cases and the admin behind dispensing records — schedules without buffers fail by week two
4. **Step 4** - Define triage rules so urgent work (for example anything involving dispensing errors) displaces routine work predictably
5. **Step 5** - Review the schedule weekly against actuals and adjust booking rules, staffing or dispensing systems availability

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a community pharmacy] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients and customers collecting medicines and advice and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a workable schedule template with priorities, buffers and escalation rules, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: dispensing errors.
- Never let this happen: controlled drug discrepancies.
- Never let this happen: expired stock supplied.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a pharmacy technician; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a community pharmacy's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
