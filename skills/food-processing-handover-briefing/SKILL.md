# Handover Briefing — Food Processing

Hand over retailers, wholesalers and consumers or active work to another professional without losing critical detail. The deliverable is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
This skill is written for a food production manager working with retailers, wholesalers and consumers in food factories and processing plants.

## When to use
- You are a food production manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in food factories and processing plants.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Traceability | following a product through every production stage |
| Shelf life | how long a product remains safe and acceptable |
| Critical control point | a step where control prevents a food safety hazard |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Summarise the current position from production plans: what is done, what is in progress, what is waiting
2. **Step 2** - Highlight live risks first — contamination incidents — and the controls or watchpoints already in place
3. **Step 3** - State the next actions with owners and deadlines, and the results expected for production yield
4. **Step 4** - Walk the receiving professional through the brief, answer questions, and confirm understanding
5. **Step 5** - Record that handover happened, who accepted it, and where the full record lives in food factories and processing plants

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in food factories and processing plants] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [retailers, wholesalers and consumers and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

- Never let this happen: traceability failures.
- Never let this happen: labelling errors.
