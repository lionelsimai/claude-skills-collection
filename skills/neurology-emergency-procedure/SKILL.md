# Emergency Response Procedure — Neurology

Respond to emergencies in a neurology department and outpatient clinic calmly, in role and in the right order. The deliverable is an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
This skill is written for a neurologist working with patients with brain, spinal cord and nerve disorders in a neurology department and outpatient clinic.

## When to use
- You are a neurologist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a neurology department and outpatient clinic.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| EEG | a recording of electrical activity in the brain |
| Lesion | an area of damaged tissue |
| Aura | warning symptoms some patients get before a seizure or migraine |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Recognise the emergency early: know the presentations that matter for patients with brain, spinal cord and nerve disorders, especially missed stroke presentations
2. **Step 2** - Take the immediate actions a neurologist is trained and authorised for, using EEG machines as drilled
3. **Step 3** - Call for help with a structured message: location in a neurology department and outpatient clinic, situation, what has been done, what is needed
4. **Step 4** - Keep patients with brain, spinal cord and nerve disorders and bystanders informed and safe while help arrives; hand over with a concise summary
5. **Step 5** - Record the event fully in neurological examination records afterwards, debrief the team, and update the procedure with lessons learned

## Output Format

The finished output is an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed stroke presentations.
- Never let this happen: status epilepticus delays.
- Never let this happen: medication interactions.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a neurologist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a neurology department and outpatient clinic's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
