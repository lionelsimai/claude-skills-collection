# Ethics & Professional Standards — Facilities Management

Apply the ethical codes and professional standards that govern a facilities manager. The deliverable is an ethics guide with principles, common dilemmas and decision steps.
This skill is written for a facilities manager working with building occupants, owners and service teams in commercial buildings and campuses.

## When to use
- You are a facilities manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in commercial buildings and campuses.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an ethics guide with principles, common dilemmas and decision steps.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

| Term | Meaning |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Know the code that applies in commercial buildings and campuses: consent, confidentiality, honesty, competence and conflicts of interest
2. **Step 2** - Work through dilemmas with a framework: facts, stakeholders, options, code provisions, then decide and record
3. **Step 3** - Protect building occupants, owners and service teams first where duties conflict, and be alert to how life-safety systems unmaintained can arise from ethical shortcuts
4. **Step 4** - Declare conflicts of interest early and manage them transparently rather than hoping they stay invisible
5. **Step 5** - Raise concerns about others' practice through the proper channels, with evidence from maintenance schedules, and support colleagues who do the same

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: life-safety systems unmaintained.
- Never let this happen: contractor failures.
- Never let this happen: energy waste.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a facilities manager; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to commercial buildings and campuses's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
