# Handover Briefing — Biomedical Engineering

Hand over hospitals, device manufacturers and patients or active work to another professional without losing critical detail. The deliverable is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
This skill is written for a biomedical engineer working with hospitals, device manufacturers and patients in hospital engineering departments and laboratories.

## When to use
- You are a biomedical engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in hospital engineering departments and laboratories.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Calibration | adjusting a device to measure or perform accurately |
| Asset register | the inventory of medical devices and their status |
| Adverse incident | an event where a device may have caused harm |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Summarise the current position from device evaluations: what is done, what is in progress, what is waiting
2. **Step 2** - Highlight live risks first — device failures harming patients — and the controls or watchpoints already in place
3. **Step 3** - State the next actions with owners and deadlines, and the results expected for device uptime
4. **Step 4** - Walk the receiving professional through the brief, answer questions, and confirm understanding
5. **Step 5** - Record that handover happened, who accepted it, and where the full record lives in hospital engineering departments and laboratories

| Core tools & equipment | test equipment; asset management systems; design software; calibration tools |
| People involved | hospitals, device manufacturers and patients; the biomedical engineer; colleagues and other services in hospital engineering departments and laboratories |

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in hospital engineering departments and laboratories] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [hospitals, device manufacturers and patients and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in device evaluations.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against device uptime.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in hospital engineering departments and laboratories.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: device failures harming patients.
- Never let this happen: maintenance backlogs.
- Never let this happen: unapproved modifications.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a biomedical engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to hospital engineering departments and laboratories's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
