# Intake & Initial Assessment — Higher Education Lecturing

Run a structured intake and initial assessment for undergraduate and postgraduate students in a university department. The deliverable is a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
This skill is written for a university lecturer working with undergraduate and postgraduate students in a university department.

## When to use
- You are a university lecturer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a university department.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Module | a self-contained unit of study within a degree |
| Formative feedback | comments that help students improve before final assessment |
| External examiner | an independent academic who checks standards |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm identity, consent and the reason for referral or engagement, and open the record that will hold module outlines
2. **Step 2** - Gather history and current circumstances from undergraduate and postgraduate students, using the standard intake structure for a university department
3. **Step 3** - Record baseline observations and measurements relevant to student satisfaction scores, noting anything that changes the priority of the case
4. **Step 4** - Flag immediate concerns — especially marking delays — and escalate or fast-track anything urgent before proceeding
5. **Step 5** - Summarise findings for undergraduate and postgraduate students in plain language, agree the next step, and file the intake section of module outlines

2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: marking delays.
- Never let this happen: assessment design flaws.
- Never let this happen: student disengagement unnoticed.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a university lecturer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a university department's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
