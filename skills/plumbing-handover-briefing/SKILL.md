# Handover Briefing — Plumbing

Hand over homeowners, landlords and commercial clients or active work to another professional without losing critical detail. The deliverable is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
This skill is written for a plumber working with homeowners, landlords and commercial clients in homes, building sites and commercial premises.

## When to use
- You are a plumber preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in homes, building sites and commercial premises.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| First fix | the initial installation stage of pipework |
| Pressure test | checking a system for leaks under pressure |
| Backflow | contaminated water flowing back into the clean supply |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Summarise the current position from job sheets: what is done, what is in progress, what is waiting
2. **Step 2** - Highlight live risks first — water damage from leaks — and the controls or watchpoints already in place
3. **Step 3** - State the next actions with owners and deadlines, and the results expected for first-time fix rate
4. **Step 4** - Walk the receiving professional through the brief, answer questions, and confirm understanding
5. **Step 5** - Record that handover happened, who accepted it, and where the full record lives in homes, building sites and commercial premises

- Never let this happen: water damage from leaks.
- Never let this happen: gas safety risks.
- Never let this happen: scalding from faulty systems.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a plumber; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to homes, building sites and commercial premises's rules.
