# Quality Audit — Procurement Practice

Audit the quality of procurement officer work in procurement departments and drive measurable improvement. The deliverable is an audit report with scores, evidence and an improvement plan.
This skill is written for a procurement officer working with businesses and public bodies buying goods and services in procurement departments.

## When to use
- You are a procurement officer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in procurement departments.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an audit report with scores, evidence and an improvement plan.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Tender | a formal process inviting suppliers to bid |
| Specification | the detailed requirement a supplier must meet |
| Total cost | purchase price plus all associated costs of ownership |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Define the audit sample and criteria up front, anchored to savings delivered and the standards for procurement departments
2. **Step 2** - Review a sample of purchase orders and observe practice where permitted, scoring consistently
3. **Step 3** - Separate isolated errors from patterns — patterns point to system fixes, not individual blame
4. **Step 4** - Report findings with evidence, and agree improvement actions that address the root causes, including supplier failure
5. **Step 5** - Re-audit after the agreed interval and publish the change in scores so improvement is visible

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in procurement departments] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [businesses and public bodies buying goods and services and other stakeholders]

The finished output is an audit report with scores, evidence and an improvement plan, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan

2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against savings delivered.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in procurement departments.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: supplier failure.
- Never let this happen: fraud in purchasing.
- Never let this happen: contract deadlines missed.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a procurement officer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to procurement departments's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
