# Risk Assessment — Banking Risk & Compliance

Identify, score and control the risks in work for banks, regulators and boards in bank risk and compliance departments. The deliverable is a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners.
This skill is written for a risk and compliance officer working with banks, regulators and boards in bank risk and compliance departments.

## When to use
- You are a risk and compliance officer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in bank risk and compliance departments.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Risk register | the recorded list of risks, owners and controls |
| Three lines | the model separating risk ownership, oversight and audit |
| Conduct risk | the risk of harm to customers or markets from behaviour |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Map the activity step by step and list what could go wrong at each stage for banks, regulators and boards and staff
2. **Step 2** - Score each risk for likelihood and impact, giving special weight to undetected misconduct
3. **Step 3** - For every high risk, define a control that removes, reduces or contains it, using risk systems where relevant
4. **Step 4** - Record the assessment in risk registers, assign an owner to each control, and set a review date
5. **Step 5** - Brief everyone involved before work starts; stop and reassess if conditions in bank risk and compliance departments change

2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: undetected misconduct.
- Never let this happen: late regulatory returns.
- Never let this happen: control failures.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a risk and compliance officer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to bank risk and compliance departments's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
