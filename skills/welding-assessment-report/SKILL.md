# Assessment & Findings Report — Welding

Assess fabrication companies, construction and repair clients and write a defensible findings report as a welder. The deliverable is a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
This skill is written for a welder working with fabrication companies, construction and repair clients in workshops, shipyards and construction sites.

## When to use
- You are a welder preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in workshops, shipyards and construction sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| WPS | a weld procedure specification defining how a weld is made |
| Penetration | how deeply a weld fuses into the base metal |
| Slag | a residue that must be removed from some welds |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Review the intake record and weld procedure specifications so the assessment builds on verified information, not assumptions
2. **Step 2** - Carry out the assessment using welding machines, following the recognised method for workshops, shipyards and construction sites
3. **Step 3** - Compare findings against the benchmarks behind weld defect rates and note where evidence is strong, weak or missing
4. **Step 4** - Draft the report: findings first, then interpretation, then recommendations in priority order
5. **Step 5** - Check the draft for accuracy and for weld failures in service; have it reviewed where the profession's standards require a second sign-off

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: weld failures in service.
- Never let this happen: eye and skin burns.
- Never let this happen: fumes exposure.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a welder; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to workshops, shipyards and construction sites's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
