# Assessment & Findings Report — Aerospace Engineering

Assess aircraft manufacturers, airlines and space agencies and write a defensible findings report as a aerospace engineer. The deliverable is a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
This skill is written for a aerospace engineer working with aircraft manufacturers, airlines and space agencies in design offices, test facilities and hangars.

## When to use
- You are a aerospace engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in design offices, test facilities and hangars.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured assessment report with findings, evidence and recommendations.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Certification | official approval that a design meets safety standards |
| Fatigue life | how long a component withstands repeated loading |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Review the intake record and design analyses so the assessment builds on verified information, not assumptions
2. **Step 2** - Carry out the assessment using simulation software, following the recognised method for design offices, test facilities and hangars
3. **Step 3** - Compare findings against the benchmarks behind test programme milestones and note where evidence is strong, weak or missing
4. **Step 4** - Draft the report: findings first, then interpretation, then recommendations in priority order
5. **Step 5** - Check the draft for accuracy and for test incidents; have it reviewed where the profession's standards require a second sign-off

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: test incidents.
- Never let this happen: certification delays.
- Never let this happen: fatigue failures.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a aerospace engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to design offices, test facilities and hangars's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
