# Supplies & Procurement — Radiology

Specify, source and manage the supplies and materials used in a hospital imaging department. The deliverable is a procurement list with specifications, stock levels and supplier checks.
This skill is written for a radiologist working with patients referred for diagnostic imaging and referring clinicians in a hospital imaging department.

## When to use
- You are a radiologist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a hospital imaging department.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a procurement list with specifications, stock levels and supplier checks.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| PACS | the system storing and displaying medical images |
| Contrast | a substance given to make structures visible on scans |
| Modality | a type of imaging, such as CT, MRI or ultrasound |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - List what the work consumes, drawing on imaging reports and the kit centred on CT scanners, with exact specifications
2. **Step 2** - Set minimum stock levels from usage rates and lead times so work never stalls waiting for supplies
3. **Step 3** - Compare suppliers on quality, reliability and total cost — not unit price alone — and check credentials
4. **Step 4** - Receive and inspect deliveries; reject or quarantine substandard stock before it can contribute to missed findings on imaging
5. **Step 5** - Track usage and waste monthly; adjust order quantities and challenge specifications that no longer fit

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed findings on imaging.
- Never let this happen: radiation overexposure.
- Never let this happen: contrast reactions.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a radiologist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a hospital imaging department's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
