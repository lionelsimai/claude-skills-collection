# Compliance & Standards Checklist — Customs Brokerage

Check work in customs offices and freight operations against the laws, standards and codes that govern a customs broker. The deliverable is a completed compliance checklist with gaps and corrective actions.
This skill is written for a customs broker working with importers and exporters in customs offices and freight operations.

## When to use
- You are a customs broker preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in customs offices and freight operations.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed compliance checklist with gaps and corrective actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Tariff classification | assigning the correct commodity code to goods |
| Duty | tax charged on imported goods |
| Origin | the country goods are deemed to come from for trade rules |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - List the obligations that apply: legislation, regulator guidance, professional codes and local policy in customs offices and freight operations
2. **Step 2** - Work through each obligation against current practice and customs declarations, marking compliant, partial or missing
3. **Step 3** - Investigate every gap — especially anything touching misclassification penalties — and record the evidence for the judgement
4. **Step 4** - Turn gaps into corrective actions with owners and deadlines, proportionate to the risk
5. **Step 5** - Re-check completed actions, file the checklist, and diarise the next compliance review

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: misclassification penalties.
- Never let this happen: clearance delays.
- Never let this happen: prohibited goods shipped.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a customs broker; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to customs offices and freight operations's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
