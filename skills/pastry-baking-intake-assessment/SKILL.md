# Intake & Initial Assessment — Pastry & Baking

Run a structured intake and initial assessment for cafe and restaurant customers, retailers in bakeries and patisserie kitchens. The deliverable is a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
This skill is written for a pastry chef and baker working with cafe and restaurant customers, retailers in bakeries and patisserie kitchens.

## When to use
- You are a pastry chef and baker preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in bakeries and patisserie kitchens.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm identity, consent and the reason for referral or engagement, and open the record that will hold recipes and production sheets
2. **Step 2** - Gather history and current circumstances from cafe and restaurant customers, retailers, using the standard intake structure for bakeries and patisserie kitchens
3. **Step 3** - Record baseline observations and measurements relevant to production waste rates, noting anything that changes the priority of the case
4. **Step 4** - Flag immediate concerns — especially allergen cross-contact — and escalate or fast-track anything urgent before proceeding
5. **Step 5** - Summarise findings for cafe and restaurant customers, retailers in plain language, agree the next step, and file the intake section of recipes and production sheets

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: allergen cross-contact.
- Never let this happen: burns from ovens.
- Never let this happen: stale stock losses.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a pastry chef and baker; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to bakeries and patisserie kitchens's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
