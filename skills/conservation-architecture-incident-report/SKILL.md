# Incident Reporting & Response — Conservation Architecture

Respond to, record and learn from incidents involving heritage bodies, owners of historic buildings and councils in historic buildings and design studios. The deliverable is a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list.
This skill is written for a conservation architect working with heritage bodies, owners of historic buildings and councils in historic buildings and design studios.

## When to use
- You are a conservation architect preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in historic buildings and design studios.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Listed building | a building protected by law for its heritage value |
| Fabric | the physical material of a historic building |
| Reversibility | repairs that can be undone without harming original material |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Make the situation safe first; attend to people before paperwork, and escalate if inappropriate repairs damaging fabric is still live
2. **Step 2** - Record facts while they are fresh: what happened, when, who was involved, what was observed — in condition surveys
3. **Step 3** - Notify the people and bodies who must know, within the timeframes that apply in historic buildings and design studios
4. **Step 4** - Support those affected, including honest communication with heritage bodies, owners of historic buildings and councils about what happened and what happens next
5. **Step 5** - Analyse causes without blame, agree preventive actions with owners and dates, and track them to closure

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: inappropriate repairs damaging fabric.
- Never let this happen: consent refusals.
- Never let this happen: hidden defects.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a conservation architect; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to historic buildings and design studios's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
