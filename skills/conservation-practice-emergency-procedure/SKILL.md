# Emergency Response Procedure — Conservation Practice

Respond to emergencies in nature reserves and countryside sites calmly, in role and in the right order. The deliverable is an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
This skill is written for a conservation officer working with landowners, visitors and conservation charities in nature reserves and countryside sites.

## When to use
- You are a conservation officer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in nature reserves and countryside sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

| Term | Meaning |

| Habitat | the natural home of a species |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Recognise the emergency early: know the presentations that matter for landowners, visitors and conservation charities, especially habitat damage from works
2. **Step 2** - Take the immediate actions a conservation officer is trained and authorised for, using survey equipment as drilled
3. **Step 3** - Call for help with a structured message: location in nature reserves and countryside sites, situation, what has been done, what is needed
4. **Step 4** - Keep landowners, visitors and conservation charities and bystanders informed and safe while help arrives; hand over with a concise summary
5. **Step 5** - Record the event fully in management plans afterwards, debrief the team, and update the procedure with lessons learned

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: habitat damage from works.
- Never let this happen: invasive species spread.
- Never let this happen: public safety incidents.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a conservation officer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to nature reserves and countryside sites's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
