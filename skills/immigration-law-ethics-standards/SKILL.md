# Ethics & Professional Standards — Immigration Law

Apply the ethical codes and professional standards that govern a immigration solicitor. The deliverable is an ethics guide with principles, common dilemmas and decision steps.
This skill is written for a immigration solicitor working with individuals, families and employers navigating immigration rules in a law firm and immigration tribunals.

## When to use
- You are a immigration solicitor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a law firm and immigration tribunals.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an ethics guide with principles, common dilemmas and decision steps.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

| Term | Meaning |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Know the code that applies in a law firm and immigration tribunals: consent, confidentiality, honesty, competence and conflicts of interest
2. **Step 2** - Work through dilemmas with a framework: facts, stakeholders, options, code provisions, then decide and record
3. **Step 3** - Protect individuals, families and employers navigating immigration rules first where duties conflict, and be alert to how missed application deadlines can arise from ethical shortcuts
4. **Step 4** - Declare conflicts of interest early and manage them transparently rather than hoping they stay invisible
5. **Step 5** - Raise concerns about others' practice through the proper channels, with evidence from application bundles, and support colleagues who do the same

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed application deadlines.
- Never let this happen: incorrect evidence submitted.
- Never let this happen: status expiry overlooked.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a immigration solicitor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a law firm and immigration tribunals's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
