# Service Plan Design — Aerospace Engineering

Design a realistic, measurable service plan for aircraft manufacturers, airlines and space agencies as a aerospace engineer. The deliverable is a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates.
This skill is written for a aerospace engineer working with aircraft manufacturers, airlines and space agencies in design offices, test facilities and hangars.

## When to use
- You are a aerospace engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in design offices, test facilities and hangars.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Certification | official approval that a design meets safety standards |
| Fatigue life | how long a component withstands repeated loading |
| Thrust | the force produced by an engine |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Start from the assessment findings and agree goals with aircraft manufacturers, airlines and space agencies — specific enough to measure against test programme milestones
2. **Step 2** - Break each goal into actions sequenced for design offices, test facilities and hangars, naming who does what and by when
3. **Step 3** - Build the plan around design analyses so progress, changes and decisions are recorded in one place
4. **Step 4** - Price or resource the plan honestly: time, simulation software and materials, with contingencies for test incidents
5. **Step 5** - Set the review date and the trigger points that would change the plan, and confirm the plan with all parties

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in design offices, test facilities and hangars] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [aircraft manufacturers, airlines and space agencies and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in design analyses.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against test programme milestones.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in design offices, test facilities and hangars.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: test incidents.
- Never let this happen: certification delays.
- Never let this happen: fatigue failures.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a aerospace engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to design offices, test facilities and hangars's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
