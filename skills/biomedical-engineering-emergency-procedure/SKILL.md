# Emergency Response Procedure — Biomedical Engineering

Respond to emergencies in hospital engineering departments and laboratories calmly, in role and in the right order. The deliverable is an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
This skill is written for a biomedical engineer working with hospitals, device manufacturers and patients in hospital engineering departments and laboratories.

## When to use
- You are a biomedical engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in hospital engineering departments and laboratories.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

| Term | Meaning |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Recognise the emergency early: know the presentations that matter for hospitals, device manufacturers and patients, especially device failures harming patients
2. **Step 2** - Take the immediate actions a biomedical engineer is trained and authorised for, using test equipment as drilled
3. **Step 3** - Call for help with a structured message: location in hospital engineering departments and laboratories, situation, what has been done, what is needed
4. **Step 4** - Keep hospitals, device manufacturers and patients and bystanders informed and safe while help arrives; hand over with a concise summary
5. **Step 5** - Record the event fully in device evaluations afterwards, debrief the team, and update the procedure with lessons learned

- Never let this happen: device failures harming patients.
- Never let this happen: maintenance backlogs.
- Never let this happen: unapproved modifications.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a biomedical engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to hospital engineering departments and laboratories's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
