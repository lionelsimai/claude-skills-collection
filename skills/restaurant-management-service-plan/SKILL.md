# Service Plan Design — Restaurant Management

Design a realistic, measurable service plan for diners, kitchen and front-of-house teams as a restaurant manager. The deliverable is a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates.
This skill is written for a restaurant manager working with diners, kitchen and front-of-house teams in restaurants and cafes.

## When to use
- You are a restaurant manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in restaurants and cafes.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

| Term | Meaning |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Start from the assessment findings and agree goals with diners, kitchen and front-of-house teams — specific enough to measure against food cost percentage
2. **Step 2** - Break each goal into actions sequenced for restaurants and cafes, naming who does what and by when
3. **Step 3** - Build the plan around menus and costing sheets so progress, changes and decisions are recorded in one place
4. **Step 4** - Price or resource the plan honestly: time, point of sale systems and materials, with contingencies for food hygiene failures
5. **Step 5** - Set the review date and the trigger points that would change the plan, and confirm the plan with all parties

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in restaurants and cafes] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [diners, kitchen and front-of-house teams and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a written plan with goals, actions, owners, timelines and review dates, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: food hygiene failures.
- Never let this happen: allergen incidents.
- Never let this happen: cash handling discrepancies.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a restaurant manager; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to restaurants and cafes's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
