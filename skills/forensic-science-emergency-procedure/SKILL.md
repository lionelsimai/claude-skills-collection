# Emergency Response Procedure — Forensic Science

Respond to emergencies in laboratories and crime scenes calmly, in role and in the right order. The deliverable is an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
This skill is written for a forensic scientist working with police, courts and legal teams in laboratories and crime scenes.

## When to use
- You are a forensic scientist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in laboratories and crime scenes.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an emergency procedure card: recognition, first actions, escalation and recording.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

| Term | Meaning |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Recognise the emergency early: know the presentations that matter for police, courts and legal teams, especially evidence contamination
2. **Step 2** - Take the immediate actions a forensic scientist is trained and authorised for, using laboratory instruments as drilled
3. **Step 3** - Call for help with a structured message: location in laboratories and crime scenes, situation, what has been done, what is needed
4. **Step 4** - Keep police, courts and legal teams and bystanders informed and safe while help arrives; hand over with a concise summary
5. **Step 5** - Record the event fully in examination records afterwards, debrief the team, and update the procedure with lessons learned

1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: evidence contamination.
- Never let this happen: interpretation overstated.
- Never let this happen: chain of custody gaps.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a forensic scientist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to laboratories and crime scenes's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
