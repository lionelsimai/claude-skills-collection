# Metrics & Outcomes Review — Conservation Architecture

Track, interpret and act on the outcome metrics that matter for a conservation architect. The deliverable is a metrics dashboard definition and an interpretation/action review.
This skill is written for a conservation architect working with heritage bodies, owners of historic buildings and councils in historic buildings and design studios.

## When to use
- You are a conservation architect preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in historic buildings and design studios.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a metrics dashboard definition and an interpretation/action review.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Listed building | a building protected by law for its heritage value |
| Fabric | the physical material of a historic building |
| Reversibility | repairs that can be undone without harming original material |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Define each metric precisely — start with survey turnaround — including source data in condition surveys and collection method
2. **Step 2** - Set review cadence and comparison baselines so trends, not single data points, drive decisions
3. **Step 3** - Investigate movements before celebrating or panicking: check data quality, case mix and one-off causes
4. **Step 4** - Link metric movements to practice changes and to risks such as inappropriate repairs damaging fabric; test one change at a time where possible
5. **Step 5** - Report results honestly to stakeholders in historic buildings and design studios, including where outcomes fell short and what changes

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: inappropriate repairs damaging fabric.
- Never let this happen: consent refusals.
- Never let this happen: hidden defects.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a conservation architect; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to historic buildings and design studios's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
