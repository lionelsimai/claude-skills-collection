# Ethics & Professional Standards — Clinical Research

Apply the ethical codes and professional standards that govern a clinical research practitioner. The deliverable is an ethics guide with principles, common dilemmas and decision steps.
This skill is written for a clinical research practitioner working with trial participants, sponsors and investigators in hospitals and research sites.

## When to use
- You are a clinical research practitioner preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in hospitals and research sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an ethics guide with principles, common dilemmas and decision steps.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Protocol | the detailed plan for a clinical trial |
| Informed consent | a participant's voluntary agreement after full explanation |
| Adverse event | an untoward medical occurrence in a participant |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Know the code that applies in hospitals and research sites: consent, confidentiality, honesty, competence and conflicts of interest
2. **Step 2** - Work through dilemmas with a framework: facts, stakeholders, options, code provisions, then decide and record
3. **Step 3** - Protect trial participants, sponsors and investigators first where duties conflict, and be alert to how consent failures can arise from ethical shortcuts
4. **Step 4** - Declare conflicts of interest early and manage them transparently rather than hoping they stay invisible
5. **Step 5** - Raise concerns about others' practice through the proper channels, with evidence from protocols, and support colleagues who do the same

## Profession-Specific Components
| Component | Details for Clinical Research |
|-----------|-------------------------------|
| Core documentation | protocols; consent forms; case report forms; monitoring reports |
| Core tools & equipment | electronic data capture; trial management systems; laboratory kits; randomisation systems |
| People involved | trial participants, sponsors and investigators; the clinical research practitioner; colleagues and other services in hospitals and research sites |
| Primary record | protocols — keep it current, dated and attributable |

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in hospitals and research sites] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [trial participants, sponsors and investigators and other stakeholders]

The finished output is an ethics guide with principles, common dilemmas and decision steps, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

### Action Plan
1. **Immediate:** Confirm scope, consent and the evidence available in protocols.
2. **Short-term:** Complete the workflow steps and record outcomes against recruitment to target.
3. **Medium-term:** Review results, close actions, and update local procedures in hospitals and research sites.

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: consent failures.
- Never let this happen: protocol deviations unreported.
- Never let this happen: participant safety signals missed.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a clinical research practitioner; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to hospitals and research sites's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
