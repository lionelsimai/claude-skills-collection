# Risk Assessment — Geological Surveying

Identify, score and control the risks in work for mining, construction and environmental clients in field sites and laboratories. The deliverable is a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners.
This skill is written for a engineering geologist and surveyor working with mining, construction and environmental clients in field sites and laboratories.

## When to use
- You are a engineering geologist and surveyor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in field sites and laboratories.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a risk assessment with likelihood/impact scores, controls and owners.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Core sample | a cylinder of rock extracted by drilling |
| Overburden | material lying above the resource of interest |
| Fault | a fracture in rock along which movement has occurred |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Map the activity step by step and list what could go wrong at each stage for mining, construction and environmental clients and staff
2. **Step 2** - Score each risk for likelihood and impact, giving special weight to field safety incidents
3. **Step 3** - For every high risk, define a control that removes, reduces or contains it, using drilling equipment where relevant
4. **Step 4** - Record the assessment in survey data, assign an owner to each control, and set a review date
5. **Step 5** - Brief everyone involved before work starts; stop and reassess if conditions in field sites and laboratories change

2. Detail, following the workflow above

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: field safety incidents.
- Never let this happen: sample mix-ups.
- Never let this happen: misinterpreted ground conditions.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a engineering geologist and surveyor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to field sites and laboratories's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
