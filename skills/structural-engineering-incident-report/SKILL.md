# Incident Reporting & Response — Structural Engineering

Respond to, record and learn from incidents involving architects, builders and building owners in design offices and construction sites. The deliverable is a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list.
This skill is written for a structural engineer working with architects, builders and building owners in design offices and construction sites.

## When to use
- You are a structural engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in design offices and construction sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Make the situation safe first; attend to people before paperwork, and escalate if calculation errors is still live
2. **Step 2** - Record facts while they are fresh: what happened, when, who was involved, what was observed — in structural calculations
3. **Step 3** - Notify the people and bodies who must know, within the timeframes that apply in design offices and construction sites
4. **Step 4** - Support those affected, including honest communication with architects, builders and building owners about what happened and what happens next
5. **Step 5** - Analyse causes without blame, agree preventive actions with owners and dates, and track them to closure

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: calculation errors.
- Never let this happen: construction deviations unnoticed.
- Never let this happen: building movement.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a structural engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to design offices and construction sites's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
