# Stakeholder Presentation — Demolition Services

Present demolition contractor findings and proposals to decision-makers and stakeholders. The deliverable is a presentation structure with evidence summary and decision asks.
This skill is written for a demolition contractor working with developers, councils and property owners in demolition sites.

## When to use
- You are a demolition contractor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in demolition sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a presentation structure with evidence summary and decision asks.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Soft strip | removing non-structural elements before demolition |
| Asbestos survey | inspection identifying asbestos before work starts |
| Arisings | materials produced by demolition for reuse or disposal |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Open with the decision or conclusion, then the evidence — busy stakeholders should not wait for the point
2. **Step 2** - Support claims with data from demolition plans and movement in project completion times, with sources and limits stated
3. **Step 3** - Address the hard parts directly, including asbestos exposure and what it would take to reduce it
4. **Step 4** - Make the ask specific: what decision, by when, with what resources for work in demolition sites
5. **Step 5** - Follow up in writing the same day: decisions made, actions agreed, owners and dates

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in demolition sites] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [developers, councils and property owners and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a presentation structure with evidence summary and decision asks, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

- Never let this happen: asbestos exposure.
- Never let this happen: uncontrolled collapse.
- Never let this happen: dust and noise breaches.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a demolition contractor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
