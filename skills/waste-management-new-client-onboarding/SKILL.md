# New Client Onboarding Pack — Waste Management

Onboard households, businesses and councils smoothly into a waste operations manager's service in waste facilities and collection rounds. The deliverable is an onboarding pack: welcome information, expectations, first steps and contacts.
This skill is written for a waste operations manager working with households, businesses and councils in waste facilities and collection rounds.

## When to use
- You are a waste operations manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in waste facilities and collection rounds.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an onboarding pack: welcome information, expectations, first steps and contacts.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Send a welcome pack before the first appointment: what the service does, what happens first, what to bring from waste transfer notes
2. **Step 2** - Set expectations honestly — timescales, costs where relevant, and how progress on collection completion will be shared
3. **Step 3** - Cover rights and responsibilities on both sides, including confidentiality limits and how to raise concerns
4. **Step 4** - Make the first contact easy: named contact, directions to waste facilities and collection rounds, and what to do if plans change
5. **Step 5** - Check in after the first session or milestone and fix friction early, before it becomes disengagement or hazardous waste mishandling

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: hazardous waste mishandling.
- Never let this happen: vehicle accidents.
- Never let this happen: site fires.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a waste operations manager; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to waste facilities and collection rounds's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
