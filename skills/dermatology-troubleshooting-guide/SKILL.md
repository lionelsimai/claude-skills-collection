# Troubleshooting & Problem Solving — Dermatology

Diagnose and resolve common problems a dermatologist meets in a dermatology outpatient clinic and minor surgery suite. The deliverable is a troubleshooting guide: symptoms, likely causes, checks and fixes.
This skill is written for a dermatologist working with patients with skin, hair and nail conditions in a dermatology outpatient clinic and minor surgery suite.

## When to use
- You are a dermatologist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a dermatology outpatient clinic and minor surgery suite.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a troubleshooting guide: symptoms, likely causes, checks and fixes.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Dermatoscope | a handheld magnifier for examining skin lesions |
| Biopsy | removing a small tissue sample for laboratory analysis |
| Eczema | a group of conditions causing inflamed, itchy skin |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Describe the problem precisely from the record in skin examination records — when it started, what changed, who it affects
2. **Step 2** - Work from most likely and most dangerous causes first; rule out anything connected to melanoma missed at triage early
3. **Step 3** - Run checks in a logical order using dermatoscopes, changing one variable at a time so the cause can be isolated
4. **Step 4** - Apply the fix, then verify against urgent suspected-cancer referral timeliness that the problem is actually resolved, not just quieter
5. **Step 5** - Document the diagnosis and fix so the next occurrence is faster, and flag recurring problems for root-cause work

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: melanoma missed at triage.
- Never let this happen: inappropriate long-term steroid use.
- Never let this happen: infection after minor surgery.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a dermatologist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a dermatology outpatient clinic and minor surgery suite's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
