# Scheduling & Workload Management — Industrial Engineering

Plan schedules, caseloads and turnaround times for a industrial engineer in factories, warehouses and service operations. The deliverable is a workable schedule template with priorities, buffers and escalation rules.
This skill is written for a industrial engineer working with manufacturing and service organisations in factories, warehouses and service operations.

## When to use
- You are a industrial engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in factories, warehouses and service operations.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a workable schedule template with priorities, buffers and escalation rules.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

| Term | Meaning |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Map demand: appointment or job types, durations, and the deadlines that drive throughput improvement
2. **Step 2** - Build the schedule around fixed commitments in factories, warehouses and service operations first, then fit flexible work around them
3. **Step 3** - Protect buffer time for overruns, urgent cases and the admin behind process maps — schedules without buffers fail by week two
4. **Step 4** - Define triage rules so urgent work (for example anything involving changes disrupting production) displaces routine work predictably
5. **Step 5** - Review the schedule weekly against actuals and adjust booking rules, staffing or simulation software availability

- Never let this happen: unmeasured bottlenecks.
