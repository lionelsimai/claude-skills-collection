# Scheduling & Workload Management — Urban Planning

Plan schedules, caseloads and turnaround times for a town planner in planning departments and consultancy offices. The deliverable is a workable schedule template with priorities, buffers and escalation rules.
This skill is written for a town planner working with developers, councils and communities in planning departments and consultancy offices.

## When to use
- You are a town planner preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in planning departments and consultancy offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a workable schedule template with priorities, buffers and escalation rules.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Local plan | the statutory document guiding development in an area |
| Determination | the formal decision on a planning application |
| Material consideration | a planning factor legally relevant to a decision |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Map demand: appointment or job types, durations, and the deadlines that drive application determination times
2. **Step 2** - Build the schedule around fixed commitments in planning departments and consultancy offices first, then fit flexible work around them
3. **Step 3** - Protect buffer time for overruns, urgent cases and the admin behind planning applications — schedules without buffers fail by week two
4. **Step 4** - Define triage rules so urgent work (for example anything involving missed consultation deadlines) displaces routine work predictably
5. **Step 5** - Review the schedule weekly against actuals and adjust booking rules, staffing or GIS mapping availability

The finished output is a workable schedule template with priorities, buffers and escalation rules, structured as:

2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed consultation deadlines.
- Never let this happen: policy conflicts.
- Never let this happen: appeals lost on process.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a town planner; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to planning departments and consultancy offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
