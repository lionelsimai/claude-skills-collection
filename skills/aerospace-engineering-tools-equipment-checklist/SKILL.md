# Tools & Equipment Checklist — Aerospace Engineering

Select, check and maintain the tools and equipment a aerospace engineer relies on. The deliverable is an equipment checklist with inspection points, maintenance dates and replacement triggers.
This skill is written for a aerospace engineer working with aircraft manufacturers, airlines and space agencies in design offices, test facilities and hangars.

## When to use
- You are a aerospace engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in design offices, test facilities and hangars.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an equipment checklist with inspection points, maintenance dates and replacement triggers.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Certification | official approval that a design meets safety standards |
| Fatigue life | how long a component withstands repeated loading |
| Thrust | the force produced by an engine |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - List the essential kit for work in design offices, test facilities and hangars, centred on simulation software, with what each item is used for
2. **Step 2** - Define pre-use checks: condition, calibration or charge status, cleanliness and safety features
3. **Step 3** - Set maintenance and replacement intervals based on usage and manufacturer guidance, and log them against design analyses
4. **Step 4** - Quarantine faulty items immediately — faulty kit is a direct route to test incidents
5. **Step 5** - Review the kit list quarterly against the work actually done and retire or add items deliberately
