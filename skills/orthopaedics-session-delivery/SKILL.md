# Session Delivery Guide — Orthopaedics

Prepare for and deliver a professional session or appointment for patients with bone, joint and muscle injuries or conditions in an orthopaedic theatre and fracture clinic. The deliverable is a session plan, delivery checklist and post-session record.
This skill is written for a orthopaedic surgeon working with patients with bone, joint and muscle injuries or conditions in an orthopaedic theatre and fracture clinic.

## When to use
- You are a orthopaedic surgeon preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in an orthopaedic theatre and fracture clinic.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a session plan, delivery checklist and post-session record.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Fracture | a break in a bone |
| Arthroplasty | surgical replacement of a joint |
| Compartment syndrome | dangerous pressure build-up within a muscle compartment |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Review operation notes before the session so you know the history, goals and any cautions such as wrong-site surgery
2. **Step 2** - Prepare the space and operating microscopes and drills; check everything works and is safe before patients with bone, joint and muscle injuries or conditions arrive
3. **Step 3** - Open the session by agreeing the focus and what a good outcome looks like, linked to surgical site infection rate
4. **Step 4** - Deliver the session, adapting in the moment while staying inside the agreed plan and professional scope
5. **Step 5** - Close by summarising what was covered, confirming follow-ups, and completing the session record the same day

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in an orthopaedic theatre and fracture clinic] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [patients with bone, joint and muscle injuries or conditions and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a session plan, delivery checklist and post-session record, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: wrong-site surgery.
- Never let this happen: compartment syndrome missed.
- Never let this happen: post-operative infection.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a orthopaedic surgeon; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to an orthopaedic theatre and fracture clinic's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
