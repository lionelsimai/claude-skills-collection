# Continuing Professional Development Plan — Structural Engineering

Plan and evidence continuing professional development for a practising structural engineer. The deliverable is a CPD plan mapped to competency gaps, with evidence and reflection records.
This skill is written for a structural engineer working with architects, builders and building owners in design offices and construction sites.

## When to use
- You are a structural engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in design offices and construction sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a CPD plan mapped to competency gaps, with evidence and reflection records.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Deflection | the bending or sagging of a structure under load |
| Foundation | the part of a structure transferring loads to the ground |
| Moment | a rotational force acting on a structure |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Audit current competence against the standards for design offices and construction sites and the evidence in your recent structural calculations
2. **Step 2** - Choose development priorities that close real gaps — including staying current on calculation errors — not just easy hours
3. **Step 3** - Mix formats: courses, supervised practice, case review, reading and teaching others
4. **Step 4** - After each activity, write a short reflection: what changed in your practice and what it means for calculation check completion
5. **Step 5** - Keep the CPD record inspection-ready and review the plan at appraisal or revalidation time

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in design offices and construction sites] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [architects, builders and building owners and other stakeholders]

1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: calculation errors.
- Never let this happen: construction deviations unnoticed.
- Never let this happen: building movement.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a structural engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to design offices and construction sites's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
