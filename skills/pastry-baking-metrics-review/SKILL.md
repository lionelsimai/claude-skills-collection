# Metrics & Outcomes Review — Pastry & Baking

Track, interpret and act on the outcome metrics that matter for a pastry chef and baker. The deliverable is a metrics dashboard definition and an interpretation/action review.
This skill is written for a pastry chef and baker working with cafe and restaurant customers, retailers in bakeries and patisserie kitchens.

## When to use
- You are a pastry chef and baker preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in bakeries and patisserie kitchens.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a metrics dashboard definition and an interpretation/action review.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Proving | allowing dough to rise before baking |
| Lamination | folding butter into dough to create layers, as in croissants |
| Scaling | weighing ingredients or portions accurately |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Define each metric precisely — start with production waste rates — including source data in recipes and production sheets and collection method
2. **Step 2** - Set review cadence and comparison baselines so trends, not single data points, drive decisions
3. **Step 3** - Investigate movements before celebrating or panicking: check data quality, case mix and one-off causes
4. **Step 4** - Link metric movements to practice changes and to risks such as allergen cross-contact; test one change at a time where possible
5. **Step 5** - Report results honestly to stakeholders in bakeries and patisserie kitchens, including where outcomes fell short and what changes

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: allergen cross-contact.
- Never let this happen: burns from ovens.
- Never let this happen: stale stock losses.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a pastry chef and baker; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to bakeries and patisserie kitchens's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
