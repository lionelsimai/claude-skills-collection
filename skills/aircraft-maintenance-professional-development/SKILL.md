# Continuing Professional Development Plan — Aircraft Maintenance

Plan and evidence continuing professional development for a practising aircraft maintenance engineer. The deliverable is a CPD plan mapped to competency gaps, with evidence and reflection records.
This skill is written for a aircraft maintenance engineer working with airlines, operators and passengers (indirectly) in hangars and line stations.

## When to use
- You are a aircraft maintenance engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in hangars and line stations.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a CPD plan mapped to competency gaps, with evidence and reflection records.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Airworthiness | the condition in which an aircraft is safe to fly |
| Task card | an instruction for a specific maintenance job |
| MEL | the minimum equipment list defining what may be unserviceable for flight |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Audit current competence against the standards for hangars and line stations and the evidence in your recent maintenance records
2. **Step 2** - Choose development priorities that close real gaps — including staying current on missed inspections — not just easy hours
3. **Step 3** - Mix formats: courses, supervised practice, case review, reading and teaching others
4. **Step 4** - After each activity, write a short reflection: what changed in your practice and what it means for task completion times
5. **Step 5** - Keep the CPD record inspection-ready and review the plan at appraisal or revalidation time

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in hangars and line stations] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [airlines, operators and passengers (indirectly) and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a CPD plan mapped to competency gaps, with evidence and reflection records, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed inspections.
- Never let this happen: incorrect parts fitted.
- Never let this happen: documentation gaps grounding aircraft.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a aircraft maintenance engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to hangars and line stations's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
