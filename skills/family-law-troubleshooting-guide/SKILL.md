# Troubleshooting & Problem Solving — Family Law

Diagnose and resolve common problems a family law solicitor meets in a law firm and family court. The deliverable is a troubleshooting guide: symptoms, likely causes, checks and fixes.
This skill is written for a family law solicitor working with clients dealing with divorce, children and family finances in a law firm and family court.

## When to use
- You are a family law solicitor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a law firm and family court.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a troubleshooting guide: symptoms, likely causes, checks and fixes.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

| Term | Meaning |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Describe the problem precisely from the record in client care letters — when it started, what changed, who it affects
2. **Step 2** - Work from most likely and most dangerous causes first; rule out anything connected to missed court deadlines early
3. **Step 3** - Run checks in a logical order using case management systems, changing one variable at a time so the cause can be isolated
4. **Step 4** - Apply the fix, then verify against case progression times that the problem is actually resolved, not just quieter
5. **Step 5** - Document the diagnosis and fix so the next occurrence is faster, and flag recurring problems for root-cause work

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: missed court deadlines.
- Never let this happen: safeguarding issues unreported.
- Never let this happen: conflicts of interest.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a family law solicitor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a law firm and family court's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
