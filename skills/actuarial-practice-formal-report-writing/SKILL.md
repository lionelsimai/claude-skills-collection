# Formal Report Writing — Actuarial Practice

Write formal reports as a actuary that stand up to scrutiny from any reader. The deliverable is a report template with structure, evidence standards and review steps.
This skill is written for a actuary working with insurers, pension schemes and regulators in insurance and pensions offices.

## When to use
- You are a actuary preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in insurance and pensions offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a report template with structure, evidence standards and review steps.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Establish the report's purpose, audience and deadline before writing; gather source material from valuation reports
2. **Step 2** - Structure: summary, background, method, findings, analysis, recommendations — in that order
3. **Step 3** - Write findings factually and separately from opinion; label professional judgement as judgement
4. **Step 4** - Support key points with evidence and with model validation results where outcomes are claimed; acknowledge limitations openly
5. **Step 5** - Proofread for accuracy and tone, check names, dates and figures twice, and consider model errors affecting reserves before release

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: model errors affecting reserves.
- Never let this happen: stale assumptions.
- Never let this happen: regulatory breaches.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a actuary; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to insurance and pensions offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
