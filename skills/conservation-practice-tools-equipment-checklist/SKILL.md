# Tools & Equipment Checklist — Conservation Practice

Select, check and maintain the tools and equipment a conservation officer relies on. The deliverable is an equipment checklist with inspection points, maintenance dates and replacement triggers.
This skill is written for a conservation officer working with landowners, visitors and conservation charities in nature reserves and countryside sites.

## When to use
- You are a conservation officer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in nature reserves and countryside sites.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an equipment checklist with inspection points, maintenance dates and replacement triggers.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Habitat | the natural home of a species |
| Biodiversity | the variety of living things in an area |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - List the essential kit for work in nature reserves and countryside sites, centred on survey equipment, with what each item is used for
2. **Step 2** - Define pre-use checks: condition, calibration or charge status, cleanliness and safety features
3. **Step 3** - Set maintenance and replacement intervals based on usage and manufacturer guidance, and log them against management plans
4. **Step 4** - Quarantine faulty items immediately — faulty kit is a direct route to habitat damage from works
5. **Step 5** - Review the kit list quarterly against the work actually done and retire or add items deliberately

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: habitat damage from works.
- Never let this happen: invasive species spread.
- Never let this happen: public safety incidents.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a conservation officer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to nature reserves and countryside sites's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
