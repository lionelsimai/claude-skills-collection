# Records & Documentation — Catering Services

Create and maintain accurate, auditable records for work with event clients and their guests. The deliverable is a documentation standard, templates and a completed record set.
This skill is written for a caterer working with event clients and their guests in venues, events and production kitchens.

## When to use
- You are a caterer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in venues, events and production kitchens.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a documentation standard, templates and a completed record set.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| BEO | a banquet event order detailing an event's requirements |
| Hot holding | keeping cooked food above a safe temperature |
| Canapé | a small bite-sized item served at receptions |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Use event orders as the single source of truth; record facts, observations and decisions — not speculation
2. **Step 2** - Write entries contemporaneously where possible, dated and attributable, in the format venues, events and production kitchens requires
3. **Step 3** - Record measurements and outcomes that feed event delivery success exactly as observed, with units and method noted
4. **Step 4** - Store and share records under the confidentiality and retention rules that apply in venues, events and production kitchens, guarding against food temperature abuse in transit
5. **Step 5** - Audit a sample of records monthly for completeness and correct anything missing while the work is still current

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: food temperature abuse in transit.
- Never let this happen: running out of food.
- Never let this happen: staff shortages on the day.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a caterer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to venues, events and production kitchens's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
