# Handover Briefing — Employment Law

Hand over employees, employers and HR teams or active work to another professional without losing critical detail. The deliverable is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
This skill is written for a employment solicitor working with employees, employers and HR teams in a law firm and employment tribunals.

## When to use
- You are a employment solicitor preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a law firm and employment tribunals.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Settlement agreement | a binding agreement ending employment and waiving claims |
| Unfair dismissal | termination of employment without a fair reason or process |
| Tribunal | the court that hears employment disputes |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Summarise the current position from contracts of employment: what is done, what is in progress, what is waiting
2. **Step 2** - Highlight live risks first — tribunal deadlines missed — and the controls or watchpoints already in place
3. **Step 3** - State the next actions with owners and deadlines, and the results expected for claim resolution times
4. **Step 4** - Walk the receiving professional through the brief, answer questions, and confirm understanding
5. **Step 5** - Record that handover happened, who accepted it, and where the full record lives in a law firm and employment tribunals

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a law firm and employment tribunals] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [employees, employers and HR teams and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: tribunal deadlines missed.
- Never let this happen: discrimination risks unadvised.
- Never let this happen: settlement terms misunderstood.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a employment solicitor; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a law firm and employment tribunals's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
