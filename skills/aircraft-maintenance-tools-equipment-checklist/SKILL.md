# Tools & Equipment Checklist — Aircraft Maintenance

Select, check and maintain the tools and equipment a aircraft maintenance engineer relies on. The deliverable is an equipment checklist with inspection points, maintenance dates and replacement triggers.
This skill is written for a aircraft maintenance engineer working with airlines, operators and passengers (indirectly) in hangars and line stations.

## When to use
- You are a aircraft maintenance engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in hangars and line stations.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces an equipment checklist with inspection points, maintenance dates and replacement triggers.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Airworthiness | the condition in which an aircraft is safe to fly |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - List the essential kit for work in hangars and line stations, centred on aircraft tooling, with what each item is used for
2. **Step 2** - Define pre-use checks: condition, calibration or charge status, cleanliness and safety features
3. **Step 3** - Set maintenance and replacement intervals based on usage and manufacturer guidance, and log them against maintenance records
4. **Step 4** - Quarantine faulty items immediately — faulty kit is a direct route to missed inspections
5. **Step 5** - Review the kit list quarterly against the work actually done and retire or add items deliberately

- Never let this happen: missed inspections.
- Never let this happen: incorrect parts fitted.
- Never let this happen: documentation gaps grounding aircraft.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a aircraft maintenance engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to hangars and line stations's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
