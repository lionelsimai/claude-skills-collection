# Formal Report Writing — Medical Laboratory Science

Write formal reports as a biomedical scientist that stand up to scrutiny from any reader. The deliverable is a report template with structure, evidence standards and review steps.
This skill is written for a biomedical scientist working with patients whose diagnosis depends on laboratory results, via clinical teams in a hospital pathology laboratory.

## When to use
- You are a biomedical scientist preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a hospital pathology laboratory.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a report template with structure, evidence standards and review steps.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Quality control | checks that a test system is performing correctly |
| Turnaround time | time from sample receipt to result |
| Haemolysis | breakdown of red blood cells that can spoil a sample |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Establish the report's purpose, audience and deadline before writing; gather source material from test requests
2. **Step 2** - Structure: summary, background, method, findings, analysis, recommendations — in that order
3. **Step 3** - Write findings factually and separately from opinion; label professional judgement as judgement
4. **Step 4** - Support key points with evidence and with result turnaround time where outcomes are claimed; acknowledge limitations openly
5. **Step 5** - Proofread for accuracy and tone, check names, dates and figures twice, and consider sample mislabelling before release

## Output Format

The finished output is a report template with structure, evidence standards and review steps, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: sample mislabelling.
- Never let this happen: delayed critical results.
- Never let this happen: analyser faults unnoticed.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a biomedical scientist; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a hospital pathology laboratory's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
