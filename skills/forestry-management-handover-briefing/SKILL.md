# Handover Briefing — Forestry Management

Hand over timber buyers, landowners and conservation bodies or active work to another professional without losing critical detail. The deliverable is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
This skill is written for a forest manager working with timber buyers, landowners and conservation bodies in forests and woodland estates.

## When to use
- You are a forest manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in forests and woodland estates.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Restocking | replanting trees after felling |
| Thinning | removing some trees to help others grow |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Summarise the current position from management plans: what is done, what is in progress, what is waiting
2. **Step 2** - Highlight live risks first — illegal felling — and the controls or watchpoints already in place
3. **Step 3** - State the next actions with owners and deadlines, and the results expected for timber yield
4. **Step 4** - Walk the receiving professional through the brief, answer questions, and confirm understanding
5. **Step 5** - Record that handover happened, who accepted it, and where the full record lives in forests and woodland estates

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in forests and woodland estates] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [timber buyers, landowners and conservation bodies and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a structured handover brief covering status, risks and next actions, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: illegal felling.
- Never let this happen: habitat damage.
- Never let this happen: machinery accidents.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a forest manager; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to forests and woodland estates's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
