# Follow-Up & Maintenance Schedule — Solar Energy

Plan follow-up and maintenance so gains for homeowners, businesses and energy companies are checked and sustained. The deliverable is a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers.
This skill is written for a solar PV engineer working with homeowners, businesses and energy companies in rooftops, solar farms and offices.

## When to use
- You are a solar PV engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in rooftops, solar farms and offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a follow-up schedule with intervals, checks and escalation triggers.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Inverter | the device converting panel DC output to usable AC |
| Performance ratio | actual output compared with theoretical maximum |
| Feed-in | electricity exported to the grid |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Set follow-up intervals from the plan and from how system performance ratio is expected to move over time
2. **Step 2** - Define what is checked at each contact, using PV panels and inverters where measurement is needed, and record it in system designs
3. **Step 3** - Give homeowners, businesses and energy companies clear guidance for the interval: what to do, what to watch for, when to make contact sooner
4. **Step 4** - Act early on warning signs — deterioration linked to falls from roofs shortens the interval, never lengthens it
5. **Step 5** - Close the follow-up cycle formally when goals are met, with a summary and re-entry route if needs return

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: falls from roofs.
- Never let this happen: electrical faults.
- Never let this happen: underperforming systems unnoticed.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a solar PV engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to rooftops, solar farms and offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
