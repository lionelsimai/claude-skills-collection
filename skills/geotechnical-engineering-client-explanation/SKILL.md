# Client Explanation & Communication Guide — Geotechnical Engineering

Explain complex geotechnical engineer matters to developers, contractors and infrastructure owners in plain, honest language. The deliverable is a communication pack: explanation script, FAQs and a written summary.
This skill is written for a geotechnical engineer working with developers, contractors and infrastructure owners in site investigation teams and design offices.

## When to use
- You are a geotechnical engineer preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in site investigation teams and design offices.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a communication pack: explanation script, FAQs and a written summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Borehole | a drilled hole used to sample ground and groundwater |
| Bearing capacity | the load the ground can safely support |
| Settlement | downward movement of the ground or a structure |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Identify what developers, contractors and infrastructure owners actually need to decide or understand, and the misconceptions to clear up first
2. **Step 2** - Translate the technical content — including terms behind ground investigation reports — into plain language without losing accuracy
3. **Step 3** - Structure the explanation: what we found, what it means, the options, the recommendation and the risks such as ground conditions misjudged
4. **Step 4** - Check understanding by asking developers, contractors and infrastructure owners to play it back in their own words; correct gaps kindly
5. **Step 5** - Follow up with a written summary the same day and record the conversation in the file

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: ground conditions misjudged.
- Never let this happen: slope failures.
- Never let this happen: foundation settlement.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a geotechnical engineer; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to site investigation teams and design offices's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
