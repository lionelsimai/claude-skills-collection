# Incident Reporting & Response — Forestry Management

Respond to, record and learn from incidents involving timber buyers, landowners and conservation bodies in forests and woodland estates. The deliverable is a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list.
This skill is written for a forest manager working with timber buyers, landowners and conservation bodies in forests and woodland estates.

## When to use
- You are a forest manager preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in forests and woodland estates.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Restocking | replanting trees after felling |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Make the situation safe first; attend to people before paperwork, and escalate if illegal felling is still live
2. **Step 2** - Record facts while they are fresh: what happened, when, who was involved, what was observed — in management plans
3. **Step 3** - Notify the people and bodies who must know, within the timeframes that apply in forests and woodland estates
4. **Step 4** - Support those affected, including honest communication with timber buyers, landowners and conservation bodies about what happened and what happens next
5. **Step 5** - Analyse causes without blame, agree preventive actions with owners and dates, and track them to closure

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in forests and woodland estates] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [timber buyers, landowners and conservation bodies and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a factual incident report and a lessons-learned action list, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: illegal felling.
- Never let this happen: habitat damage.
- Never let this happen: machinery accidents.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a forest manager; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to forests and woodland estates's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
