# Intake & Initial Assessment — Midwifery

Run a structured intake and initial assessment for pregnant and postnatal women and newborn babies in a maternity unit, birth centre and community clinics. The deliverable is a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
This skill is written for a midwife working with pregnant and postnatal women and newborn babies in a maternity unit, birth centre and community clinics.

## When to use
- You are a midwife preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a maternity unit, birth centre and community clinics.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Key Concepts
| Term | Meaning |

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm identity, consent and the reason for referral or engagement, and open the record that will hold antenatal records
2. **Step 2** - Gather history and current circumstances from pregnant and postnatal women and newborn babies, using the standard intake structure for a maternity unit, birth centre and community clinics
3. **Step 3** - Record baseline observations and measurements relevant to antenatal appointment attendance, noting anything that changes the priority of the case
4. **Step 4** - Flag immediate concerns — especially fetal distress escalated late — and escalate or fast-track anything urgent before proceeding
5. **Step 5** - Summarise findings for pregnant and postnatal women and newborn babies in plain language, agree the next step, and file the intake section of antenatal records

## Output Format
**Context:** [Situation in a maternity unit, birth centre and community clinics] | **Objective:** [What this must achieve] | **Audience:** [pregnant and postnatal women and newborn babies and other stakeholders]

The finished output is a completed intake record and initial assessment summary, structured as:
1. Summary of position and evidence
2. Detail, following the workflow above
3. Decisions, actions, owners and dates
4. Review point and escalation route

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: fetal distress escalated late.
- Never let this happen: postnatal depression unrecognised.
- Never let this happen: newborn feeding problems missed.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a midwife; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a maternity unit, birth centre and community clinics's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
