# Intake & Initial Assessment — Secondary Science Education

Run a structured intake and initial assessment for secondary school students in a school science department and laboratories. The deliverable is a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
This skill is written for a secondary science teacher working with secondary school students in a school science department and laboratories.

## When to use
- You are a secondary science teacher preparing, delivering or reviewing this part of your work in a school science department and laboratories.
- You need a defensible, repeatable approach that produces a completed intake record and initial assessment summary.
- A colleague, auditor or client asks how this was done and what evidence supports it.

## Workflow
1. **Step 1** - Confirm identity, consent and the reason for referral or engagement, and open the record that will hold schemes of work
2. **Step 2** - Gather history and current circumstances from secondary school students, using the standard intake structure for a school science department and laboratories
3. **Step 3** - Record baseline observations and measurements relevant to examination results, noting anything that changes the priority of the case
4. **Step 4** - Flag immediate concerns — especially practical safety incidents — and escalate or fast-track anything urgent before proceeding
5. **Step 5** - Summarise findings for secondary school students in plain language, agree the next step, and file the intake section of schemes of work

## Guardrails & Quality Checks
- Never let this happen: practical safety incidents.
- Never let this happen: misconceptions in core concepts.
- Never let this happen: equipment faults.
- Work only within the competence and authority of a secondary science teacher; escalate or refer anything beyond it.
- Record facts and observations, not speculation, and keep records confidential to a school science department and laboratories's rules.
- Verify names, dates, figures and measurements twice before anything is issued or signed off.
- Use only real, verified information from the case in hand — never invent results, credentials or evidence.
